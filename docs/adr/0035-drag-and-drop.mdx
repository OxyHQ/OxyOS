# ADR 0035: Drag and drop between applications

Status: **accepted; implemented** (the protocol, the scene, the compositor, `oxyos-client`, the C ABI and the session's `TrashFiles`; Bloom's `DropZone` and drag, OxyHQ/Bloom#578, and the Bloom host's, the dock's Trash, Stash (OxyHQ/Stash#9) and Astro (OxyHQ/Astro#87); see [Results](#results)). Builds on [ADR 0001](./0001-native-platform-boundaries) (serials, grabs, isolation between clients), [ADR 0002](./0002-bloom-platform-surface-api) (Bloom's platform adapter), [ADR 0007](./0007-clipboard) (offers, types and pipes; a request that cites the user's input), [ADR 0028](./0028-file-access) (files as `file://` URIs, the Trash), [ADR 0029](./0029-chromium-on-oxyos) (Astro; decision 7 left drag and drop between applications to the protocol), [ADR 0033](./0033-protocol-versions) (versions only add) and [ADR 0034](./0034-the-dock) (the dock's Trash).

Two items of [Not in this decision](#not-in-this-decision) are done.

## Problem

Nothing can be dragged from one OxyOS application to another. A file dragged out of Stash stops at its window's edge; the dock's Trash cannot take a file dropped on it (ADR 0034 left it out for want of a protocol); a link or an image dragged in Astro drops only inside Astro's own window (OxyHQ/Astro#82), fed by the pointer the press keeps, and a release outside it drops nothing. On a Mac every application is a source and a target, and Finder, the Dock's Trash and Safari meet through one drag.

A drag is a protocol matter, not an application's:
- the source and the target are two processes, and neither may see where the other's windows are (ADR 0001);
- the pointer, while a drag goes on, is no single application's: it passes over windows of others, which must learn of the drag without learning of the pointer's every move elsewhere;
- what is dragged may be large (an image, hundreds of files) and must reach only the application the person dropped it on, never an application the pointer merely crossed;
- a drag is a classic leak and a classic attack, as the clipboard is: an application reading what is dragged over it without a drop, or starting a drag itself to push content into another application without the person.

## What others do

- **Wayland's data device** (`wl_data_device`, `wl_data_source`, `wl_data_offer`, version 3 with actions): the source offers media types and actions (copy, move, ask) and starts the drag with the serial of a press still held, with a surface of its own as the drag's icon. The compositor owns the drag: it sends the surface under the pointer `enter` (with a new offer and its types), `motion` and `leave`, and the target answers with the type it would take (`accept`) and the actions it supports and prefers (`set_actions`). The compositor chooses the action (the target's preferred one, or one the person forces with a modifier) and tells both sides. On release over a target that accepted, the target is sent `drop`, receives the types it wants through pipes (`receive`, the source writing each one) and says it is done (`finish`); the source learns `dnd_drop_performed`, then `dnd_finished`, and deletes its original after a move. Otherwise the source learns `cancelled`.
- **macOS** (`NSDraggingSession`, `NSDraggingSource`, `NSDraggingDestination`): a view begins a session from a mouse-dragged event, with pasteboard items and an image per item; the source says which operations it allows (copy, link, generic, move, delete) per context (inside the application, outside it). A destination registers the types it takes (`registerForDraggedTypes`) and answers `draggingEntered` and `draggingUpdated` with the operation it would perform; it reads the pasteboard only in `performDragOperation`. Option forces a copy, Command a move, both a link (an alias). Escape cancels, and the image slides back to where it came from. Finder, the target, moves or copies the files itself; the source deletes nothing.
- **The web** (HTML5 drag and drop): a draggable element's `dragstart` fills `DataTransfer` with types and data; `dragenter`, `dragover`, `dragleave` and `drop` go to the element under the pointer, which calls `preventDefault()` to accept and sets `dropEffect`. During the drag the store is in *protected mode*: a page sees the types (`Files`, `text/uri-list`) and not the data, which it reads only in `drop`. `effectAllowed` and `dropEffect` are copy, move, link.

All three agree on what matters here: types and actions are known during the drag, content only at the drop, and only to the target the person chose.

## Decision

1. **The source offers types and actions; content moves only after a drop, to the target that accepted it, through a pipe.** As the clipboard does (ADR 0007, decisions 1 and 3), a drag carries no content. The source names its offer by a number of its own (`drag`), each media type it can produce, most faithful first (`DragType`, at most 16), and the actions it allows (`DragActions`: copy, move, link, any of them), and starts the drag (`StartDrag`). After a drop, the target asks for the types it wants (`ReceiveDrop`, carrying a pipe's write end); the compositor passes each pipe to the source (`DragSend`), which writes that type and closes it. The content never passes through the compositor.

2. **Only a press the person is holding starts a drag.** `StartDrag` cites `serial`, the serial of a pointer press delivered to the source, as `BeginMove` does (ADR 0001): the latest press, on the window (or a popup of it) whose surface the drag starts from, with a button still held, while no popup grab is open, nothing is moving or resizing and the session is not locked. Anything else is refused: the source is told `DragCancelled { reason: Refused }` and nothing else happens. An application cannot start a drag by itself, nor from a press the person already released, nor while the person is in another application. A touch drag is not in this decision (see the end).

3. **The compositor owns the drag.** From an accepted `StartDrag` until the drag ends:
   - **The pointer is the drag's.** The press that began it is cancelled for the source (`Pointer Cancel`, then `Leave`), so it never becomes a click; no surface receives pointer events (presses, motion, scrolls, pinches, resting fingers) until the drag ends. Other buttons pressed meanwhile are consumed with their releases.
   - **The drag's image** is a surface of the source's, of the role `DragIcon` (`CreateDragIcon { surface, size, hotspot }`): the compositor configures it at the scale of the output under the pointer, shows it above every window (beneath the lock, which ends the drag anyway), with its hotspot at the pointer, moving with it, and never gives it input; it is hit by nothing, so the target under it is what is under the pointer. `StartDrag` names it (0 for none); it is shown only while that drag goes on, and the source destroys it once the drag ends. A drag icon not named by a drag is never shown.
   - **The pointer's shape** is the arrow throughout, a Mac's, with the action's badge below and right of its tip while the drop would copy (a green plus) or link (a curved arrow); none for a move or a drop that would be refused ([The drag's image](#the-drags-image)).
   - **The keyboard** goes on reaching the focused surface, except Escape, which cancels the drag (decision 7); the press and its release reach nobody. The modifiers held choose the action (decision 5).

4. **What a target sees: the drag over it, the types, never the content.** The surface under the pointer, of a client of version 10 or newer, is the drag's target, and only it learns of the drag:
   - `DragEnter { surface, offer, serial, x, y, actions, types, modifiers }`, followed by its `types` media types (`DragOfferType`), when the pointer enters it during a drag (or the drag starts over it). `offer` is the compositor's name for this drag, never the source's number, so a target cannot tell which application or offer it is; `actions` are the source's; `x`, `y` are surface-local, as a `Pointer`'s; `serial` names this entering.
   - `DragMotion { surface, offer, x, y, time_ns, modifiers }` as the pointer moves over it.
   - `DragLeave { surface, offer }` when the pointer leaves it, the drag ends without dropping on it, or the source goes.
   
   A drag of files offers, beside `text/uri-list`, `application/x-oxyos-files` with the same URIs: before the drop a target sees only the types, and this one says the URIs are files rather than links, as the web's `Files` type does (a page's `<input type="file">` takes a drag only when it carries files).

   A surface the pointer never enters during the drag learns nothing of it: no other window, no other client, receives a drag event. A client of an older version is never sent one, and its surfaces are no target: a drag over them can drop nothing. The source, when its own surface is under the pointer, is that surface's target like any other: a drag inside one window goes the same way as between two.

5. **The action is negotiated, as Wayland's and a Mac's are.** The target answers each entering and motion with what it would do: `AcceptDrag { offer, serial, mime, actions, preferred }`, citing the entering's `serial` (an answer to an older entering is ignored), the one offered type it would take (empty: none) and the actions it supports and prefers. The compositor chooses the action from the source's actions (S), the target's (T) and the modifiers held, as a Mac does:
   - **Option and Command (Alt and Super) together force a link, Option alone a copy, Command alone a move.** The forced action, if both allow it; none if either does not (the drop is refused, so the person sees that it is).
   - **Otherwise the target's preferred action**, if both allow it; else the first both allow of copy, move, link.
   - **None** while the target has accepted no type.
   
   The target is told the action (`DragAction { offer, action }`) and the source too (`DragSourceAction { drag, action }`), each time it changes; none means a release here drops nothing. A target that wants its action to depend on where in it the pointer is answers again on each motion.

6. **A drop, then the transfer, then the end.** When the person releases the last button:
   - **Over a target whose action is not none**, the target is sent `DragDrop { surface, offer, serial, x, y, action }` and the source `DragDropped { drag, action }`. The drop's `serial` is a user input of the target's (one of its latest 16, as a press is): a request that must cite one (`ReceiveDrop` needs none, but the dock's `TrashFiles` does) can cite it.
   - **The target receives** each offered type it wants once (`ReceiveDrop { offer, mime }`, with its pipe), and says it is done (`FinishDrop { offer }`); the source is told `DragFinished { drag, action }`. Only the client that was dropped on, for that offer, for a type offered and not yet received, before it finished, is answered; anything else is refused without a protocol error (as a refused paste, ADR 0007 decision 4), its pipe closed so its read ends at once, empty.
   - **A move of data** (text dragged out of a field, a card out of a board) is deleted by the source once it is told `DragFinished` with move. **A move of files** (`text/uri-list` of `file://` URIs) is the target's to perform, as Finder and every Linux file manager do: the target moves the files; the source deletes nothing and finds them gone.
   - The compositor keeps a drop open until the target finishes, the target or the source goes, or eight later drops push it out (the source is then told `DragCancelled { reason: Abandoned }`). It is not the drag: another drag may begin meanwhile.

7. **Cancelling.** The drag ends without a drop, nothing transferred, and the source told `DragCancelled { drag, reason }` (its target, if any, `DragLeave`), when:
   - the person presses **Escape** (`Escape`);
   - the last button is released over no target, or over one whose action is none (`NotAccepted`);
   - the source cancels it (`CancelDrag { drag }`, `Cancelled`), or destroys the surface it began from;
   - the session locks (`Locked`);
   - it was refused at its start (`Refused`).
   
   If the source disconnects, its drag and its drops end and their targets are told `DragLeave` (a drop already made just has its receives refused). Every drag ends in exactly one of `DragFinished` or `DragCancelled`, after at most one `DragDropped`. A drag that dropped nothing (but for the lock) slides its image back to where the drag began, a Mac's ([The drag's image](#the-drags-image)).

8. **Protocol version 10** (ADR 0033), added only. A target is sent drag events unasked, so never a client of version 9 or older; the client library refuses the requests to a compositor of version 9 or older, and an application there drags only within itself, as before.

   `DragActions` is a set of bits (copy 1, move 2, link 4); `DragCancelReason` is `Refused`, `Escape`, `NotAccepted`, `Cancelled`, `Locked`, `Abandoned`. `ReceiveDrop` and `DragSend` carry a descriptor, as `ClipboardReceive` and `ClipboardSend` do, checked the same way by `oxyos-ipc`.

9. **The client library and the C ABI.** `oxyos-client` holds what its client drags (`Client::start_drag`, each type's bytes) and writes each `DragSend` itself from a thread of its own, as it answers pastes (ADR 0007); a pipe for a drag it does not hold stays the program's. The C ABI gains:
   - `oxyos_client_create_drag_icon`, drawn with `oxyos_client_present` as any surface;
   - `oxyos_client_start_drag (surface, icon, serial, actions, entries, count)`, returning the drag's number, and `oxyos_client_cancel_drag`;
   - `oxyos_client_accept_drag`, `oxyos_client_receive_drop` (the pipe's read end, read to its end as a paste's) and `oxyos_client_finish_drop`;
   - `oxyos_client_trash_files` (the shell);
   - the events `OXYOS_EVENT_DRAG_ENTER`, `_DRAG_OFFER_TYPE`, `_DRAG_MOTION`, `_DRAG_LEAVE`, `_DRAG_ACTION`, `_DRAG_DROP`, `_DRAG_SOURCE_ACTION`, `_DRAG_DROPPED`, `_DRAG_FINISHED` and `_DRAG_CANCELLED`, and `OXYOS_DRAG_COPY`, `_MOVE`, `_LINK`.

10. **Bloom: one drop target and one drag, through the platform adapter, with HTML5's on the web.** React Native has no desktop drag and drop, and Bloom had only drags inside one component (the sidebar's rows, tabs, boards) and one web-only drop of a file (`FileUpload`). Bloom gains, as it gained popups (ADR 0002):
    - **`DropZone`** (`@oxy.so/bloom/drop-zone`), a view that takes drops: what it accepts (`accept`: `files`, `text`, `url`, `html`, or media types), the actions it supports and prefers, and `onDragEnter`, `onDragOver`, `onDragLeave` and `onDrop`. Its events carry the point (view-local), the action, the kinds offered, and at the drop the content: the files (`file://` URIs, with their names and types), the text, the URLs, the HTML. On the web it is HTML5's drag and drop on its element: types only until the drop (protected mode), the files as the browser's `File` objects. On a platform whose adapter reports `drag-and-drop`, it is the adapter's `DropTarget` host component. Elsewhere (a phone today) it is a view that takes nothing.
    - **The platform's drag** (`usePlatformDrag()`, the adapter's `drag`): `start({ data, actions, previewRef, count })` begins a drag from the press the application is handling, with what it drags (`files` as `file://` URIs, `text`, `url`, `html`), the actions it allows, the dragged view itself (its image, as it looks) and how many items go, and resolves with the action the drop took, or none. An application that runs its own pointer gestures (Stash's items) calls it once its own drag begins; Bloom's own drags (the sidebar's rows) keep their own way.
    - Bloom stays free of OxyOS and of the protocol: the adapter's types describe the drag; the OxyOS adapter lives with the runtime (`sample/oxyos/platform-surface.js`).

11. **The Bloom host.** The adapter's `DropTarget` is the host component `OxyOSDropTargetView`, with its accepted kinds and actions as props; the host answers the compositor itself, from its own tree, never waiting for JavaScript:
    - on each entering and motion it finds the innermost drop target under the point that accepts an offered type, and answers `AcceptDrag` with that type and the target's actions (none if no target takes one), and gives the targets their `onDragEnter`, `onDragOver` and `onDragLeave`;
    - at the drop it receives the types that target accepts (each once, from the loop, within a bound of time and size, as a paste), gives the target its `onDrop` with the content, then finishes the drop.
    - `drag.start` is `OxyOSDrag`: the host offers the content with the serial of the press still held, draws the preview view's subtree in a drag icon (as a popup's subtree is drawn in its surface), and resolves the call with the action at `DragFinished`, or none at `DragCancelled`.
    - Every React Native `TextInput` is a source and a target of text of the platform's own, as an `NSTextView` is ([ADR 0037](./0037-text-editing), decision 9): its selected text drags out from a press on it, its image the text itself; an editable field that is not secure is a target for text among the drop targets, the innermost under the point taking the drag (a field inside a `DropZone` takes the text dropped on it; files go to the zone), its own text moving within it and anything else copied in by default.

12. **The dock's Trash takes files.** The shell wraps the Trash's icon in a `DropZone` accepting `files` with move alone (a Mac's Trash), highlighted while a drag it would take is over it. On the drop the shell asks the session to put the files in the Trash: `TrashFiles { input, index, last, text }` (its `shell` grant), the URIs one a line in chunks, citing the drop's serial. The session moves each `file://` file of the person's that its scope reaches into the freedesktop.org Trash (`oxyos_files::trash::trash`, ADR 0028 decision 5), as Stash's own Move to Trash does, and the dock's Trash follows (`TrashItems`). A request citing no input of the shell's moves nothing; a URI the scope refuses (another user's file, the home, a Trash's insides) is passed over, said in the session's log.

13. **Stash is a source and a target, through Bloom's API alone.** Its own drag of items (a mouse press moved 4 pixels on an item) becomes the platform's drag where there is one (`usePlatformDrag()`), of the selected local items as `file://` URIs, allowing copy, move and link; elsewhere it keeps its drag inside its window. Its items' area is a `DropZone` accepting `files` (copy and move, preferring move): a drop on a folder moves (or with Option copies) the files into that folder, a drop elsewhere into the folder shown, the folder under the pointer highlighted meanwhile. A drop of Stash's own items goes this way too. Nothing of OxyOS is imported.

    **5 November 2026, amended:** what is dragged is never a target, as on a Mac. A test machine's home was selected whole, and a press on Pictures moved past the 4 points and was released over Pictures itself. The drop went into the folder under the pointer, which was one of the dragged items, and everything else in the home moved into Pictures. Now:
    - **The source's own items:** a window never lights the items it is dragging, and they never take its drop.
    - **Into itself:** files dropped into one of themselves, or into a folder inside one, move nothing. This is checked on the dropped URIs, so it also holds for a drag from another window.
    - **Places:** a move that includes one of the platform's places (the home, its standard folders, a volume) moves nothing and says why. The file service refuses them anyway ([ADR 0028](./0028-file-access), decision 2).
    - **Undo:** Edit › Undo (Ctrl+Z) takes a move back, as Finder's does. A field with the keys keeps its own Undo, Redo and Select All.

    [OxyHQ/Dropsea#39](https://github.com/OxyHQ/Dropsea/pull/39).

14. **Astro is a source and a target.** Its `oxyos` Ozone platform (`ui/ozone/platform/oxyos`) speaks version 10 when the compositor does:
    - **as a source,** Chromium's `StartDrag` offers the drag's `OSExchangeData` as media types (`text/uri-list`, `text/x-moz-url`, `text/html`, `text/plain;charset=utf-8`, Chromium's custom data, a file's contents as its type), with the actions Chromium allows, its drag image drawn by Chromium in a drag icon, and runs its nested loop until `DragFinished` or `DragCancelled`;
    - **as a target,** it gives its window's drop handler the drag's types at `DragEnter` (an `OSExchangeData` that holds the kinds offered, so a page sees `Files` in `dragenter`, and `<input type="file">` takes the drag), answers `AcceptDrag` with what the page's `dragover` chose, and at `DragDrop` receives the types it uses and gives the drop handler the real content before finishing.
    - Against a compositor of version 9 or older it keeps its drag inside its window (OxyHQ/Astro#82).

## Tests

- **Protocol:** every new message round-trips; `DragActions` takes only its bits; a drag event is never written for version 9 and refused from it; which version introduced each request and event; `ReceiveDrop` and `DragSend` carry a descriptor and nothing else among the new messages does.
- **Scene** (`scene/tests/drag.rs`): a drag from a held press reaches the window under the pointer with its types, and no other window receives anything; refused with another client's serial, a released press, a grab open, while locked; the action chosen from both sides' actions, the preferred one and each modifier; an answer citing an old entering ignored; a drop to an accepting target, a receive per type once, refused from another client, for a type not offered, twice, after finishing; Escape, a release over nothing, the source's cancel, the source's origin destroyed, the lock, the source and the target leaving; the icon placed at the pointer, following it, hit by nothing, hidden once the drag ends; a client of version 9 is no target.
- **Compositor, through real sockets:** one application drags `text/uri-list` to another, which receives exactly the offered bytes once through the pipe; the dock's `TrashFiles` puts a file in the Trash.
- **C ABI:** a drag between two clients through the library, the library writing the content.
- **Bloom:** `DropZone` on the web (HTML5 events, types before the drop, the content at it, `dropEffect` from the actions), with the adapter's `DropTarget` when it reports `drag-and-drop`, and inert elsewhere; `usePlatformDrag` null without the adapter.
- **Scenarios** (`regress.py`, the development compositor), each at scale 1 and 1.25:
  - `drag-trash`: a file dragged from a fixture application onto the dock's Trash is in the home's Trash afterwards, the Trash's count told, and only the dock and the source were told anything of the drag;
  - `drag-drop`: text and a file dragged from one application to another: the target receives exactly the offered data once, a third window the pointer never crossed receives no drag event, and Escape midway cancels with nothing transferred;
  - `stash-drag`: a file dragged from one Stash window onto a folder in another moves into it, and one dropped on the dock's Trash goes to the Trash;
  - `dropsea-drop-onto-itself` (5 November 2026): the home selected whole (Ctrl+A), Pictures pressed, moved past the threshold and released over itself moves nothing. A file dragged onto a folder moves into it, Ctrl+Z puts it back and Ctrl+Shift+Z moves it again. Documents dragged onto a folder stays, saying why;
  - `astro-drag`, extended: a file dragged from a fixture into Astro's `<input type="file">` reaches the page; a link dragged out of Astro drops in a fixture as `text/uri-list`.

## Results

**Step 2** (the protocol, the scene, the compositor, the client library and the C ABI):
- Version 10's eight requests and eleven events, in the protocol's table (`docs/native-foundation.mdx`); `DragActions` takes only its three bits; `ReceiveDrop` and `DragSend` carry a pipe, checked as the clipboard's are.
- The scene's drag (`scene/drag.rs`): a drag only from the source's latest press on that window with its button held, no grab, nothing moving and not locked; the press cancelled for its surface and the pointer the drag's; the target the surface under the pointer, of a client of version 10, told the drag and its types and nothing else; the action negotiated (`negotiate_action`) and told to both sides as it changes, Option and Command held included; the drop a user input of the target's; each type received once by the client dropped on, before it finishes; Escape (its press and release reaching nobody), a release over nothing that takes it, the source's cancel, its window destroyed, the lock, either side leaving; at most eight drops open; the icon at the pointer, above every window, hit by nothing and shown only during its drag.

## The drag's image

Added 5 November 2026. On the ThinkPad a file dragged in Dropsea showed, under the pointer, a purple pill with the file's name: not the item, not where it was gripped, and the previous drag's name. Dropsea drew a view of its own for the image (a label on the accent, parked 10,000 pixels left of its window) and set its name in the same gesture that started the drag, so the first drag of a window had no image at all and every later one pictured the label as the drag before had left it, its icon sized for that name and its text cut; and as the view was far off screen, the point pressed in it (`hotspot`) was clamped to its corner, leaving the label up and to the left of the pointer. Nothing badged the action, and the image vanished at once whatever happened.

A Mac draws a drag of files from the items themselves: each one's icon or thumbnail and name as they look, about three quarters opaque, the point gripped kept under the pointer; several stacked, with a red count at the top right; the arrow badged with a green plus for a copy (Option, or a drop elsewhere that copies) and a curved arrow for a link, nothing for a move or over a target that refuses; and a drop on nothing, refused or cancelled slides the image back to its items in about a quarter second, fading. OxyOS does the same, each part in its layer, nothing in an application's own code:

1. **Bloom: the dragged view is the image.** `PlatformDragRequest`'s `previewRef` is the view pressed, where it is (never a view made for the purpose), and `count` is how many items go (Bloom 17.10.0). On the web `setDragImage` gives an HTML5 drag the same image from the dragged element (stacked and badged for several) through `DataTransfer.setDragImage`.
2. **The Bloom host draws it** (`dragStack`, `drawDragImage` in `renderer.cpp`): the view's subtree drawn as it looks, its transparent margins (a cell's column around its icon and name) found from its own pixels and left out; for two items one copy of it behind, for more two, stacked 3 pixels down and to the right, the whole three quarters opaque, so the front item hides those behind; the count over its top right corner on a pill of a Mac's red, in the system font, opaque. It is drawn at the scale of the output its icon surface is configured for, so it is crisp at 1.25. The hotspot is the press within what the view draws, offset into the stack: the grip stays under the pointer.
3. **The compositor badges the pointer** from the action it negotiated (decision 5): `RenderItem::DragBadge`, 16 logical pixels below and right of the tip, above everything, drawn by `drag_badge.rs` at the output's scale; never in a capture, as the pointer is not. Since the pointer is on the display's cursor plane, the badge is composed into the frame and follows it.
4. **The compositor slides it back** (`Change::Returned`): a drag that ends with nothing taking it (released over nothing or a refusing target, Escape, the source cancelling) has its icon's last frame go back to where the icon was as the press began (the press less the grip: the item's own place), on a critically damped spring at rest in 250 ms, fading over its last 120. A drop that is taken removes the image at once; the lock ends it at once. With Reduce motion it crossfades in place. The source destroys its icon surface when told the drag ended, as before: one icon surface a drag, none left after.

Dropsea passes its items' own views: in icon view the whole cell (its icon or thumbnail over its name label, selected as it is), in list view the icon and name (the selection's accent behind them, so white text reads), and the count of the files dragged.

Tests: the scene's (`scene/tests/drag.rs`): a drag that drops nothing, or is cancelled with Escape, returns its icon's centre to the item's place, a taken one does not, and one icon surface is all there is; the pointer's badge for a copy (Option) and a link (Option and Command) at its place below the tip, above the icon, following the pointer, none for a move, over a target that takes nothing or once the drag ended. The compositor's: the badges' pixels at 1 and 1.25; the return a quarter second long, each frame nearer the item, whole and then fading, its centre the item's at the end. Bloom's: `drag-image.web.test.ts`. The scenarios `dropsea-drag-image.json` and `dropsea-drag-image-scaled.json` hold a drag midway (`drag_hold`, then `move_output`, `key_down` and `release_output`) and check its pixels at 1 and 1.25: the item's colours at its grip under the pointer, three quarters opaque over what is beneath; three items stacked with a red 3 at the top right; Option's green plus over Dropsea's items and no badge over the desktop, which takes nothing; the slide back held on the compositor's clock at three points, each nearer the item, and its curve and end point on the running clock; one drag icon surface during each drag, none after.

## Vanishing drags, files' types and files opened with an application

Added 6 November 2026. On a Mac an icon dragged out of the Dock and let go goes up in a puff of smoke, with a soft sound, rather than sliding back (its dragging session's `animatesToStartingPositionsOnCancelOrFail` off, and `NSShowAnimationEffect(NSAnimationEffectPoof)` where it ended); a file dragged over an application's Dock icon highlights it only if that application opens the file; dropped there, the application opens it. OxyOS's drags had none of it: every drag dropped on nothing slid back, a target saw only `application/x-oxyos-files` and never what files they were, and nothing opened a file with an application the person chose.

1. **A source may say its drag vanishes** (`DragVanish { drag }`, before its `StartDrag`, as its types are said). Released over no target, or over one that takes nothing of it (`NotAccepted`), its image is not slid back: the compositor draws a poof where it was (`Change::Vanished`, its centre; `poof.rs`, a Mac's `NSAnimationEffectPoof`: a cloud of soft white puffs, each shaded beneath so it reads over anything, bursting out from the item's centre for 0.3 s, whole for its first third and then fading, a little larger than the item, drawn with oxyos-vector at the scale of the output it is on), the image itself gone beneath it within a tenth of a second, and plays the `poof` sound once, its own as the screenshot's shutter is ([ADR 0044](./0044-system-sounds), 6 November 2026). With Reduce Motion the image fades out in place, no cloud. Escape, the source's cancel and the lock put it back (or end it at once) as before, and a drop a target takes removes it at once. The source is told `DragCancelled { NotAccepted }` as before: what was dropped on nothing is its to act on (the dock forgets the icon).
2. **A drag of files says their media types** (`DragFileType { drag, index, last, text }`, before its `StartDrag`, each type in chunks: an office document's is longer than a chunk; distinct, at most 16). A target is told them after the offered types, with how many in `DragEnter`'s `file_types` (`DragOfferFileType { offer, index, last, text }`), never the files: what a web page sees of a dragged file before the drop (`DataTransferItem.type`). They are the source's word; whoever acts on a drop checks the files themselves (decision 3). The Bloom host says them for every drag of files it starts, each `file://` URI's type as the platform names it (`oxyos_files_media_type`: `inode/directory` for a folder, else its name's, oxyos-files' table, else `application/octet-stream`).
3. **The session's shell opens files with an application** (`OpenFiles { input, index, last, text }`, its `shell` grant: the application's id on the first line, the files' `file://` URIs after, in chunks), citing an input of its own (the drop on the application's icon in the dock). The session opens each file the person's scope reaches (`oxyos_compositor::open_files`) whose media type the application's entry declares (`MimeType`: the type, or its `type/*`; `Launcher::open_with`), with that application, as a file is opened by its type (ADR 0028, decision 6): started with the file in its launch record and as its `Linking` initial URL. A request citing no input, a file outside the scope or of a type the application does not declare is passed over, said in the session's log. The shell is told what each application opens (`opens` in the shell's state, from its entry's `MimeType`).
4. **Bloom and the Bloom host.** Bloom's `DragData.other` (media types by name, with their text: an application dragged out of a launcher), `PlatformDragRequest.vanish` and `onCancel` (why a drag dropped nothing: `escape`, `not-accepted`, `refused`, `cancelled`), `DropTargetDragEvent.fileTypes`, and `DropZone`'s `acceptFileTypes` (media types or `type/*`: a drag of files is taken only when each of its files' types matches one). The host offers `other`'s types after the kinds; `OxyOSDropTargetView`'s `acceptFileTypes` takes a drag of files as `files` only when every file type it was told matches one (none told: not taken), and its events carry `fileTypes`; `OxyOSDrag.start` takes `{ vanish }` and an `onCancel`; `OxyOSShell.openFiles(application, uris)` cites the latest input, the drop. A drop target accepting a media type by name (`application/x-oxyos-application`) receives it in `other`, as before.
5. **Protocol version 26** ([ADR 0033](./0033-protocol-versions)), added only:

   | Direction | Opcode | Message |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | Request | 248 | `DragVanish { drag }` |
   | Request | 249 | `DragFileType { drag, index, last, text }` |
   | Request | 250 | `OpenFiles { input, index, last, text }` (shell) |
   | Event | 252 | `DragOfferFileType { offer, index, last, text }`, never to a client of version 25 |
   | Event | 240 | `DragEnter` grows `file_types` (a version 25 reader reads none) |

   The client library tells an older compositor neither a drag's vanishing nor its files' types (the drag goes as before) and refuses `OpenFiles` to it. In the C ABI: `oxyos_client_start_drag_with` (`OXYOS_DRAG_VANISH`, the file types one a line), `OXYOS_EVENT_DRAG_OFFER_FILE_TYPE` (`DRAG_ENTER`'s `height` the count), `oxyos_client_open_files` and `oxyos_files_media_type`.

Tests: the protocol's (the three requests and the event version 26's, `DragEnter`'s count left out for version 25 and read back as none); the scene's (`scene/tests/drag.rs`): a vanishing drag dropped on nothing or on a refusing window vanishes where it is, its centre the image's, and the source is told `NotAccepted`; Escape still puts it back; taken, neither; one that said nothing slides back; a drag's files' types told its target after its types, a long one in chunks, each once, and nothing to a window the pointer never crossed; the shell's `OpenFiles` taken whole with whether it cited its input, refused from any other client; the compositor's: the poof's frames (whole, then fading, gone at its end; crisp at 1.25) and its transition (in place, 300 ms, linear; Reduce Motion a fade), and through real sockets at 1 and 1.25 a vanishing drag dropped on the desktop: the cloud where it was at once and growing, the point between it and its window unchanged (nothing slides back), the image gone beneath it, the desktop again at its end, the `poof` sound once (`tests/poof.rs`); `open_files`: only the person's drop opens, only files the scope reaches; the launcher's `open_with`: only a type the application declares (or its `type/*`); the C ABI's: a vanishing drag's requests, a long file type in chunks, a target reading them, `oxyos_client_open_files` and `oxyos_files_media_type`. Scenarios, at 1 and 1.25: `drag-vanish.json` and `drag-vanish-scaled.json` (`sample/VanishProbe.tsx`): an application dragged out and let go over nothing goes up in a poof where it is (the transition `vanished` and no `returned`, the cloud's white at its centre on the held clock, the `poof` sound once, the source told `not-accepted`), and Escape puts it back with no sound; `drop-file-types.json` and `drop-file-types-scaled.json` (`sample/ShellDropProbe.tsx` run as the shell, Inbox declaring `text/markdown`): `notas.md` dropped on a zone taking `text/markdown` is opened with Inbox through the session (`files_opened`), `foto.png` over a zone taking any files shows its type `image/png`, and dropped on the `text/markdown` zone is refused and opens nothing; an application dragged onto a zone taking `application/x-oxyos-application` is kept there and does not vanish.

## Not in this decision

- **Touch drags** (a long press then a drag, as iPadOS and Android do): the pointer's drag first; a touch drag needs the touch's own serial rule and is to come.
- ~~**Spring-loaded folders** (a folder opening under a drag held over it)~~: [ADR 0047](./0047-finder-drags-and-the-desktop), with the desktop's files and Finder's move-or-copy by volume. The image sliding back and the copy and link badges came after ([The drag's image](#the-drags-image)).
- **A target changing the image** (a Mac's file dragged over a text field showing its name): the image is the source's throughout.
- **Scrolling a target while dragging** with the wheel or two fingers; a target may scroll itself as the pointer nears its edge.
- **Dragging the dock's icons** to reorder them and out to unkeep them (ADR 0034): the shell's, on what [Vanishing drags, files' types and files opened with an application](#vanishing-drags-files-types-and-files-opened-with-an-application) gives it.
- **Dragging a screenshot's thumbnail** (ADR 0024, decision 10) into an application: the shell as a source, to come.
- **Promised files** (a Mac's file promises: a file the source writes only where it is dropped). A source offers content or existing files.
