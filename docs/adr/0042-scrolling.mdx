# ADR 0042: Scrolling as on a Mac

Status: **accepted; implemented** (the Bloom host: `input.h`, `scrollbars.h`, `scroll-physics.h`, `bloom-host.cpp`'s keys; natural scrolling, decision 6: `oxyos-preferences`, `oxyos-input`, Settings' Mouse and Trackpad pages). Builds on [ADR 0039](./0039-keyboard-access) (Full Keyboard Access: which control has the keyboard, decision 1's scroll views), [ADR 0037](./0037-text-editing) (what a field does with its keys; Ctrl is the applications' ⌘) and the Scrolling and Overscroll rows of [the Android adaptation](../react-native-android-adaptation) (the wheel, the touchpad's drag and glide, the stretch).

## Problem

The Bloom host plays Android for what React receives (React Native's Android `ReactScrollView`, `OverScroller`, `View`'s scrollbars) and macOS for what the person sees. An audit of scrolling against both found the wheel, the touchpad's drag and glide and Android 12's stretch done and checked, and four gaps:

- **No scrollbars at all.** Nothing showed where a list was or how long it was; `showsVerticalScrollIndicator` and `persistentScrollbar` were ignored, and there was nothing to drag.
- **No keyboard scrolling.** Page Down, Space, the arrows, Home and End scrolled nothing outside a text field; a person without a touchpad or wheel could read only what Tab reached.
- **Tab's scroll-to-focused** scrolled only the innermost scroll view, by a margin of 8 that was neither Android's (0) nor the focus ring's, and pushed the top of a control taller than its view out of it; no scenario checked it.
- **JavaScript's ScrollView commands were dropped:** `scrollTo`, `scrollToEnd` and `flashScrollIndicators` did nothing (the host passed commands only to text inputs), so `FlatList.scrollToIndex`, a chat's jump to its end and the shell's overview, which positions its desktops with `scrollTo`, never moved.

A fifth was found on 5 November 2026, and decision 6 added for it: **no natural scrolling.** Every device scrolled as libinput leaves it, traditionally: a touchpad's two fingers moved up moved the content down, against them, where a Mac's content follows them, and nothing could change it.

## Decision

1. **A wheel's notch moves at once**, as Android's `ScrollView` takes a wheel and as AppKit's `NSScrollView` takes a mouse's discrete wheel (the smoothing a Mac user sees in a browser is the browser engine's own, and the reason utilities such as Mos exist). The adaptation's earlier remark that macOS animates it was wrong and is corrected. A touchpad's continuous scroll stays Android 14's drag and glide.

2. **Overlay scrollbars, as macOS draws its overlay scrollers** (`scrollbars.h`), drawn by the host with Skia over each scroll view's content, unscrolled and unstretched, within its padding box:
   - **What shows:** nothing at rest. A scroll the person makes (the wheel, the touchpad's drag and glide, the keyboard, the scrollbar itself, a screen reader's scroll action) shows a thin thumb along the far edge of each axis the content scrolls along; it holds 500 ms after the last movement and fades out over 250 ms (Android's fade, `ViewConfiguration.getScrollBarFadeDuration`). JavaScript's scrolls and Tab's scroll-to-focused show none, as a Mac's programmatic scrolls do not; `flashScrollIndicators` shows them and fades them, as Android's `awakenScrollBars`.
   - **How it looks:** the thumb 6 logical pixels wide, 3 from the view's edge and from the track's ends, never shorter than 20, its length the viewport's share of the content and its place the offset's share of the way, fully rounded; with the pointer over the track (15 wide, a legacy scroller's width) it grows to 9 over 120 ms and a translucent track with a hairline on its inner edge comes in, and it stays shown while the pointer is there, as a Mac's does even over a scrollbar that was hidden. Both bars shown, each track stops short of the other's corner. Every edge is on a whole pixel of the display (`scrollbarGeometry`), so it is crisp at 1.25.
   - **Colours:** NSScroller's knob styles: a dark thumb (black at 50%, a white hairline at 35%) in the light appearance and a light one (white at 50%, a black hairline at 25%) in the dark; `indicatorStyle` `black` or `white` chooses one. The track is near-white (#fafafa at 80%) or near-black (#2b2b2b at 80%).
   - **Props:** `showsVerticalScrollIndicator` and `showsHorizontalScrollIndicator` hide one; Android's `persistentScrollbar` (`setScrollbarFadingEnabled(false)`) keeps them shown at rest. All three are in React Native's portable `BaseScrollViewProps`.
   - **The pointer on it:** a press on a scrollbar that shows is the scrollbar's, as a Mac's scroller takes it: nothing under it is pressed (no pointer event, no touch). On the thumb it drags, the content following the pointer by the track's free length to the content's, as Android's `View#handleScrollBarDragging` moves a `ScrollView`; React hears what Android's `ReactScrollView` sends for it: `onScrollBeginDrag` as it is taken (`handleInterceptedTouchEvent`), `onScroll` as it moves, and `onScrollEndDrag` (velocity 0) as it is let go, which Android does not send (`View.dispatchTouchEvent` gives the release to the scrollbar before `ReactScrollView.onTouchEvent` would end the drag), so that the drag JavaScript was told began also ends. A press in the track pages towards it, animated, as NSScroller pages, and again every 100 ms after 400 ms while held, until the thumb reaches the pointer; Android's track does nothing.
   - **Damage:** a change of what shows damages the bands along the view's right and bottom edges only, not its content.

3. **Keyboard scrolling, as a Mac's scroll view takes the keys its first responder leaves** and Android's `ScrollView.executeKeyEvent` takes them for React:
   - **The keys and their steps:** Page Down and Page Up, Space and Shift+Space, a page; ↓ and ↑ a line down a view, → and ← across one; Home and End, and Ctrl+↓ and Ctrl+↑ (a Mac's ⌘↓ and ⌘↑), to the ends. The steps are Safari's WebKit's on a Mac (and Chromium's): a line is 40 logical pixels (`Scrollbar::pixelsPerLineStep`), a page the view's length less the 40 kept in sight, at least seven eighths of it (`Scrollbar::pageStep`). Android's own steps are not taken: its page is the whole height and moves the focus into the new page, and its arrows move the focus to the next control (`arrowScroll`), which on a Mac is Tab's.
   - **The motion:** animated as Android's `ScrollView.smoothScrollBy` animates them (`OverScroller.startScroll`: 250 ms on Scroller's viscous-fluid curve), stepped once a frame; React hears only its `onScroll` events, as Android sends for one. A key pressed while one animates (a key held down, repeating) goes on from where that one was going, so the view glides on as a Mac's does under a repeating key, where Android's `ScrollView` jumps (`ANIMATED_SCROLL_GAP`). Offsets land on the display's pixels.
   - **Which scroll view:** the innermost around the keyboard's focus that can move that way, outward when it cannot (nested scroll views, as a key goes up Android's view tree); with the focus in no scroll view (or none at all), the window's main one, the largest that scrolls that way (the main one across for → and ←). A dialog popup's and a shell region's keys scroll theirs; a menu's keys are its own.
   - **What keeps its keys:** a focused text field keeps every one of them ([ADR 0037](./0037-text-editing)); a focused control keeps those it uses, before scrolling: Space and Return on a button, the arrows on an adjustable control ([ADR 0039](./0039-keyboard-access)), and the keys Bloom's own controls take (offered to JavaScript first: a segmented control's arrows, a list's Space). Shift with any key but Space, Alt and Super scroll nothing.
   - **Nothing leaked:** a key a scroll view takes reaches nothing else, nor its release. A scroll view at its end still takes the key, as a Mac's does, and nothing moves or hears it.

4. **Tab brings the focused control into view in every scroll view it is in** (`PointerInput::reveal`), innermost first, as Android's `ScrollView.requestChildFocus` does for each: at once, by the least move that shows it (`computeScrollDeltaToGetChildRectOnScreen`: the bottom brought up, or the top down; a control taller than the view shows its top), never past the content's end, one scroll event each. What it brings into view is the control with its focus ring round it (`kFocusRingReach`, 5 logical pixels, on the display's pixels), so the ring is never cut by the scroll view's edge. The rectangle an outer scroll view brings in is the control's as the inner one shows it (clipped by it), as AppKit's `scrollRectToVisible` passes a rectangle up its clip views; React Native's Android `ReactScrollView.scrollToChild` brings the whole inner scroll view instead. Offsets are worked out from the host's own offsets, not the tree's, which the commit carrying an inner scroll may not have reached yet. A modal's window, a popup's or a shell region's surface is drawn on its own: the scroll views its view sits in in the tree do not hold it and stay where they are (the first version of this decision scrolled Settings' page 2,600 pixels behind its display trial dialog as Tab reached the dialog's buttons).

5. **JavaScript's ScrollView commands, as `ReactScrollViewManager` receives them:** `scrollTo` [x, y, animated] and `scrollToEnd` [animated] stop a glide or an animation first (`abortAnimation`), then go at once (one `onScroll`) or animated as `ReactScrollViewHelper.smoothScrollTo` animates them: an animator of the default scroll duration (250 ms) on `AccelerateDecelerateInterpolator`, its momentum events framing it (`startFlingAnimator`; sent whether JavaScript listens or not, as the host's momentum events are, the Scrolling row). `scrollToEnd` goes to the end of the axis the content scrolls along. A press, a wheel or fingers stop an animated scroll, as a touch aborts Android's scroller.

6. **Natural scrolling, a trackpad's and a mouse's, set by libinput** (Settings › Trackpad and Settings › Mouse; the preferences `trackpad_natural_scrolling` and `mouse_natural_scrolling`, [ADR 0019](./0019-settings)):
   - **What it turns:** with natural scrolling the content follows the hand: two fingers moved up move the content up (the view down, its offset growing), a wheel turned toward the person moves it down. Off, the view follows the hand, as a wheel has always scrolled. Only scrolling turns: a pinch, resting fingers and the three- and four-finger swipes (Mission Control and App Exposé, [ADR 0045](./0045-app-expose)) are the same either way, as a Mac's are.
   - **Two switches, not one.** macOS shares one switch between its Trackpad and Mouse pages: turning either turns both. OxyOS keeps them apart, because a wheel's notch turned toward the person scrolling down is what every mouse has done and every mouse user expects, while a touchpad's content following the fingers is how a Mac ships; one switch would make whoever uses both give one up. So the trackpad's is on by default, as a Mac's, and the mouse's off.
   - **Which device is which:** libinput's own. A device that offers tapping (`libinput_device_config_tap_get_finger_count` above 0) is a touchpad, as sway tells touchpads apart, and follows the trackpad's switch; every other device that has the choice (`libinput_device_config_scroll_has_natural_scroll`: a mouse's wheel, a trackpoint, a button's scrolling) follows the mouse's. A device without it (a keyboard, a touchscreen, a tablet) is left as it is.
   - **Where it is applied:** by libinput, the proven engine, as GNOME, KDE and sway apply it (`libinput_device_config_scroll_set_natural_scroll_enabled`). `oxyos-input` sets it on each device as libinput adds it (as the seat opens, a device plugged in, and every device again after a terminal switch's resume), before libinput reads the device's first events, and on every device at once when either preference changes (`Input::set_natural_scrolling`, given by `Settings::apply` through `Compositor::set_natural_scrolling`, and to the devices as the program running the compositor gives them, `Compositor::set_input`): no restart, the next scroll goes the new way. Nothing after libinput turns anything. The scene, every client, the Bloom host's scroll views, its drag and glide, the scrollbars and the dock receive scrolls already in the chosen direction, "down" (`dy` above 0) still the view moving down; the protocol is unchanged.
   - **The dock's icons** ([ADR 0045](./0045-app-expose), decision 3) read the scroll as delivered, as a Mac's Dock does: up (`dy` below 0) opens the application's App Exposé. With a trackpad's natural scrolling, two fingers moved down on an icon open it and up close it, as on a Mac; a wheel turned away opens it, unless the mouse's natural scrolling is on.
   - **Settings:** Mouse and Trackpad after Keyboard, as a Mac's System Settings lists them (`RiMouseLine`; `RiHand` for the trackpad, Remix having no trackpad), each with a "Natural scrolling" switch and macOS's line under it, the same on both as macOS has it: "Content tracks finger movement" ("Desplazamiento natural", "El contenido sigue el movimiento de los dedos"). Each is a Settings switch as every other is, named by its title and reached from the keyboard ([ADR 0039](./0039-keyboard-access)). The sidebar has fifteen rows.

## Tests

Host scenarios at 1 and 1.25 on `sample/ScrollProbe.tsx`, each step checking exactly what React received and how often, the offsets, the keyboard's focus, what the host took and each change of the scrollbars the host reported (`scrollbars`, `scrollbar_thumbs` in `regress.py`), pixels as a complement:

- `scroll-keys`, `scroll-keys-scaled`: every key above with nothing focused (the main list, the strip across), in a field (kept), on an adjustable stepper (kept), on a row (the row's list, its Space a press), at the end (taken, nothing moves), and with the focus on a button in no scroll view (the main list).
- `scroll-reveal`, `scroll-reveal-scaled`: a sheet (a `Modal` inside the main list in the tree) opened from the keyboard, Tab through it and Escape, nothing behind it scrolling; Tab down every row, along the chips (a scroll view in a scroll view) and back, each offset exact, one scroll event each, no scrollbar; Shift+Tab to a chip out of view in both scroll views brings it in through both.
- `scrollbars`, `scrollbars-scaled` (light at 1, dark at 1.25): the wheel shows and fades the thumb; no bar where the indicator is hidden; a persistent bar at rest; the pointer over the bar brings it wide with its track (pixels); its thumb dragged (a drag to React, no press below, the offset by the track's ratio); a press in the track pages; the pointer leaving fades it.
- `scroll-commands`, `scroll-commands-scaled`: `scrollTo` animated (momentum begin, scrolls, end, the offset), `scrollToEnd` at once, `flashScrollIndicators` (shown, faded, nothing scrolled), no scrollbar for the scrolls.

Natural scrolling (decision 6) is libinput's, so its scenarios scroll through it: the development compositor's virtual touchpad and wheel mouse (`--uinput`, a scenario's `uinput`), which only root may make. The touchpad scrolls with two fingers 20 mm apart moving together (`Devices::two_fingers`), resting before they lift so nothing is flung; a `scroll`, `scroll_output` or `scroll_named` step through the devices is the hand's, the wheel turned toward the person or the fingers moved down for a positive `dy`. Each step checks the scrolls the client's host received (`scroll_input`: every one that moved one way, and how many), React's events and that the scroll view only moved that way (`scroll_moved`):

- `natural-scrolling`, `natural-scrolling-scaled` (in Spanish, at 1.25): Settings with ScrollProbe beside it. Two fingers moved up scroll its `main` list down, its content following them (the trackpad's default), and the wheel turned toward the person scrolls it down (the mouse's); Settings › Trackpad's switch, on, turned off, kept in the file, and the same fingers scroll the list up at once; Settings › Mouse's, off, turned on, and the same wheel scrolls it up. Settings hears none of the scrolls.
- `natural-scrolling-dock`, `natural-scrolling-dock-scaled` (at 1.25 with both preferences turned from the defaults): Allo's two windows and the shell. Two fingers moved down on Allo's dock icon open its App Exposé and up close it, the wheel turned away opens it and toward closes it; at 1.25, the other way round for each. The shell's host receives every scroll up, or down, as the step says; neither application's React tree hears anything.

Unit tests: the two preferences' defaults, file and refused values (`oxyos-preferences`); a touchpad following the trackpad's switch and every other device the mouse's (`oxyos-input`); the compositor given the preferences as they are and at once on a change Settings asks for (`settings.rs`).

## Not in this decision

- Snapping (`snapToInterval`, `snapToOffsets`, `pagingEnabled`), a glide's end left where the spline puts it.
- macOS's rubber band (OxyOS follows Android 12's stretch) and the thumb shrinking while it shows.
- Scrollbars flashing as a scroll view first appears; a preference to always show scrollbars (a Mac's "Show scroll bars: Always").
- Option+click in the track (jump to the spot), and an accessibility scroll action's animation.
