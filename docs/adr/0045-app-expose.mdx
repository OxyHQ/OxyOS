# ADR 0045: App Exposé

Status: **accepted; implemented**. Builds on [ADR 0009](./0009-session-shell) (the shell's surfaces), [ADR 0011](./0011-transitions) (transitions and the session's springs, which this extends: its "Not in this decision"'s windows flying to their places), [ADR 0022](./0022-window-views) (windows shown live, which this extends with windows *moved* into their views), [ADR 0024](./0024-system-shortcuts) (system shortcuts), [ADR 0033](./0033-protocol-versions) (protocol version 24), [ADR 0034](./0034-the-dock) (the dock's menu, whose Show All Windows this replaces), [ADR 0039](./0039-keyboard-access) (keyboard access and the screen reader) and [ADR 0043](./0043-the-genie) (the genie, by which a minimized window comes back).

## Problem

The dock's menu had Show All Windows, as a Mac's Dock has, but it opened the overview with the application's windows alone: Mission Control's desktop cards, filtered. A Mac's opens App Exposé, which is something else: one application's windows laid out as large as they fit across the screen, every other window gone, the desktop dimmed behind them, the application's minimized windows in a row at the bottom; the windows are the windows themselves, not cards, and they fly from where they are to their places as it opens and back as it closes. A Mac opens it from the Dock's menu, with Control-↓, with three (or four) fingers swiped down on the trackpad, and, as an option, with a scroll up on a Dock icon; Control-↑ and the swipe up are Mission Control, and either way back closes what the other opened.

OxyOS had none of that beyond the filtered overview: no shortcut for Mission Control or App Exposé, no swipe (libinput's swipe gesture went nowhere), no scroll on a dock icon (React Native has no wheel event a plain view receives), and window views that could only be copies drawn over a window still where it is (ADR 0022).

## Options

**Where the windows fly.** (a) *The shell animates its window views*, a Reanimated spring per view, the host sending each view's new place with each frame of the overlay, as the overview's dragged card is sent. Each frame of the flight would be a frame of the shell's overlay, drawn on the host's CPU (about 20 ms for a window's frame, ADR 0004) and composed after, behind the display; the windows' own places would still be drawn under the overlay, so the shell would have to hide them too, which it cannot. (b) *The compositor flies them*, as it already moves every surface it animates (ADR 0011): the shell says once that its surface's windows are moved into its views; the compositor draws each window in its view alone, and draws the view from the window's place to its slot on the overlay's own spring, on the display's clock, whole and filtered, leaving the window's place empty. Nothing of the shell is drawn again during the flight. (b), as a Mac's window server moves the windows themselves for Mission Control and the Dock process draws only its chrome.

**Show All Windows.** Keep the filtered overview beside App Exposé, or replace it. Two ways to show one application's windows would be two layouts, two keyboards and two sets of strings for the same intent. Replaced: Show All Windows is App Exposé, and the overview shows every window again.

**Its layout.** Its own, or the overview's rows (ADR 0022's cards: a row's windows one height keeping their proportions, fitting the width). The overview's, moved to `overview-geometry.ts` (`windowRows`) and used by both; App Exposé picks the number of columns that gives the windows the most room, never draws a window larger than it is, and centres the rows (`exposeLayout`).

## Decision

1. **App Exposé is the shell's, over a dimmed desktop** (`apps/shell/src/components/AppExpose.tsx`). Two surfaces covering the screen but the dock, as Launchpad's: a backdrop with the wallpaper dimmed (`AppExposeBackdrop`), which the compositor only fades, so the other applications' windows fade away under it; and an overlay with the application's icon and name at the top, its open windows laid out large between (the one used last first), and its minimized windows in a row at the bottom below a line. An application with one window has App Exposé too, the window at its own size in the middle. Each window is the platform's `WindowView` (ADR 0022) inside one button named by its title; the pointer over it, or the keyboard on it, rings it and shows its title under it.

2. **Windows moved into their views** (`MoveWindowsToViews { surface, moved }`, request 94, protocol version 24; the shell's `Surface` prop `movesWindows`). While a surface whose windows are moved is shown, each window it shows is drawn in its view alone, never where it is. As the surface arrives, a view first shown with it flies from where its window is drawn to its slot: its rectangle and clip each edge from the one to the other, its corners from the window's radius to the view's, whole (not faded with the overlay), on the overlay's motion (`OVERLAY_ARRIVES`, 320 ms critically damped: the same spring the overview settles on). As the surface goes, each view that flew there flies back, from its last frame, to where its window is drawn then, on `OVERLAY_GOES`, landing in the windows' order (the one chosen, now in front, last); the window is drawn where it is again once its view is there. A window that was not drawn when the surface arrived (minimized, on another desktop, hidden) does not fly: its view fades in and out with the surface. A window coming out of the dock as the surface goes (a minimized one chosen) comes out through the genie (ADR 0043), never flying. With Reduce Motion nothing flies: the views are copies that crossfade with their surface, the windows staying where they are under the backdrop. The scene marks the views (`RenderItem::View`'s `moved`); the compositor keeps where each flight began (`flights`) and draws it (`between`); a window moved out is left out with its popups and dialogs. The shell never moves a window: every window is where it was when App Exposé closes.

3. **Ways in, and back.**
   - **The dock's menu**, Show All Windows ([ADR 0034](./0034-the-dock), amended).
   - **Super+↓**, a Mac's Control-↓: App Exposé of the active application (`SystemShortcut::AppExpose`, 13). **Super+↑**, Control-↑: Mission Control, the overview (`MissionControl`, 12). Each again, or the other, closes what is open. The scene's, as Super's keys are: no application hears them.
   - **Three or four fingers swiped down** on a touchpad: App Exposé; **up**: Mission Control (`Scene::pointer_swipe`, from libinput's swipe gesture, `oxyos_input::Action::Swipe`). Once the fingers have gone 40 logical pixels up or down, further than across, the shell of the display under the pointer is told the shortcut, once a swipe. *Amended by [ADR 0046](./0046-interactive-gestures) (5 November 2026): the swipes follow the fingers, the windows as far along as they have gone, and the shell is told Open as they begin and Close if they let it go back; across slides the desktops.* They are the system's: no application is told of them, as libinput never reports two fingers as a swipe (a scroll, a pinch). Across does nothing yet. The same gesture the other way closes what it opened: the swipe up from App Exposé, the swipe down from Mission Control.
   - **A scroll up on an application's dock icon**, as a Mac's Dock offers, and down closes it again: a wheel's notch at once, a touchpad's two fingers once they have gone 12 points, once until they lift (`dock.ts`, `iconScroll`). "Up" is the scroll whose content moves toward its top (`dy` below 0), as a wheel turned away. A plain view has no scroll event in React Native; the host gives the scroll no scroll view or text input under the pointer took to the innermost `OxyOSScrollTarget` around it, as an Android view takes the `ACTION_SCROLL` its children left, and the shell's platform offers it as `ScrollTarget` (`apps/shell/src/platform.tsx`), which the dock wraps each application's icon in.
   - While App Exposé is open, a press on another application's dock icon shows that application's windows instead, as a Mac's does.

4. **Choosing.** A window pressed (or chosen from the keyboard) is focused, on its desktop, and App Exposé closes: the window comes forward with the keyboard. A minimized one is restored out of the dock through the genie. A press on nothing, Escape, or the gesture or shortcut the other way closes it with nothing chosen; the keyboard goes back to the window that had it.

5. **The keyboard and the screen reader** ([ADR 0039](./0039-keyboard-access)). App Exposé has the keyboard while open, opened from the keyboard or not, on its frontmost window. The arrows go from window to window (`neighbour`: ← → along a row, past its end to the next in order; ↑ ↓ to the nearest above or below), Tab between the open windows and the minimized row (each area one stop, on its current window), Return and Space choose. The surface is a dialog named by the application ("Ventanas de Allo"); each window a button named by its title and its place among its area's windows (`accessibilityCollectionItem` in an `accessibilityCollection`: "Allo, botón, 2 de 2").

6. **Protocol version 24** ([ADR 0033](./0033-protocol-versions)), added only: request 94 `MoveWindowsToViews { surface, moved }` (the shell, on its own shell surfaces: any other client `Unauthorized`, any other surface `InvalidRole`); `SystemShortcut` values 12 and 13, never sent to a shell of version 23 or older. In the C ABI: `oxyos_client_move_windows_to_views`, `OXYOS_SHORTCUT_MISSION_CONTROL` and `OXYOS_SHORTCUT_APP_EXPOSE`. The Bloom host tells a compositor of version 23 nothing of moved windows, and App Exposé's views are then copies.

## Tests

- **Protocol:** the request round-trips and is version 24's; the shortcuts are never written for version 23 and refused by its reader; the closed set of shortcuts.
- **Scene** (`scene/tests/expose.rs`): Super+↑ and Super+↓ tell the shell Mission Control and App Exposé, a menu open in the window closing first, the window hearing no key; three fingers up and four down tell it once each however far they go, the window under them told nothing; short of the distance, across, or cancelled, nothing; locked, nothing; only the shell moves windows into its own shell surface's views.
- **Compositor**, through real sockets on the test's clock (`tests/moved_views.rs`): a window moved into the overlay's view is drawn, at the overlay's first frame, exactly where it is; at 80 ms on its way, more than 40 % of it (eased out); at the end in its view, and nothing of it where it is; closing, it flies back from its view, every frame further, and is drawn where it is, the window never having moved; with Reduce Motion nothing flies and the window stays where it is.
- **C ABI:** the request sent as the protocol has it, the constants and the header.
- **The shell** (`apps/shell/tests`): `windowRows` as the overview had its rows; `exposeLayout` keeps proportions, never enlarges a window, keeps them apart and inside the area, one window at its own size centred, two side by side; `neighbour`'s arrows; `exposedWindows` (the one used last first, the minimized apart, a window the session did not start not the application's); `iconScroll` (a notch at once, the fingers once past 12 points and once until they lift, across never).
- **Scenarios** (`regress.py`, the development compositor; each at 1 and at 1.25, the ThinkPad's, `-scaled`), with Allo as two windows (its own and the `companion` the session launched for it, titled Companion). `regress.py` checks the views the compositor drew once a step settled (`window_views`: which windows, moved, apart, inside the work area, the minimized row below the rest), each window's flight frame by frame (`flights`: from exactly where the window is to within a pixel of its view, every edge only ever one way, and back), and the windows' places against an earlier step's (`windows_placed_as`); the director has `swipe`, `scroll_named` and `scroll_output` steps.
  - `app-expose`: Show All Windows from the dock's menu, the two windows flying to their places, the dimmed desktop where they were; a press on Allo's window brings it forward with the keyboard, both flying back to where they always were; Super+M, then Super+↓ with the companion open and Allo alone in the minimized row, which does not fly; a press on it restores it through the genie with the keyboard; a press on nothing closes it.
  - `app-expose-keys`, with the screen reader: Super+↓ with the keyboard on the window used last ("Ventanas de Allo, diálogo", "Companion, botón, 1 de 2"), → and ← each said once and nothing past the last, Return choosing Allo's; with Allo minimized, Tab and Shift+Tab between the open windows and the minimized row, one stop each ("Allo, botón, 1 de 1"), Return restoring it; Escape closing it with the keyboard back with the window.
  - `app-expose-gestures`: three fingers down and up, four fingers up (Mission Control: copies, nothing flies) and down, a wheel's notch up and down on Allo's dock icon, two fingers up (once however far) and down on it, Super+↑ and Super+↓ closing Mission Control, three fingers across doing nothing.
  - `app-expose-reduce-motion` (1.25): one window, the backdrop and overlay crossfading in and out where they are, nothing flying.

  Every step of the three checks that neither application's React tree hears anything and exactly what each window is delivered (its focus going and coming back, the pointer's crossing; never a press, a scroll or a key), and bounds what the shell re-renders (components rendered; exactly its two windows as Tab moves the keyboard between the areas). In the deploy gate.

## Not in this decision

- **Mission Control's windows moved**, **following the fingers** and **three fingers across** between desktops: [ADR 0046](./0046-interactive-gestures).
- A natural scrolling preference (which would turn which way a dock icon's scroll is "up" for a touchpad).
- **Recent documents** below the windows, which a Mac's App Exposé shows for document applications.
