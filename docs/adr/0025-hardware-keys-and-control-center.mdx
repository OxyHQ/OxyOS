# ADR 0025: Hardware keys and Control Center

Status: **accepted; implemented** (the protocol, the scene, the session's controls, the C ABI, the Bloom host and the shell); what only the ThinkPad can show is in [What to verify on the ThinkPad](#what-to-verify-on-the-thinkpad). Builds on [ADR 0006](./0006-seat) (logind), [ADR 0009](./0009-session-shell) (the shell and its grants), [ADR 0014](./0014-screen-reader) (announcements), [ADR 0018](./0018-several-displays) (a shell per display), [ADR 0019](./0019-settings) (the session keeps the preferences) and [ADR 0024](./0024-system-shortcuts) (keys the system takes before applications).

## Problem

On the owner's ThinkPad P14s Gen 3 (Intel, Debian trixie, PipeWire, logind) the volume, mute, microphone mute and brightness keys did nothing in an OxyOS session: the keys reached the focused application as ordinary keys, and nothing acted on the machine. The shell's top bar drew a battery and a Wi-Fi icon that were pictures ("Indicadores de ejemplo, sin conexión al sistema"), and its "Controles del sistema" button opened Settings. On a Mac those keys change the level whatever has the keyboard and show it in a bezel, the menu bar shows the real battery and Wi-Fi, and Control Center holds Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, the display, the sound and the appearance.

What the ThinkPad has (looked at read-only, `ssh` to it, nothing changed):
- **Keys:** the volume keys from the AT keyboard (codes 113–115), brightness from the ACPI "Video Bus" (224, 225), and from "ThinkPad Extra Buttons" the microphone's mute (248) and the keyboard backlight's (228, `KEY_KBDILLUMTOGGLE`); Fn+Space changes the keyboard backlight in the firmware itself, which the kernel reports (`tpacpi::kbd_backlight/brightness_hw_changed`).
- **Backlights:** `/sys/class/backlight/intel_backlight` (`raw`, 0–19393) and `/sys/class/leds/tpacpi::kbd_backlight` (0–2); logind's session object has `SetBrightness` (`ssu`).
- **Audio:** PipeWire 1.4.2 with WirePlumber 0.5.8; `wpctl get-volume @DEFAULT_AUDIO_SINK@` answers `Volume: 0.41`; the mute and microphone-mute LEDs are `platform::mute` and `platform::micmute`, which the kernel's audio LED triggers drive from ALSA's switches.
- **Power and network:** UPower 1.90.9 (`DisplayDevice`: 78 %, charging, `TimeToFull`), NetworkManager 1.52.1 on `wlp0s20f3` (connected, 73 %), BlueZ 5.82 with a powered adapter. Debian trixie's desktop tasks install NetworkManager, and OxyOS's image manifest has `network-manager`, `pipewire-audio` (which brings WirePlumber), `upower` and `bluez`.

## Decision

1. **The session owns the machine's controls** (`crates/oxyos-controls`, run by `oxyos-compositor`'s `controls.rs`), as it owns the preferences (ADR 0019): it validates every change, applies it, reads back what the system did and tells the shell. Nothing here is the shell's or an application's to do; the shell shows and asks.
   - **Volume: WirePlumber's `wpctl`**, a process per call with a 2 s deadline, on PipeWire's default output and input (`@DEFAULT_AUDIO_SINK@`, `@DEFAULT_AUDIO_SOURCE@`): `get-volume`, `set-volume` (a fraction, never above 1) and `set-mute`. Why not PipeWire's native API: its Rust binding (`pipewire`) builds libpipewire's headers with `bindgen` and libclang, links the C library into the session and needs its own main loop and object tracking for the default node, which is WirePlumber's to choose; WirePlumber has no D-Bus interface; and PulseAudio's protocol through `pipewire-pulse` would make the compatibility server the session's interface. `wpctl` is WirePlumber's own tool, shipped by the package that keeps the defaults, the established way PipeWire desktops' keys are bound, and it applies WirePlumber's volume (its cubic scale, as GNOME's slider and `pavucontrol` show it). It is robust as used: the output is one line, parsed strictly (`Volume: 0.41` with an optional `[MUTED]`); a failing `wpctl` (no default device, no PipeWire) is a missing control, not an error; one that does not answer within the deadline is killed, and the call fails, never the session. The screen reader's `espeak-ng` ([ADR 0014](./0014-screen-reader)) is the precedent of the system's own program run by the session.
   - **Brightness: logind's `SetBrightness`** on the session, as ADR 0006 foresaw: no root and no writable sysfs, logind writes it for the session's user while the session is the active one. The display's device is the most trusted of `/sys/class/backlight` (`firmware`, then `platform`, then `raw`, as systemd and GNOME choose); the keyboard's is the `leds` device whose name ends in `kbd_backlight`. Both are read from sysfs (`brightness`, `max_brightness`), every time, so a backlight that appears later is found. One with `brightness_hw_changed` is changed by the firmware itself: its toggle key only says it did, and the session reads it and never sets it (else a ThinkPad's Fn+Space would be undone).
   - **Battery: UPower**, over the system bus (`zbus`, already the session's for logind): its display device (every battery as one: state, percentage, time to empty or full) and `OnBattery`. Not sysfs's `power_supply`: UPower is in the image and on every Debian desktop, aggregates several batteries, smooths the times and knows a charge threshold's "pending charge"; reading sysfs as well would be a second path to keep right. Without UPower there is no battery, and the top bar shows none.
   - **Network: NetworkManager**, over the system bus: Wi-Fi's state (no device, off by software or by the hardware switch, disconnected, connecting, connected), the active access point's name (its bytes as text, cut to one 32-byte chunk at a whole character) and signal, and whether a wired connection is up. **Wi-Fi on and off is in scope:** it is NetworkManager's `WirelessEnabled` property, which polkit gives the user of the active local session without asking (`org.freedesktop.NetworkManager.enable-disable-wifi`, `allow_active=yes`), as `nmcli radio wifi` does. Choosing a network and passwords are not.
   - **Bluetooth: status only**, from BlueZ's object manager (the adapter powered or not, devices connected). Turning it on and off, pairing and devices are a decision of their own.
   - **No call waits in the compositor's turn.** Every call may wait on another process or the bus, so the session runs them on a thread of their own (`Worker::spawn`), which wakes the turn through a socket when it has answers. A slider dragged faster than `wpctl` follows sets where it is, not every place it was (`coalesce`). The battery, the network and Bluetooth are looked at every 5 s and when the Control Center opens; the levels every 30 s (another program may change them) and when it opens. The bus and logind are reached when first needed and again after a failure.
2. **The hardware keys are the system's** (`scene/controls.rs`), as Super's chords are (ADR 0024): Mute, Volume Down, Volume Up (113–115), Brightness Down and Up (224, 225), the keyboard backlight's toggle, down and up (228–230) and the microphone's mute (248). The compositor takes them before the system shortcuts, the input method and every application, press and release, with any modifier held and with Super held; no application or input method ever receives them. **Locked, they still act**, as a Mac's do; the lock screen receives none.
   - **By key code**, the kernel's, which XKB's evdev keymaps name `XF86AudioMute`, `XF86AudioLowerVolume`, `XF86AudioRaiseVolume`, `XF86MonBrightnessDown`, `XF86MonBrightnessUp`, `XF86KbdLightOnOff`, `XF86KbdBrightnessDown`, `XF86KbdBrightnessUp` and `XF86AudioMicMute` on every layout (`oxyos-input` tests it on us, es, fr and de); they type nothing. As ADR 0024 decided, the scene has no keymap, and a keymap that moves them is not followed.
   - **Precedence:** the keymap's own, then the screen reader's keys, then these, then the system shortcuts, the input method and the application.
   - **Steps as a Mac's:** sixteen steps from nothing to all, a key moving to the next step beyond (a level between two goes to the one beyond); with Shift and Alt (Option) held, a quarter of a step; never past either end, where the bezel still shows the level. A device with fewer brightnesses than sixteen steps in its own (the ThinkPad's keyboard: off, low, high). Volume keys unmute; Mute and the microphone's mute toggle; the keyboard's toggle turns it off and back to where it was. The display may go to nothing, as a Mac's lowest step does.
   - **At once:** a key's new level is told to the shell in the same turn, from the level the system last said, and the system is asked after; its answer follows and corrects it if it did otherwise. A key with nothing to act on (no default output, no keyboard backlight) shows nothing.
3. **The bezel is the shell's**, as the switcher is (ADR 0024: the shell owns what needs a picture, the compositor what acts): a macOS-classic bezel (`Bezel.tsx`), a dark rounded square above the dock with the control's icon (speaker, muted speaker, microphone, sun, keyboard) and its level in sixteen segments, shown for 1.5 s from each press and then faded over 0.3 s. Not compositor-drawn: the compositor draws no text or icons of its own besides the splash, and the shell's bezel is Bloom's, accessible and localized like the rest of the shell. It is a `notification` shell surface (above the overlays, below the lock), which takes no input; while the session is locked it is drawn in the lock screen's surface. The compositor sends the key's level with `bezel` to the shell of the output in front (the active window's, else the pointer's) that holds `shortcuts`, as the switcher's; every shell is told the level itself. **Accessible:** each press is said to the screen reader ("Volumen, 56 %", "Sonido silenciado", "Micrófono silenciado", "Brillo, 69 %"), and the bezel is a progress bar named by its control with its level as value. The bezel shows the level as the session says it now, so it follows the system's answer while shown.
4. **The Control Center** (`ControlCenter.tsx`) opens from the top bar's button (now "Centro de control"), a panel under the top bar's right end in an overlay, closed by a press outside it, as a Mac's: **Wi-Fi** (a switch, the network's name or its state), **Bluetooth** (what it is: off, on, how many devices; not a switch), **Dark Mode** (a switch), the **display's brightness**, the **keyboard's** if the machine has one, the **sound** (its icon mutes it) as sliders, each one element for the assistive technology with its value and a sixteenth up or down as its actions, the **battery** in words, and "Ajustes del sistema…", which opens Settings. **Do Not Disturb is omitted**: OxyOS has no notifications yet. What the session has not said, or the machine does not have, is left out. Opening it looks at the machine again. The top bar shows the battery (its charge filled in, a bolt while charging, red at 10 % and below) and Wi-Fi (its signal in arcs, faint when off or not connected) only as the session says them, each named for the assistive technology ("Batería: 64 %, 3 h 20 min restantes", "Wi-Fi: LukeSkyRouter"); without them, none.
5. **The appearance goes the settings' way.** Dark Mode is the user's `appearance` preference (ADR 0019), so the shell asks for it with `SetAppearance`, and the scene hands it to the session as a preference change like Settings' (validated, written to the preferences file, told to every client, answered with `PreferenceResult`). The shell does not get the `settings` grant: it may change this preference alone.
6. **The protocol** (the `shell` grant), in the ranges kept for this decision:
   - **Events, to every shell, when it connects and as they change:** `ControlLevel` (110: the control, its level 0–65536, muted, available, `bezel`), `Power` (111: the battery's state, percent, on AC, minutes until empty or full), `Network` (112: Wi-Fi's state, signal, wired, the SSID in one chunk), `Bluetooth` (113: state, devices connected).
   - **Requests:** `SetControlLevel` (120), `SetControlMuted` (121), `SetWifiEnabled` (122), `SetAppearance` (123, with a serial) and `RefreshControls` (124). A level past 65536 is `InvalidMessage`; a client without `shell` is closed (`Unauthorized`); while locked none is taken but `RefreshControls` (`SetAppearance` is answered refused).
   - **The session is the authority:** the scene keeps only what the session said and hands it the keys and requests; the session validates, applies through the machine and tells what the machine then has. The shell's requests are intents; the next events say what happened.

## Results

- **Protocol:** the `Control`, `BatteryState`, `WifiState` and `BluetoothState` wire enums, `FULL_LEVEL`, events 110–113 and requests 120–124. **C ABI:** `OXYOS_EVENT_CONTROL_LEVEL`, `_POWER`, `_NETWORK`, `_BLUETOOTH`, `OXYOS_CONTROL_*` (controls and the muted, available and bezel bits), `OXYOS_BATTERY_*`, `OXYOS_WIFI_*`, `OXYOS_BLUETOOTH_*`, and `oxyos_client_set_control_level`, `_set_control_muted`, `_set_wifi_enabled`, `_set_appearance`, `_refresh_controls`.
- **The scene** (`scene/controls.rs`): `hardware_key` in `Scene::key` and `Scene::keyboard` after the reader's and before the system shortcuts; `take_control_requests`, `set_control_level`, `set_power`, `set_network`, `set_bluetooth`; a shell given the grant is told everything known.
- **`crates/oxyos-controls`:** `System` (the machine), `Session` (`wpctl`, sysfs and logind, UPower, NetworkManager, BlueZ), `Fake` (a machine described by `key = value` lines, which writes back what was done to it), `stepped`, `Worker` (inline or on a thread) and `coalesce`. `oxyos-seat`'s `Brightness` is logind's `SetBrightness` on the caller's session, on a connection of its own.
- **The session** (`oxyos-compositor`'s `controls.rs`, `Compositor::controls`): keys to levels, the Control Center's requests, the periodic looks and the worker's answers to the scene, served in every turn; the worker's socket wakes the turn. `oxyos-session` gives the compositor a `Session` on a worker thread, on the login screen too.
- **The development compositor:** `--controls FILE` plays a machine (`Fake`, acted on in the turn so a scenario is the same every run), each step's result says it (`controls`), and `--wait MS` serves the session that long with no input (what fades on its own clock). `regress.py` writes a scenario's `controls` to that file and checks `controls`; `preferences_file` is checked for every scenario.
- **The Bloom host:** `ShellState` keeps the controls (`controls` in `OxyOSShell`'s state); a level with `bezel` is `OxyOSShellBezel` to JavaScript; `OxyOSShell.setControlLevel`, `setControlMuted`, `setWifiEnabled`, `setAppearance` and `refreshControls`; `notification` shell surfaces (no input); an element's `accessibilityValue` is its value for the assistive technology.
- **The shell:** `ShellControls` in its model, `controls.ts` (the bezel's segments, what it says, the battery, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth in words), `Bezel`, `ControlCenter`, the top bar's real battery and Wi-Fi; the shell entry builds them from `OxyOSShell`. Where the shell runs without a session (the window probe, `shell.json`; the browser preview) it is given a played machine of its own, a full battery and Wi-Fi, so its top bar is drawn as before.

## Tests

- **Protocol and C ABI:** every new message round-trips; the four enums are closed sets; the table matches `native-foundation.mdx`; the header's constants and declarations match; each event is converted field by field; each request is sent as the compositor reads it, and a bad control or level is refused before it is.
- **Scene** (`scene/tests/controls.rs`, 8 tests): every hardware key's press and release, with Ctrl, with Super and through `Scene::key`, reaches no application (Ctrl's own press and release do, exactly) and each press is handed over once, in order; an ordinary key is the application's; Shift+Alt is a fine step, Shift or Alt alone is not; the input method never sees one; locked, they are handed over and the lock screen receives none, its password's keys still its own; the output in front is named and only that output's shell is told `bezel`, a level unchanged is told to it alone; the shell is told what changed, a late shell everything, applications nothing; the Control Center's requests need `shell`, a level past all is `InvalidMessage`, locked only a refresh is taken; the appearance becomes a preference change, refused while locked.
- **The session's controls** (`oxyos-compositor`, `controls/tests.rs`, 11 tests, on a `Fake`): the shell is told the machine at start (and a bare machine's levels unavailable); volume keys step and unmute, Shift+Alt a quarter, twenty presses stop at all with a bezel each, and the application is told nothing; mute and the microphone's mute toggle; brightness to nothing and back to all in sixteen; the keyboard's three brightnesses and its toggle back to where it was; a firmware-changed keyboard light read, never set; a key with nothing to act on shows nothing; the Control Center sets levels, mutes and Wi-Fi, and its refresh sees another program's change; the battery after 5 s, the levels after 30 s; locked, keys act and requests do not; an SSID cut at a whole character.
- **`oxyos-controls`** (12 tests): sixteen steps up and down, between steps, fine steps, a two-step keyboard; `wpctl`'s output parsed, the commands it is run with (a script in its place), no device as none, a `wpctl` that never answers failing at the deadline; the backlights chosen as systemd does, the ThinkPad's keyboard LED and its firmware flag, raw values through the setter; a `Session` over a described sysfs, a script `wpctl` and a recording setter; UPower's, NetworkManager's and BlueZ's states mapped; the `Fake`'s file read, changed and written back; commands answered with what the system then has, inline and on a thread; level sets coalesced.
- **`oxyos-input`:** the nine codes are their XF86 keys on us, es, fr and de, with Shift too, and type nothing.
- **The shell** (`tests/controls.test.ts`): segments, percentages and silence; what the bezel says; the battery, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth in words.
- **Host scenario** `scenarios/control-center.json`, Allo (`EditingProbe`, its field focused) and the shell over a played ThinkPad, every step with exact `react` and `delivered` (Allo's): the top bar names the battery and Wi-Fi as played; Volume Up (0.5 → 0.5625, the bezel's pixels with nine segments filled, a `ProgressBar` "Volumen" valued "56 %", "Volumen, 56 %" said), Volume Down, Mute (muted, no segment), Volume Up unmuting, Shift+Alt+Volume Up (0.578125; Shift and Alt themselves reach Allo, as a Mac's modifiers do), the microphone's mute, Brightness Down and Up (0.6875, 0.75), each changing the played machine exactly and Allo receiving nothing; 2.2 s later the bezel is gone (its pixels and node); the Control Center opens from the top bar (a `Menu`, the Wi-Fi switch checked, Bluetooth "1 dispositivo", Dark Mode unchecked, the `Slider`s "Pantalla" and "Sonido", the battery in words); a press at a quarter of the sound's slider sets 0.25, at the middle of the display's 0.5; Wi-Fi's switch turns the machine's Wi-Fi off (the switch, its pixels and the top bar say "Desactivada") and on again (connected); Dark Mode writes `appearance = dark` in the preferences file and Allo is told dark; a press outside closes it; the control, "ok" typed, is Allo's. It passes. **With the compositor not taking the hardware keys**, 33 expectations fail in all eight key steps (Allo receives every key, no bezel, nothing said, the machine unchanged); **with the session ignoring the Control Center's requests**, 8 fail in the three steps that set the sound, the display and Wi-Fi. Every other host scenario that runs without a display passes with the same build and bundle (36), but `sample.json`, which fails the same way on `main` with Bloom's main branch (Bloom #519's layout).

## What to verify on the ThinkPad

Nothing here could be tried on the machine: its OxyOS session is in use. With this branch's `oxyos-session` and Bloom host and bundle deployed as the previous ADRs' were, in a session on a seat (`loginctl show-session $XDG_SESSION_ID -p Active` says `yes`):

1. **Keys reach the session.** `OXYOS_INPUT_TRACE=1` in the session's environment; press Fn+F1 (mute), F2, F3 (volume), F4 (microphone mute), F5, F6 (brightness) and Fn+Space: each logs `Key { code: 113 | 114 | 115 | 248 | 224 | 225 | 228, … }`, and nothing reaches a focused application (Allo's field types nothing, no shortcut fires).
2. **Volume.** Volume Up from `wpctl get-volume @DEFAULT_AUDIO_SINK@` = 0.41: the bezel shows 7 of 16 segments, then `wpctl` says 0.4375; sixteen presses reach 1.00 and stop; with Shift+Alt one press adds 0.0156. Mute: `[MUTED]` and the speaker's mute LED (`platform::mute`) lights; Volume Up unmutes it and the LED goes out. Played audio follows (`paplay` or `pw-play` of any WAV).
3. **Microphone.** F4: `wpctl get-volume @DEFAULT_AUDIO_SOURCE@` says `[MUTED]` and `platform::micmute` lights; again, both go. If the LED does not follow, WirePlumber's software mute is not reaching ALSA's capture switch: record it as a WirePlumber setting to decide, not a session change.
4. **Display brightness.** F5/F6: `cat /sys/class/backlight/intel_backlight/brightness` moves by 1212 (19393 / 16) per press, from 16897 to 15757 first (the next step below), down to 0 and up to 19393; the session's journal shows no `SetBrightness` refusal. Switch to another terminal (Ctrl+Alt+F3) and press F5: logind refuses an inactive session's `SetBrightness` and the level does not change; back on the session it works again.
5. **Keyboard backlight.** Fn+Space cycles `tpacpi::kbd_backlight` 0 → 1 → 2 → 0 in the firmware; each press shows the bezel at 0, half and all, and `journalctl` shows no `SetBrightness` for `leds` (the session only read it). If the kernel sends no `KEY_KBDILLUMTOGGLE` (step 1 logs nothing for Fn+Space), there is no bezel and nothing else is wrong.
6. **Bezel and screen reader.** The bezel shows on the display with the active window, fades 1.5 s after the last press; with the reader on (Super+F5), each press is said ("Volumen, 50 %"). Locked (Ctrl+Super+Q), the keys still change the levels and the bezel shows over the lock screen; nothing is typed into the password.
7. **Top bar.** The battery icon matches `upower -i /org/freedesktop/UPower/devices/DisplayDevice` (percentage; the bolt while `state: charging`); unplugging AC shows it discharging within 5 s. Wi-Fi's arcs match `nmcli -t -f ACTIVE,SSID,SIGNAL dev wifi` and the assistive name is "Wi-Fi: LukeSkyRouter".
8. **Control Center.** Its Wi-Fi switch off: `nmcli radio wifi` says `disabled` with no password asked (polkit), the top bar's Wi-Fi goes faint; on again, it reconnects. Bluetooth says what `bluetoothctl show` says. The sliders move `wpctl` and the backlight; Dark Mode writes `appearance = dark` to `~/.config/oxyos/preferences` and every application redraws dark. Changing the volume with `wpctl set-volume @DEFAULT_AUDIO_SINK@ 0.2` and opening the Control Center shows 20 %.
9. **No stall.** `systemctl --user stop wireplumber` then a volume key: the bezel does not show (no default device), the session keeps drawing (the pointer moves smoothly), and after `systemctl --user start wireplumber` the next press works.

## Not in this decision

- Media keys (play, pause, next), the calculator, the display switch (`XF86Display`) and the other ThinkPad Extra Buttons.
- Choosing a Wi-Fi network, passwords, VPNs; Bluetooth on and off, pairing and devices; choosing the output device; Do Not Disturb (with notifications); Night Shift; key repeat of the hardware keys (when key repeat comes, they repeat as a Mac's do).
- An ambient light sensor and automatic brightness; external displays' brightness (DDC/CI).
- Signals (PropertiesChanged) in place of looking every few seconds; WirePlumber's own events in place of looking every 30 s.
- Sounds on volume change (a Mac's feedback click).
