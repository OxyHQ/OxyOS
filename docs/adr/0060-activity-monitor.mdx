# ADR 0060: Activity Monitor

Status: **accepted; implemented** (the samplers, the session's side, the application, scenarios at 1 and 1.25). Builds on [ADR 0013](./0013-launching-applications) (the session knows each application's processes), [ADR 0015](./0015-application-model) (a system application built from Bloom), [ADR 0030](./0030-installing-applications) (system grants, the image's policy, the session's vocabulary over `SystemRequest`), [ADR 0033](./0033-protocol-versions) (a version only adds), [ADR 0034](./0034-the-dock) (Quit and Force Quit), [ADR 0057](./0057-battery-and-energy) (each application's energy) and [ADR 0058](./0058-the-platform-api) (the platform API's shape). Leaves for later what [Not in this decision](#not-in-this-decision) says.

## Problem

Nothing on OxyOS said why the machine was slow, what filled its memory or what drained its battery: Settings › Battery listed the applications' energy (ADR 0057) and nothing else of the kind. A Mac has Activity Monitor: five views of every process (CPU, Memory, Energy, Disk, Network), each with the machine's summary and a graph below; the processes grouped by application; search; *Quit* and *Force Quit* after a confirmation; and *Inspect*, a process's parent, memory, open files and ports. It costs almost nothing while open and nothing while hidden.

## Decision

1. **A system application, `so.oxy.activitymonitor`, with a grant of its own: `activity`.** Activity Monitor is OxyOS's (ADR 0015, decision 1; ADR 0058, decision 2: it imports the platform API directly). The image's policy gives it `activity` (ADR 0030, decision 7) and no other application has it: it sees every process of the machine and quits the person's. A grant, not a capability, since nothing installed may ask for it.

2. **The session samples, as the person's user; there is no root process.** The session authority already knows which processes are each application's (it launched them, ADR 0013) and already reads them for the battery's energy (ADR 0057, decision 7). `crates/oxyos-activity` reads `/proc` and `/sys`; `oxyos_compositor::activity` is the session's side, on a thread of its own. What the kernel lets any user read (every process's `stat` and `status`, the machine's counters) is shown for every process; what only a process's own user may read (its storage I/O, its descriptors, its memory page by page) is shown for the person's own. Nothing runs as root for it.

3. **Sampled every two seconds, only while its window is shown.** Activity Monitor follows a view (`activityFollow`); the session tells it a sample at once (half a second after following begins, so the first numbers do not wait a whole interval) and every two seconds (a Mac's *Normally*) while one of its windows is shown: mapped, neither minimized nor hidden, on the desktop shown, the session unlocked and the displays on (`Scene::client_shown`). Otherwise the sampling thread waits on its jobs alone, with no deadline: nothing is read, nothing is sent and the application is woken by nothing (docs/performance.mdx, *Idle*). Each view reads only what its columns need, since reading every process is the whole cost:

   | View | Per process | The machine |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | every view | `stat`: % CPU (its own user and system time over the interval, a hundred for one core), CPU Time, Threads, PID, its parent (for grouping), its start; its user by the file's owner | `/proc/stat` (User, System, Idle), `loadavg`, `meminfo`, `pressure/memory`, `diskstats`, `net/dev` |
   | CPU | Idle Wake Ups: its threads' runs a second (each thread's `schedstat`, `pcount`: a thread that sleeps and wakes runs once per waking); % GPU and GPU Time (its DRM clients' engines, as ADR 0057 reads them) for the applications' processes and the session's | |
   | Memory | `status`: Memory, Swap, resident; GPU memory (`drm-total-*`) for the applications' and the session's | Pressure (PSI), Physical, Used, App, Wired, Compressed (zswap and zram), Cached Files, Swap Used |
   | Energy | Energy Impact | each application's, and its twelve-hour average |
   | Disk | `io` (the person's): Bytes Read and Written, and their rates | the disks' (hardware block devices alone: no partition, loop, zram or device-mapper view counted twice) |
   | Network | none (decision 8) | each interface but the loopback |

   A process is named by its pid and its start (`started`, in the clock's ticks since boot), so a pid the kernel gives another process is never taken for the one before. A `comm` cut at 15 bytes is completed from its command's first argument once per process.

4. **Memory is the process's own: its anonymous memory, resident and swapped, and its GPU memory.** `status`'s `RssAnon` and `VmSwap` are the kernel's counters, read without walking a page. perf.py's footprint (its unique pages, `Private_Clean` and `Private_Dirty`, and its GPU memory) walks every page of `smaps_rollup`, which costs about 70 µs a megabyte mapped: 170 ms for a browser of 2.5 GB, a second and a half for every process of a desktop, every two seconds. The column leaves out a process's clean file pages, which the kernel drops and reads again at will, as a Mac's footprint does; *Inspect* reads `smaps_rollup` for the one process asked and shows perf.py's footprint, unique, proportional, resident, swapped and GPU memory. **Memory pressure** is the kernel's pressure stall information: the share of the interval in which some tasks, and all of them, waited for memory. The graph draws *some*; it is green below 10 %, yellow from 10 %, red from 40 % *some* or 10 % *full*, short of the 60 % at which systemd-oomd ends a unit.

5. **Energy is counted once, as the battery counts it.** A process's *Energy Impact* is its processor and GPU time over the interval, a hundred for one core busy (the GPU's time counted as a core's, as ADR 0057 decision 7 counts it). An application's is its processes'; its *12 hr Power* is the battery's meter's (`oxyos_power::energy::Meter`): Activity Monitor's samples of the applications' use go into that meter (`Power::sample_energy`), which now keeps a checkpoint of each application's use at most once an hour and averages over the newest one at least twelve hours old, or since the application began. Settings › Battery and Activity Monitor therefore say the same; without the battery's part (a development compositor without its played services) the average is not shown. Applications this session ran that have quit are listed with their average, greyed.

6. **Quitting is the person's own processes', confirmed, and never the session's.** Activity Monitor asks *Are you sure you want to quit this process?* with *Force Quit*, *Cancel* and *Quit*, as a Mac does; the request cites the press that confirmed it (`activityQuit`, `needsInput` without one). The session opens a pidfd on the pid, then checks that the process it holds started when the person's choice said (else `notFound`: the pid is another's now) and that its real, effective and saved user are the session's (else `notPermitted`), and signals through the pidfd: SIGTERM to quit, SIGKILL to force. The session's own process and those it runs under are refused: ending one ends the session, which *Log Out* does. An application's row quits as the dock does (`activityQuitApp`): *Quit* is its own close request to each of its windows (ADR 0034, decision 2), *Force Quit* the session's (decision 4), only for clients the session launched. Another user's processes are listed and cannot be quit (decision 7).

7. **Another user's processes need an administrator, whom OxyOS cannot ask yet.** A Mac asks an administrator's password to quit or sample root's processes. On Linux that is a helper run by polkit (`pkexec` and an action of `auth_admin_keep`), and polkit asks the password through the session's **authentication agent**, which OxyOS does not have (ADR 0019, *Not in this decision*), and checks it through PAM, while an OxyOS account's password is a verifier the account service checks (ADR 0020). A helper that no one can authorise would be shipped and never run; it waits for the authentication agent's own decision, which makes it the helper's second half. Until then Activity Monitor shows another user's processes with what any user may read, its *Quit* is disabled for them and *Inspect* says that their files, ports and memory need an administrator.

8. **The network is per interface.** Bytes per process need the kernel to attribute packets to processes: eBPF (a cgroup socket program or a kprobe) needs `CAP_BPF` and `CAP_PERFMON`, a root process; cgroup v2 has no network counters (v1's `net_cls` only tags); NETLINK_SOCK_DIAG gives an unprivileged user each live TCP socket's `bytes_acked` and `bytes_received`, but nothing of UDP (QUIC: a browser's video, most of today's web), nothing of a connection that closed between two samples and nothing of what a socket moved before the first; packet capture needs `CAP_NET_RAW`. A per-process column that silently missed a browser's QUIC traffic would mislead the person it is for, so the Network view lists each interface (Data Received and Sent, Packets In and Out, their rates) and says, small, that each app's network use is not available. A root helper of decision 7's kind could give it with eBPF; it would be its own decision.

9. **The platform API's shape** (ADR 0058, decision 3). The vocabulary is the session's (`activityFollow`, `activityQuit`, `activityQuitApp`, `activityInspect`, strict, every request needing `activity`); in JavaScript it is `@oxyos/platform`'s framework `activity`: `sample` is followed state (`current`, `subscribe`) whose first follower begins following (`activityFollow` with the view) and whose last ends it (`{view: null}`); `setView(view)` keeps the view and tells it at once while followed; `quit(pid, started, force)`, `quitApp(id, force)` and `inspect(pid, started)` are asynchronous calls that reject with a `PlatformError` of the domain `activity`. Following begins with the first subscriber and ends with the last, which is exactly the rule decision 3 needs from the application's side.

10. **The application** (`apps/activity-monitor`, Bloom's components, no look-alikes): a toolbar (*Quit Process*, *Inspect*, the view's segmented control, Search), Bloom's `Table` with sortable columns, its rows virtualized and unchanged ones kept so a sample redraws only what it changed; the applications' processes under their application's row (its icon, its values summed, a disclosure triangle); *All Processes* and *My Processes* in the View menu; ↑ ↓ to select, Return and ⌘I to inspect, ⌥⌘Q to quit. Below the table, each view's summary and its graph of the last three minutes sampled (a gap where the window was hidden). English and Spanish. No timer of its own: the session's samples drive every change.

11. **What the session is told on the wire, and how.** A sample (`{"activity": …}`, serial 0) holds the view, the interval, the machine's summary, the applications running (id, name, pid, icon) and each process with the columns of its view; an inspection holds the process's parent, user, start, CPU time, threads, memory, open files (up to 512), ports (TCP and UDP addresses and states, Unix sockets' paths; up to 256), arguments (up to 64) and control group. A sample of three hundred processes is about 60 KB, and `SystemText` carries 32 bytes an event: nearly two thousand events every two seconds, a syscall each on both sides, and past the 1,024 a client may leave unread before the compositor drops it as unresponsive (a host busy with its first frames did not read them in time, and was dropped). So **protocol version 33 adds `SystemData`** (event 165): an answer of the session's vocabulary as one sealed memory file (`memfd`, sealed against writing, shrinking and growing) the event carries, of `size` bytes up to 4 MiB, as Wayland sends a keymap; the client library reads it whole (`Client::system_data`, `oxyos_client_system_data`) and the Bloom host hands it to JavaScript as the text it is, which Hermes' `JSON.parse` reads (`OxyOSSystemAnswer`'s `text`): made into a `folly::dynamic` and then into JavaScript values, a sample was thousands of JSI calls. A client of version 32 or older is never sent it and is told the same answer in chunks.

## Results

- **`crates/oxyos-activity`**: `Sampler` (each view's reads, rates against the sample before, applications' process trees by parent, names), `system` (the machine's counters and memory pressure's level), `inspect` (`smaps_rollup`, descriptors, the sockets of the process's namespace, the control group), `quit` (pidfd, start, user, the session's chain).
- **`oxyos-power`**: `Proc::drm` (each DRM client's busy time and memory, once per client), the meter's hourly checkpoints and `Energy::average`.
- **The scene**: `Grants::activity`, `client_shown`, `quit_application_by_id`, `force_quit_application_by_id`, `system_data`. **`oxyos-apps`**: the `activity` grant.
- **Protocol version 33**: `SystemData` (event 165, `MAX_SYSTEM_DATA_BYTES`); `Compositor::system_data` (the sealed file); the client library's `system_data`; the C ABI's `OXYOS_EVENT_SYSTEM_DATA` and `oxyos_client_system_data`; the Bloom host's `Window::systemData`.
- **The session**: `oxyos_compositor::activity` (the vocabulary, the followers shown, the thread that samples), served by `oxyos-session` (not at the login screen) and the development compositor, whose steps say what it did (`activity`: samples taken and sent, its thread's and the application's wakeups).
- **The deploy** (`tools/deploy/deploy.sh`) installs it as a system application, its entry not in the dock, the policy giving it `activity`.
- **The application**: `apps/activity-monitor` (`ActivityMonitor.tsx`, `Summary.tsx`, `Inspector.tsx`, `model.ts`, `messages.ts`), `sample/ActivityMonitorApp.tsx` over `@oxyos/platform`'s `activity` (its session's `activity` state and `activityView`, a long answer's `text` parsed), its package (`so.oxy.activitymonitor`), development entry and icon (`scripts/make-activity-monitor-icon.py`).

## Tests

- **`oxyos-activity`** (13): over a `/proc` of the test's own, the first sample without rates and the next over its interval (a process's % CPU, wakeups, the machine's split), a pid used again being another process, memory and pressure (and its levels), the disks that are hardware and a process's storage, another user's processes, energy of processes and applications, names cut and chosen, the JSON's keys; over this machine's, a child inspected whole and restricted as another user would see it, a port listened on, quitting only the person's process that started then (SIGTERM, SIGKILL), the session's chain.
- **`oxyos-power`**: a DRM client's memory once per client; the twelve-hour average over checkpoints and since an application began.
- **The protocol and the compositor** (`tests/system_data.rs`): a 200 KB answer through real sockets arriving whole in one sealed file, never in chunks, the client never dropped; `SystemData` encoded and decoded.
- **The scene**: a client shown while a window of its can be seen (minimized, another desktop, locked: not); quitting and force-quitting an application by its id.
- **The session's side** (`activity/tests.rs`): only `activity` asks, strictly, quitting citing an input; a shown follower sampled at once and every two seconds, then minimized and **its thread not woken once in five seconds**, nor after following stops; the displays off sampling nothing; a child inspected and quit, the session never.
- **The application** (`apps/activity-monitor/tests`): grouping, sorting within and across groups, search, who may be quit, formatting in English and Spanish, the graphs' gaps.
- **Scenarios** (`activity-monitor`, `activity-monitor-scaled` at 1.25): two processes the run starts and knows (`processes`, `activity-process.py`: one busy a fifth of the time, one asleep, each recording the signals it receives) found by Search; sorted by name both ways and by % CPU; the views; *Inspect*; *Quit* confirmed delivering exactly one SIGTERM to the one chosen and none to the other; *Force Quit*; the keyboard's focus; minimized, no sample taken or sent and the sampling thread not woken.

## Not in this decision

- Quitting and inspecting another user's processes (decision 7), and per-process network use (decision 8): both need a privileged helper that asks an administrator through the session's authentication agent.
- **The Dock icon's CPU history.** A Mac's Activity Monitor can draw its Dock icon. OxyOS's dock draws each application's registry icon; an application has no way to draw its own tile (a Mac's `NSDockTile`). That is a capability of its own (the protocol, the shell's dock and Bloom's adapter), not a part of this decision.
- A Mac's *Sample Process* (a stack sample needs `ptrace`), *Spindump*, *System Diagnostics*, the update frequency (fixed at two seconds), the GPU History window, and *Preventing Sleep* (logind's inhibitors by process).
