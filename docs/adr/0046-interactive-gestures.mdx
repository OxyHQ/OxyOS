# ADR 0046: Swipes that follow the fingers

Status: **accepted; implemented**. Builds on [ADR 0011](./0011-transitions) (the session's springs), [ADR 0022](./0022-window-views) (window views), [ADR 0033](./0033-protocol-versions) (protocol version 25) and [ADR 0045](./0045-app-expose) (App Exposé, Mission Control's shortcut and swipes, windows moved into their views), whose "Not in this decision" it settles: following the fingers, Mission Control's windows moved, and three fingers across.

**8 November 2026: More Gestures, the person's choice** (the owner's question: whether choosing the gestures in Settings was done; it was not, they were fixed). Settings › Trackpad has a Mac's More Gestures, and the swipes and a new pinch follow it; see *Amendment: More Gestures*, below. Decisions 1 and 4 hold with the fingers the person chose.

## Problem

A Mac's Mission Control and App Exposé track the fingers: swiped up, the windows head for their places in Mission Control as far as the fingers have gone, and come back as the fingers do; when they lift, the motion completes or goes back by how far it got and how fast it was going, the spring taking the rest at the fingers' speed. The swipe back closes it the same way. Across, the desktops (spaces) slide under the fingers, and past the first or the last they pull against the end. OxyOS acted once the fingers had gone 40 pixels, and the motion was a spring from rest whatever the fingers did; Mission Control's windows were copies drawn over the windows, which stayed where they were; three fingers across did nothing although OxyOS has desktops (ADR 0009).

## Options

**Who follows the fingers.** (a) *The shell*, told each motion, animating its overlay and telling the compositor where each window's view is each frame. Each frame of the motion would be a frame of the shell drawn on the host's CPU after the input (ADR 0004), so the windows would lag the fingers by that frame at best, and the windows' own places still the compositor's. (b) *The compositor*, which already flies the windows into their views and back on the overlay's spring (ADR 0045) and animates every shell surface it shows: the fingers' progress replaces the spring's time, and each frame it composes after their motion draws the windows exactly there. Nothing of the shell is drawn again while the fingers move. (b), as a Mac's window server drives Mission Control from the trackpad's gesture and the Dock draws only its chrome.

**What the shell is told, and when.** The shell decides what Mission Control and App Exposé show; the compositor must know when it shows them, which of its surfaces they are, and must not have them close before the fingers decide. A key's shortcut toggles (Super+↑ again closes Mission Control, as Control-↑ does); a swipe cannot toggle: the shell might not have opened what the fingers began (App Exposé of an application with no window), and a second toggle would then open it. So the swipe's shortcut says what it asks: Open as the fingers begin to drive it, Close if they let it go back (or close it). And the shell says which of its surfaces are Mission Control's and App Exposé's, backdrop and overlay alike, before their first frame (`SetGestureOverlay`), so the compositor knows what to drive and what is open, and a swipe back over what is open closes it without the shell being told anything until the fingers let it go past closing.

**The distances, the speed, the decision.** GNOME Shell's swipe tracker, measured on libinput's same unaccelerated deltas and refined over years: a full swipe is 300 logical pixels of the fingers vertically and 400 across (about a third of a touchpad), a release faster than 0.3 pixels a millisecond is a flick that goes where it was going, slower goes to the nearer end. The speed is the fingers' over their last 100 ms by their device's time (the way they went before the axis was chosen counted from when they began, never as taking no time), nothing if they rested 40 ms before lifting (Android's VelocityTracker horizon and stop, which the Bloom host's pans use, ADR 0042).

**The spring after the release.** A spring let go at rest (ADR 0011's `Spring::at`) would stop the motion dead at the release and start it again; a Mac's and UIKit's (`UISpringTimingParameters`' initial velocity) take the motion on at the fingers' speed. The same equation of motion with its displacement and speed at the release (`motion::Released`), on the overlay's own responses (`OVERLAY_ARRIVES` opening, `OVERLAY_GOES` closing) and the desktops' (`WORKSPACE`).

**Mission Control's windows.** ADR 0045 moved App Exposé's windows into its views and left Mission Control's copies; the same request on its overlay (`movesWindows`) gives it the same flights, so the two share one implementation and a swipe drives both alike.

## Decision

1. **The scene follows the fingers and decides** (`scene/swipes.rs`). Three or four fingers (libinput's swipe gesture, the system's: no application is told) choose up, down or across once they have gone `AXIS_LOCK` (8 logical pixels), counted from where they began so nothing jumps. A `Tracker` keeps their way along the swipe (progress is the way over `OVERLAY_DISTANCE`, 300, or `SPACE_DISTANCE`, 400) and their speed. Up opens Mission Control and down App Exposé, the shell of the display under the pointer told at once (`SystemShortcut` with `ShortcutIntent::Open`); over what is open (a surface of the shell's said to be Mission Control's or App Exposé's is shown), the swipe back closes it as far as the fingers go, and the same way again does nothing, as a Mac's. Caught while a swipe's spring still takes them (`swipe_from`), the fingers drive it from where it is drawn, either way. When they lift, `release_target`: a flick (`FLICK`, 300 pixels a second) goes on the way it was going however short, anything slower to the nearer end. The shell is told `Close` when the fingers let an opening go back or let a closing go through; a cancelled swipe (libinput's: a finger more) goes back. The compositor reads what the swipe drives each frame (`swipe_drive`: its openness or the desktops' position, and once released where it goes and how fast).

2. **The compositor draws it where the fingers have it** (`swipes.rs`, `transitions.rs`). While the fingers are down, Mission Control's or App Exposé's surfaces are drawn exactly as open as the scene says, in the frame composed after their motion: their arrival's motion that far along (the overlay from 104 %, the backdrop's fade), arriving, going, or (shown when the swipe back began) joined, and each window moved into a view flies exactly that share of the way from where it is to its view (ADR 0045's `flights`). A frame is composed when the fingers move, or a spring does, never while they rest. When they lift, `motion::Released` takes it from where it is drawn to where the scene says, starting at the fingers' speed. The shell's first frame of what the fingers opened comes a few frames after they began, and what it opens may be several surfaces drawn one after the other (App Exposé's backdrop, then the overlay its windows are moved into; on a loaded machine tens of milliseconds apart): nothing of it moves until every one of the shell's surfaces said to be it has its first frame drawn (`Scene::gesture_overlay_drawn`), and from that frame the windows set off from where they are and catch up with the fingers on a short spring (`CATCH_UP`), never a jump; fingers that lifted before it was drawn start their spring from there. However late the shell is, no window starts its flight from part of the way. Let go closed, its surfaces are held closed until the shell takes them away (at most `CLOSED_HOLD`, a second). A new swipe of either ends what the last one was still taking away.

3. **Mission Control's windows are the windows** (`Shell.tsx`: the overview's `Surface` has `movesWindows`): the current desktop's windows fly into their cards and back as App Exposé's do; those of other desktops, not drawn, fade in with the overview (ADR 0045, decision 2). Choosing a window, closing it with nothing chosen, or the swipe back: every window is where it was.

4. **Three or four fingers across slide the desktops.** The desktops' position (in desktops: the active one's number at rest) follows the fingers, one desktop at most from where they began: left brings the next desktop in from the right, as a Mac's spaces. The active desktop's windows are drawn moved by the output's width times how far they are from it, and the windows of the desktop coming in are drawn beside (`Scene::show_beside`: the frame's list has them; they take no input and have no focus). Past the first or the last desktop the windows pull against the end, approaching a seventh of the width as UIKit's rubber band does. When the fingers lift, a desktop brought in past halfway, or flicked, is the active one at once (its windows focus, the other's lose what they held), without a slide of its own: the compositor's spring takes the windows on from where the fingers left them. Over Mission Control or App Exposé, across does nothing (their own scrolls and arrows move between desktops). The shell's panels (top bar, dock, wallpaper) do not move, as with Super's switch (ADR 0011).

5. **Reduce Motion** (ADR 0019): nothing follows the fingers. Lifted, a swipe that would have gone through opens, closes or changes desktop at once, and the compositor crossfades it as it does every motion then; the shell is told `Open` only then.

6. **Protocol version 25** ([ADR 0033](./0033-protocol-versions)): `intent` at the end of `SystemShortcut` (`ShortcutIntent`: a key's `Toggle`, a swipe's `Open` and `Close`), the toggle what every shortcut of an older version was, Open and Close never sent to a shell of version 24 or older (a swipe then opens nothing there); request 95 `SetGestureOverlay { surface, overlay }` (`GestureOverlay`: `None`, `MissionControl`, `AppExpose`), the shell's on its own shell surfaces as `MoveWindowsToViews` is. In the C ABI: the intent in the event's `flag` (`OXYOS_SHORTCUT_INTENT_*`), `oxyos_client_set_gesture_overlay` and `OXYOS_GESTURE_OVERLAY_*`. The Bloom host gives JavaScript the intent (`OxyOSShellShortcut`'s `intent`) and sends a `Surface`'s `gesture` prop ("mission-control", "app-expose").

## Amendment: More Gestures (8 November 2026)

### Problem

The swipes were fixed: three or four fingers up opened Mission Control, down App Exposé, across the desktops, and nothing could change it. A Mac's System Settings › Trackpad › More Gestures lets the person choose each (*Swipe between pages*, *Swipe between full-screen applications*, *Notification Center*, *Mission Control*, *App Exposé*, *Launchpad*, *Show Desktop*), each with a film of the hand. OxyOS had no pinch of the system's at all: a pinch of any fingers went to the application under the pointer.

### What a Mac offers, and what OxyOS takes of it

| A Mac's | Its choices | OxyOS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Swipe between pages | Scroll left or right with two fingers, swipe with three, either, off | **Not offered.** No application on OxyOS honours a page back and forward from the system: the Bloom host plays Android, whose back is `hardwareBackPress`, which the host does not send (`DeviceEventManager.invokeDefaultBackPressHandler` is unsupported), and a mouse's back button reaches an application as a W3C button 3, which none reads as back. A gesture that went back in no application would be a promise kept nowhere. It comes with a platform back and forward that applications honour; with it, three fingers taken by pages leave Mission Control's and the desktops' swipes four, as a Mac's do. |
| Swipe between full-screen applications | Three fingers, four, off | **Swipe between desktops**, three, four or off: OxyOS's full-screen windows are on their desktop (*Not in this decision*), so the swipe goes between desktops, and is named so. |
| Notification Center | Swipe left from the right edge with two fingers, on or off | **Not offered.** OxyOS has a Notification Center (ADR 0026), but which edge of the touchpad two fingers start from is not something libinput tells: it reports a two-finger scroll's motion, never where the fingers are. Reading the touchpad's own touches beside libinput would be a second touchpad driver, against using the proven engine (ADR 0042, decision 6). Super+N and the clock open it. |
| Mission Control | Swipe up with three fingers, four, off | The same. |
| App Exposé | Swipe down with three fingers, four, off | The same. A Mac ships it off; OxyOS keeps it on, as it has had it since ADR 0045. |
| Launchpad | Pinch with thumb and three fingers, on or off | The same, new: below. |
| Show Desktop | Spread with thumb and three fingers, on or off | **Not offered.** OxyOS has no Show Desktop (every window moved aside to the screen's edges and back); a spread over nothing does nothing. It comes with that. |

### Decision

7. **The preferences** (`oxyos-preferences`, [ADR 0019](./0019-settings)): `mission_control_swipe`, `app_expose_swipe` and `desktops_swipe`, each `three`, `four` or `off`, and `launchpad_pinch`, `true` or `false`; all `three` and `true` by default. Mission Control and App Exposé share their fingers, as a Mac's do (its `TrackpadThreeFingerVertSwipeGesture` and `TrackpadFourFingerVertSwipeGesture` are the vertical swipes', both): kept as one count for the swipes up and down and whether each is on (`TrackpadGestures`), so three or four chosen for one is the other's too, unless it is off, and a file can never say otherwise. The desktops' are their own. Settings sends the one it changes; the session, the file's one writer, tells it both.

8. **Three takes four, four leaves three** (`scene/swipes.rs`, `TrackpadGestures`: the fewest fingers of each, or none). A Mac set to three fingers swipes with four as well (its four-finger keys stay on); set to four, three do nothing there. So with three chosen, three or four fingers make the swipe, as OxyOS has always taken them, and with four only four do. The scene decides when the fingers choose their axis: up, down or across, the swipe acts only if the choice for what it would open, close or slide takes those fingers; otherwise it does nothing at all, the shell told nothing and nothing drawn, and no application hears it either way. The swipe back closes Mission Control or App Exposé with the fingers of its own swipe: Mission Control on four, three fingers down over it do nothing. Launchpad shown is no swipe's: up over it opens Mission Control, across slides the desktops, as before. The compositor is given the choices as every preference is (`Settings::apply`, `Scene::set_trackpad_gestures`), from the next swipe on, with no restart.

9. **Launchpad pinched, and spread closed** (`Scene::launchpad_pinch`). Three or more fingers pinching (libinput's pinch gesture, which tells a pinch of three or more from their swipe by itself, and counts them: the thumb and three fingers are four, or three when it takes the thumb for a resting one) are the system's while the choice is on, the session unlocked and no screenshot interface open; otherwise, and always for two, the pinch goes to the application under the pointer as it did. Once the fingers have pinched `PINCH_LOCK` in or out (5 %), in opens Launchpad on the display under the pointer and out over it closes it, followed as a swipe is: its progress is the logarithm of how much closer the fingers came over `PINCH_DISTANCE` (0.5: about 60 % of their spread opens it, 165 % closes it, in and out the same way along), its release a swipe's (`release_target`, `PINCH_FLICK` the same share of the way a second as a swipe's flick), and the compositor draws Launchpad's two surfaces, its backdrop and its icons, as open as the fingers have them, with the same motion as they arrive with (ADR 0011: the backdrop's fade, the icons from 104 %). The shell is told `Search`, the launcher Super alone opens, `Open` as the fingers begin to drive it and `Close` if they let it go back or spread it shut: it opens it with its search taking the keyboard, as a Mac's Launchpad takes what is typed. Spread over nothing (a Mac's Show Desktop) or pinched in over Launchpad, nothing. With Reduce Motion, as decision 5.

10. **Protocol version 33** ([ADR 0033](./0033-protocol-versions)): `GestureOverlay::Launchpad` (3), the shell's Launchpad surfaces said to be what the pinch drives (`gesture="launchpad"` on both); `OXYOS_GESTURE_OVERLAY_LAUNCHPAD` in the C ABI. The client library refuses it to a compositor of version 32 or older, and the Bloom host does not send it there.

11. **Settings › Trackpad › More Gestures** (`apps/settings`): below Natural scrolling, which stays where it was, a section titled More Gestures with a Mac's rows in its order: *Swipe between desktops* ("Swipe left or right with three fingers", "… four fingers", "Off"), *Mission Control* ("Swipe up with three fingers", "… four fingers", "Off"), *App Exposé* ("Swipe down with three fingers", "… four fingers", "Off"), each a menu named by its row, and *Launchpad*, a switch with a Mac's line under it, "Pinch with thumb and three fingers" (in Spanish *Más gestos*, *Pasar de un escritorio a otro*, "Deslizar hacia arriba con tres dedos", *Desactivado*, "Pellizcar con el pulgar y tres dedos"). A Mac plays a film of the hand beside each choice; Bloom plays no film, so each row's icon is its gesture drawn (`GestureIllustration`, `gestures.ts`): a small trackpad, the fingers on it (three or four, as chosen; Launchpad's thumb below three), the way they go drawn still under them, and the fingers moving that way, twice over and still again, when the row's choice changes and when the pointer comes over the row. Off, it is dimmed and still; with Reduce Motion it never moves; it is decoration, the screen reader reading the row by its title and its control. Settings' search finds the page by its gestures.

### Tests

- **Rust:** the preferences' defaults, values and refusals, Mission Control's and App Exposé's fingers going together but for off, the file read back (`oxyos-preferences`); the scene's swipes as each choice says, three and four, up, down, across and off, the swipe back by its own fingers, no application told (`scene/tests/expose.rs`, `the_persons_choice_of_fingers_decides_what_each_swipe_does`, `the_swipe_back_is_the_one_that_opens_it_with_its_fingers`); the pinch opening Launchpad as far as the fingers go, a cancel back, spread over it closing it, three fingers counting, nothing spread over nothing, a swipe up over it still Mission Control (`the_thumb_and_three_fingers_pinched_open_launchpad_and_spread_close_it`), and a pinch the application's with two fingers, with the choice off and while locked (`a_pinch_is_the_applications_unless_it_is_launchpads`); the compositor given the choices and a change at once (`settings.rs`); the protocol's value and its version, and the C ABI's.
- **libinput**, as root in CI: the development compositor's virtual touchpad, now of four fingers, whose swipes libinput reads as three and four fingers going most of their way and whose thumb and three fingers pinched as a pinch of four (`uinput.rs`, `Devices::swipe`, `Devices::pinch`, `libinput_counts_the_virtual_touchpads_swipes_and_pinches_of_three_and_four_fingers`).
- **Scenarios**, through real libinput (a scenario's `uinput`, the gate's run on the kernel's virtual devices): a `swipe` step is the fingers on the virtual touchpad and a `pinch` step's fifth number its fingers, as libinput recognizes and counts them (without the devices the same steps reach the compositor's input as libinput's gestures do). `trackpad-gestures` (at 1) and `trackpad-gestures-scaled` (in Spanish, at 1.25), Settings and the shell: each choice changes what the same fingers do. As shipped, three fingers up open Mission Control (Settings' window moved into it) and down close it; Mission Control on four (App Exposé following it, kept in the file), three up do nothing, four open and close it; off, four do nothing. A second desktop, three fingers across going to it and back; on four, three across do nothing and four slide; off, nothing. The thumb and three fingers pinched open Launchpad (the keyboard to its search) and spread close it; the switch off, the same pinch is Settings' own, delivered to it, and Launchpad stays closed. Each step checks what the compositor delivered to Settings exactly (its focus going and coming back; never a swipe), the views the shell shows, the desktops, the shell's surfaces and the preferences file.

## Not in this decision

- **Spaces of full-screen windows.** A Mac swipes between full-screen applications as between desktops; OxyOS's full-screen windows are on their desktop.
- **Mission Control's Spaces bar** at the top with each desktop's thumbnail; the overview's desktop cards stay ADR 0022's.
- **Swipe between pages**, **Notification Center's swipe from the edge** and **Show Desktop's spread**: see the amendment's table.
