# ADR 0040: Language and region

Status: **accepted; implemented** (chosen with the platform's author). Builds on [ADR 0014](./0014-screen-reader) (the reader's speech), [ADR 0019](./0019-settings) (preferences, Settings; this decision amends its keys), [ADR 0020](./0020-oxy-account-login) (the login screen and the session's environment), [ADR 0023](./0023-installing-oxyos) (setup chooses the system's language) and [ADR 0033](./0033-protocol-versions) (version 15).

## Problem

The desktop speaks Spanish whatever the person speaks:
- the shell's text is written in Spanish in its source: the top bar and its menus, Control Center, the dock's menu, Launchpad, the overview, notifications, the lock screen and the system sheets;
- the clock is formatted with a fixed `"es"` (`lun, 2 nov`), and its hours are always 24;
- the login screen is Spanish too.

Other parts follow the environment's `LANG` instead, read once: Settings (Spanish or English), the screen reader's speech, the host's `Intl`. Nothing can change while the session runs. `clock_24h` is kept and shown in Settings, but nothing follows it. React Native's `I18nManager` says `en-US` to every application.

A Mac has one answer, at the root: System Settings › General › Language & Region. The person chooses a language, a region and a time format; the system's own interface follows them, every application is told them through the platform's APIs (`NSLocale`, `Locale.current`), and VoiceOver speaks the language with its voice.

## Decision

1. **The person's choice is a preference, the system's is the default.** Three keys join the user's preferences ([ADR 0019](./0019-settings), decision 1), kept by `crates/oxyos-preferences` and changed only through the session:
   - `language`: one of the languages OxyOS speaks, `en` or `es`; empty (the default) is the system's.
   - `region`: a region as CLDR names one, two ASCII capitals (`ES`, `US`, `MX`, `GB`); empty is the system's.
   - `hour_cycle`: `auto` (the region's), `h12` or `h23`.

   `clock_24h` is retired: nothing followed it, and a boolean cannot say "the region's". A file that still has it is read as any unknown key is, and the next write leaves it out.

   **The system's** is the session's environment, which the login program gives it from the system's locale (`/etc/locale.conf`, written by systemd-localed; setup chooses it, [ADR 0023](./0023-installing-oxyos)): `LC_ALL`, else `LC_MESSAGES`, else `LANG`, POSIX's order. Its language when OxyOS speaks it, else English; its territory, else the language's likely region (`en` → `US`, `es` → `ES`).

   Why not the alternatives:
   - **`LANG` alone** is read when a process starts. Changing it means a new session, and glibc only formats in locales generated on the machine.
   - **The Oxy account's languages** are the account's (`oxy` ADR 0022): what Oxy's applications speak on the web and on phones. They need the account's API to be read and changed, the login screen has no account yet, and a Mac keeps the system's language apart from the Apple ID's. *Amended 9 November 2026:* on OxyOS an Oxy application speaks the system's language, as a Mac's applications speak the system's preferred languages, signed in or not: the platform's session host names it in every snapshot (`preferredLanguages`, `oxy` ADR 0058 R11, decision 4), and the account's languages neither decide nor delay it. Following the account had made Dropsea's window blank until the token and the account's profile came over the network (6 to 15 s on the ThinkPad), then speak the fallback when the account named no language.

2. **The session's locale is one BCP 47 tag.** It is the language, the region and, only when the person chose one, the hour cycle as a Unicode extension: `es-ES`, `en-US`, `en-ES` (English, formatted for Spain), `es-MX-u-hc-h12`. `oxyos_preferences::session_locale` computes it from the preferences and the environment. The login screen has no person's preferences: its tag is the system's alone.

3. **Every client is told it.** `Locale` (event 181, protocol version 17, [ADR 0033](./0033-protocol-versions)) carries the tag in chunks, as `KeyboardLayout` does. It is sent to every client when it connects and to every client whenever it changes, the screen reader among them. A client of version 16 or older is not told it and goes on with its environment's, as before. The C ABI gives it as `OXYOS_EVENT_LOCALE`.

4. **Applications get it through the APIs they already use.** The Bloom host gives it to:
   - **`Intl`'s default locale.** `Intl.DateTimeFormat().resolvedOptions().locale`, every `toLocale*String()` without a locale, and Bloom's own default (`runtimeLocale`) are the session's, with its hour cycle.
   - **React Native's `I18nManager`:** `localeIdentifier` as Android gives it (`es_ES`), left to right for both languages.
   - **expo-localization:** `getLocales()` (the tag, language, region, separators, measurement system, temperature unit) and `getCalendars()` (Gregorian, `uses24hourClock`, the region's first weekday, the time zone). On a change, the host sends `onLocaleSettingsChanged` and `onCalendarSettingsChanged`, as Android does, so `useLocales()` and `useCalendars()` render again.
   - **The Oxy SDK** (applications the session launched): the session host's snapshot (`sample/oxyos/session-host.js`, [ADR 0020](./0020-oxy-account-login), decision 6) names the tag, its Unicode extensions left out, as `preferredLanguages` (`oxy` ADR 0058 R11), and a new snapshot of the same revision when it changes. The SDK speaks it from the application's first render and never waits for the account.

   The host also provides `Intl.PluralRules` (ICU4X's CLDR plural rules), so plurals go through `Intl` as numbers and dates do.

   An application that reads the language once speaks the new language the next time it opens, as on a Mac. That is what Dropsea and Atlas do through `Intl` and the Oxy SDK, and Inbox through Bloom.

5. **The system's own applications follow it live.** The shell (top bar, menus, Control Center, dock, Launchpad, overview, notifications, lock screen, screenshots, system sheets), the login screen and Settings change at once.
   - **One catalogue per application.** Each has one message catalogue (`messages.ts`: the shell's, the login screen's, Settings'). Its English is the shape, and Spanish is typed to it, so a missing translation fails the type-check. A count is a function of `Intl.PluralRules`; a number, a size or a percentage goes through `Intl.NumberFormat`. Every catalogue follows one glossary, [Wording](../wording) (10 November 2026): the same term for the same thing in each language, checked by `apps/shell/tests/wording.test.ts`.
   - **Bloom's mechanism.** Each application's root gives the session's locale to `BloomProvider` (`locale`). Its catalogue is chosen from `useBloomLocale()` with Bloom's `resolveBloomLanguage`, so Bloom's own components (window controls, menus, dialogs) switch in the same render.
   - **Dates and times** are `Intl.DateTimeFormat` in that locale: the top bar's clock, the lock screen's date and time, notifications' times. Twelve or 24 hours is the locale's hour cycle: the region's, or the person's choice.

6. **The screen reader speaks it.** `oxyos-reader` takes `Locale` as any client does. From the next thing it says, its words (roles, states, positions, its own announcements) and its eSpeak NG voice (`es`, `en`) are the language's. Until told, it speaks its environment's.

7. **Settings › General › Language & Region.** Settings' System group becomes **General**, as on a Mac. Its new page **Language & Region** has:
   - **Language:** Automatic (the system's, named), English, Español; each language is named in itself, as macOS lists them.
   - **Region:** the system's, or one of a list of regions, named in the session's language.
   - **Time format:** Automatic (the region's), 12-hour or 24-hour.
   - **A sample:** a date, a time and a number as the choice formats them.

   Date & Time's 24-hour switch shows the hour cycle in effect and sets `h23` or `h12`. A change is a preference like any other (`SetPreference`, the `settings` grant). The session validates it, keeps it, tells every client the new locale and its reader the new voice, and Settings itself switches language in the same moment.

8. **Scenarios run in a fixed language.** `regress.py` runs every scenario with `LANG` and `LC_ALL` set to `en_US.UTF-8`, never the machine's. A scenario whose expectations are in a language says so (`locale`), and its reader speaks that language. The ones written against Spanish text are pinned to `es_ES.UTF-8`. `language-es` and `language-en` check the shell's text, the clock's format, the reader's speech and voice, and a change made in Settings applied live.

## Not in this decision

- **More languages.** A language joins when the shell's, the login screen's and Settings' catalogues and the reader's speech have it complete. Bloom ships fifteen.
- **Right-to-left** layout, which arrives with the first right-to-left language.
- **A list of preferred languages** (macOS orders several), and per-application languages.
- **Changing the system's language** (localed's, the login screen's) from Settings, which needs the administrator's authentication dialog ([ADR 0019](./0019-settings), decision 2). Setup chooses it.

## Steps

1. The preferences, the locale, the protocol, the session, the development compositor and the reader. `Intl.PluralRules` and a settable default locale in `crates/oxyos-intl`.

   *Done.* `crates/oxyos-preferences` keeps `language`, `region` and `hour_cycle` (`LANGUAGES` lists `en` and `es`; `clock_24h` is gone, an old line dropped on the next write) and `session_locale` makes the tag (tested: `es-ES`, `en-ES`, `es-MX-u-hc-h12`, `fr_FR` as `en-FR`, `C` as `en-US`). `Locale` is event 181 of protocol version 17, never sent to a version 16 connection; the scene tells it on connecting and on a change (`Scene::set_locale`), and the C ABI gives it as `OXYOS_EVENT_LOCALE`, in chunks as `OXYOS_EVENT_KEYBOARD_LAYOUT`. The session's settings (`crates/oxyos-compositor`'s `Settings`) compute it at start and after a change; `oxyos-session --greeter` and `oxyos-offscreen --login` use the environment's alone. `crates/oxyos-intl` has `set_default_locale` (the whole process's), `system_locale` and `plural` (`select` of the number as written, `resolve`), in the C ABI as `oxyos_intl_set_default_locale`, `oxyos_intl_system_locale`, `oxyos_intl_plural_select` and `oxyos_intl_resolve_plural` (`pluralCategories` joined by `,`). The reader assembles `Locale`, speaks its language from the next utterance with that language's voice (`Backend::set_language`; a record writes each utterance's `voice`), and says it is on once the events that come with Welcome are in; `regress.py`'s `spoken` takes a `voice`.
2. The host: `Intl`, `I18nManager`, expo-localization, live.

   *Done.* The window keeps the tag `Locale` tells (`window.h`) and makes it `Intl`'s default (`oxyos_intl_set_default_locale`) before the bundle runs and at every change; the host tells JavaScript (`OxyOSLocaleChanged`) and reports it (`locale`). `intl.js` reads the default at each call, never keeping it, and has `Intl.PluralRules`. `I18nManager.localeIdentifier` is the tag as Android gives it (`es_ES`); expo-localization's module (`sample/oxyos/expo-adapter.js`, over the host's `OxyOSLocale`) answers `getLocales()` and `getCalendars()` and sends both of its events on a change; `sample/oxyos/locale.js`'s `useSessionLocale()` is what the system's applications give `BloomProvider`. Settings' system facts have the system's locale (`locale`, `oxyos_intl_system_locale`), what Language & Region names as Automatic. `scenarios/locale.json` checks it all live, `scenarios/intl.json` the plural rules against ICU's ([react-native-android-adaptation](../react-native-android-adaptation)).
3. The shell's, the login screen's and Settings' catalogues, and the Language & Region page.

   - *Done* (Settings): `apps/settings/src/messages.ts` is Settings' one catalogue (English the shape, Spanish typed to it, counts through `Intl.PluralRules`), chosen from `useBloomLocale()` with `resolveBloomLanguage`; `sample/SettingsApp.tsx` gives `BloomProvider` the session's locale (`useSessionLocale`), the fixture's from its own preferences. The General group has Language & Region (`apps/settings/src/locale.ts`: the session's tag as `session_locale` computes it, the choices and the sample, tested in `apps/settings/tests/locale.test.ts`), and Date & Time's switch is the hour cycle in effect.
   - *Done* (shell and login): `apps/shell/src/messages.ts` and `apps/login/src/messages.ts`, read with `useShellMessages()` and `useLoginMessages()`; the clocks through `apps/shell/src/clock.ts`, notifications' times and every count and number through the catalogue's `Intl` formats; ShellProbe's and LoginSession's `BloomProvider` given `useSessionLocale()`; the shell preview's `?lang=`.
4. The scenarios, pinned and new, in the deploy gate.

   *Done.* `regress.py` runs every scenario in `en_US.UTF-8` or its own `locale`, never the machine's, and its reader speaks that locale (a scenario's `reader` names none); `texts_matching` checks what follows the wall clock. The scenarios that run the shell or the login screen and those whose reader named a locale state it. The ones that mixed two languages (Spanish shell text beside English Settings, or the reader's Spanish words around Settings' English names, which a single language makes impossible) are pinned to one and their other strings written in it. `language-es` and `language-en`, at 1 and 1.25, start the desktop in their language, check Settings' pages, the shell's menus, the top bar's clock as the region writes it and the reader's words and voice, then choose the other language and a time format in Language & Region and check that all of them changed at once. `locale` checks what applications are told.

   Screenshots are named in the session's language (`Settings` gives the writer the locale at every change), and the shell lists the applications by their entries' names in it (`oxyos_applications` reads `Intl`'s default locale, which the host keeps the session's, and the host lists them again on a change), so the dock's names change with the rest.
