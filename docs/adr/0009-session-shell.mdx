# ADR 0009: The session shell

Status: **accepted** (decisions 1 and 2, chosen with the shell's author); steps 1 to 3 implemented; menus, thumbnails and launching are left to their own decisions (decision 3). Builds on [ADR 0001](./0001-native-platform-boundaries) (roles, grants, isolation), [ADR 0002](./0002-bloom-platform-surface-api) (Bloom's platform adapter) and the shell surfaces of the protocol (`CreateShellSurface`).

## Problem

The OxyOS shell (`apps/shell`) is one React tree: the wallpaper, the top bar, the dock, and the overview and launcher that open over everything. It renders from a `ShellSnapshot` (applications, folders, windows, workspaces, the active workspace and application, the active application's menus) and sends `ShellActions` (activate an application, focus or close a window, switch, add and move to workspaces, a menu command). Its browser preview fakes both.

On OxyOS it runs in the Bloom host, which so far makes one toplevel window (`sample/ShellProbe.tsx`). A session needs more:

- **Layers.** The wallpaper must be below every window, the top bar and dock above them with the space they take kept out of where windows are placed, and the overview and launcher above everything. One window cannot be all of these. The protocol already has the roles: background, panel (with an edge and an exclusive zone) and overlay shell surfaces, granted by the session.
- **Windows and workspaces.** Nothing tells the shell which windows exist, which application each belongs to, its title or its workspace. The compositor has no workspaces.

## Decision

1. **One tree, wrapped regions.** The shell stays one React tree. Its root is the background surface: the Bloom host, started as the shell, makes a background shell surface where an application makes its window, and lays the tree out at its size, the output's. So the wallpaper, drawn in the root, needs no wrapper. Each other region that is a surface of its own is wrapped in a `ShellSurface` element (`kind`: `panel` or `overlay`; for a panel, its `edge` and whether it keeps its `exclusive` zone). On OxyOS it renders a host component, `OxyOSShellSurface`, whose subtree the Bloom host draws in a shell surface of its own, as it draws Bloom's popups (`OxyOSPopupView`), and leaves out of the root's drawing. Everywhere else (the browser preview) it renders its children in place. The regions:
   - the top bar: a panel at the top edge;
   - the dock: a panel at the bottom edge;
   - the overview and the launcher: an overlay, which exists only while one is open.

   Each region stays where the shell's layout puts it. A panel is the strip of the output from its edge to the far side of its region, which is drawn at its own place in it; that strip is kept out of the work area. An overlay covers the output, with the region at its own place. Each surface takes input only over its region (`SetInputRegion`), so the rest of a strip or of an overlay is transparent and lets presses through. Presses, focus, popups (the top bar's menus) and accessibility work per surface as in any window. The edits in `apps/shell` are the three wrappers and the component itself; the shell's own layout does not change.

   The shell is a Bloom host started by the session with the `shell` grant, as the session's own program, never by an application.

2. **The compositor owns workspaces and windows.** Each toplevel window belongs to a workspace; only the active workspace's windows are shown, take input and can have focus. A new window opens on the active workspace. Workspaces are numbered, at least one, at most a session limit; removing one moves its windows to its neighbour.

   A privileged protocol, for clients with the `shell` grant, tells the shell what the compositor holds and takes its actions:
   - events: a window, identified by a number the compositor gives it (never a client's surface id), with its application id, title, workspace and state (minimized, focused), when it appears or changes, and its removal; the workspaces and the active one;
   - requests: focus (and restore) a window, close it (the application is asked, `CloseRequested`, and may refuse), minimize it, move it to a workspace, switch to a workspace, add one, remove one.

   The shell shows what the compositor confirmed: a move or a switch is an intent until the next event says it happened, as the shell's model already says (`moveWindowToWorkspace` is "intent only").

   Applications say their title (`SetTitle`) and application id (`SetAppId`) on their toplevels. The application id is the application's own claim until the session's launch records (#35) give each launched process its id; the shell shows it, and nothing is authorized by it.

3. **Not decided here.** Each needs its own decision when its turn comes:
   - **The active application's menus** in the top bar: applications publishing their menus, commands sent back.
   - **Window thumbnails** in the overview: the compositor sharing scaled copies of windows' frames with the shell.
   - **Launching applications** from the dock and the launcher, and the application registry: the session's launch records (#35).

   Until then the shell gets no menus, no thumbnails (it shows each window's icon and title), and `activateApp` focuses the application's newest window if it has one, and otherwise does nothing.

## Order of work

1. **Shell surfaces in the Bloom host.** Shell surfaces and input regions in the C ABI; a host started as the shell (the root a background surface at the output's size); `OxyOSShellSurface`; and `ShellSurface` with its three wrappers in `apps/shell`. The development compositor launches the shell with the `shell` grant and an application window; a scenario checks each region is its own surface in its layer, the work area excludes the panels, and an application's window is placed between them.

   As implemented: the shell's `platform` prop (`apps/shell/src/platform.tsx`) takes a `Surface` component, which renders its children in place by default (the browser preview unchanged); the OxyOS shell entry passes one rendering `OxyOSShellSurface` (`sample/oxyos/shell-surface.js`), as Bloom takes `platform={oxyosSurfaces}`, so `apps/shell` depends on nothing of OxyOS. The Bloom host runs as the shell with `--shell OUTPUT`, the output the session names, since clients cannot list outputs yet. Bloom's popups (the top bar's menus) are children of the surface their anchor is drawn in, so they show above their panel and the windows; parented to the background, they would never show, which the scenario catches. The development compositor grants `shell` to same-user clients of a second socket, a stand-in for launch records, and numbers them from the top so the application keeps its number; `oxyos-offscreen --launch --shell HOST,BUNDLE,MODULE` starts the shell there before the application, and `--press-output` presses anywhere on the output. `scenarios/session-shell.json` opens the launcher from the dock (an overlay over everything) and closes it, opens and closes the top bar's menu, and presses into the window, checking the shell's surfaces, the work area, the window's place, pixels and the application's events; with panels that keep nothing out of the work area, or menus parented to the background, it fails. The overview needs `react-native-gesture-handler`, which the host does not have yet.
2. **Workspaces in the scene.** Windows on workspaces, switching, moving, visibility, focus and input, with scene tests.

   As implemented (`scene/workspaces.rs`): a window's workspace is the one active when it was made; visibility, and with it drawing, hit testing and focus, shows only the active workspace's windows and what is under them. Switching, moving a window, adding and removing (at most 16, at least one) each leave the windows that stop being shown as a minimized one is: popups dismissed, pointer capture released, focus to the topmost window shown. All are refused while the session is locked.
3. **The shell protocol.** Its events and requests, titles and application ids, `CloseRequested`; in the shell, the snapshot built from the events and the actions sent as requests, with a scenario that drives both directions.

   As implemented: windows are numbered when made and told once mapped. The scene tells each shell client what changed since it was last told, when events are sent, so focus moving anywhere, a window minimized, moved or closed, or a name set reaches it without each place that changes them knowing; a shell that connects late is told everything, applications never. Titles (at most 256 bytes) and application ids (128) come in chunks. `FocusWindow` shows the window's workspace and restores it. The Bloom host names its window from `OXYOS_APP_TITLE` and `OXYOS_APP_ID`, which its launcher gives it, and finishes on `CloseRequested`, as an Android activity does when its task is closed. As the shell, it gathers the events into `OxyOSShell` (a module with the state, the actions and an `OxyOSShellChanged` event), from which `sample/oxyos/shell-state.js` and the shell's entry build its snapshot and actions; applications are still the fixture's, and `activateApp` focuses an application's newest window. `scenarios/shell-windows.json` launches an application the dock does not pin: the shell is told its window and the dock shows it; minimized, it leaves the screen and stays in the dock; pressed there, the shell asks for it and it is restored and focused. With the compositor ignoring that request, the scenario fails.

## Consequences

- The shell is the session's own Bloom program in the compositor's real layers, and the shell's author keeps one React tree.
- Workspaces are the compositor's: input and focus never reach a window the user cannot see, and the shell cannot hide windows the compositor thinks are shown.
- The shell sees every window's application, title and workspace, and acts on any window. That is why only the `shell` grant opens the protocol.
