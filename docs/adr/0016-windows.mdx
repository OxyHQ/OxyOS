# ADR 0016: Windows

Status: **accepted** (chosen with the platform's author); steps 1 and 2 implemented. The `window` capability of [ADR 0015](./0015-application-model). Builds on [ADR 0002](./0002-bloom-platform-surface-api) (Bloom's platform adapter), [ADR 0004](./0004-gpu-composition) (composition), [ADR 0009](./0009-session-shell) (the shell) and [ADR 0011](./0011-transitions) (transitions).

## Problem

An OxyOS application's window is its client's buffer and nothing else: no controls, no shadow, square corners. It cannot zoom or minimize itself, cannot be resized by its edges, and is never told whether it is the active window. Its client can ask to be moved (`BeginMove`), but nothing in Bloom does.

The windows wanted are a Mac's, as macOS 26 and 27 draw them, not a title bar with buttons on it:
- **No title bar.** The content fills the window.
- **The controls are three glass bubbles** (close, minimize, zoom) **at the window's top leading corner, always in the same place.** The content is arranged around them:
  - in Mail, the sidebar starts below them;
  - in an application without a sidebar, the toolbar starts after them;
  - in a photo or a video, the content fills the window and the bubbles float over it.
- **Standard corners and a shadow,** deeper on the active window.

## Decision

1. **The window has no title bar; its content is the whole window.** The title stays a property of the window (`SetTitle`, for the shell, the overview and the screen reader), not something drawn over it.
2. **The controls are Bloom's, in the platform's place.** On OxyOS, Bloom draws the controls inside the application, as AppKit draws a Mac's inside each application, never the window server. So the glass is the application's own material, and the content is placed around them in the same frame. The platform says where they are, the same for every application:
   - **Size and place.** Three bubbles, each 14 px across in a 20 px target, 4 px apart, their centres 26 px from the window's top, the first 24 px from its leading edge (the left, or the right in a right-to-left locale).
   - **Their room** is 52 px from the top and 88 px from the leading edge.
   - **Colour.** On the active window they are red, yellow and green glass. On an inactive one they are neutral glass. The pointer over the group shows their symbols (×, −, and zoom's).
   - **The press.** A press shrinks a bubble (to 88 %) and lets it spring back as it acts, as macOS 27's do.

   Elsewhere the platform may draw its own controls (a Mac's traffic lights, a browser's over an installed web application) and report their room, which Bloom keeps the same way; a platform without windows (a phone, a browser tab) reports no capability and has none.
3. **The content adapts; the controls never move.** AppShell (Bloom's layout compositor, [ADR 0015](./0015-application-model)) keeps their room. The application declares nothing but its shape:
   - **A sidebar in flow** starts its content below their room; the controls sit at its top.
   - **Without one** (none, hidden or collapsed into a drawer), the header or toolbar starts after their room.
   - **Full-bleed content** (`windowControls="overlay"`: a photo, a video, a map) keeps no room. The controls float over it on a glass plate. While media plays they fade out when the pointer has been still for two seconds or has left the window, and come back as it moves, with the page's own controls.
   - **A full-screen window** shows no controls.
4. **The window is moved by its empty chrome.** The sidebar's top band and the header or toolbar are drag regions: a press there that no control inside takes starts a move (`BeginMove`, with the press's serial), and a double press zooms. Bloom's `WindowDragRegion` is that region, and AppShell uses it; an application needs none of its own.
5. **The compositor resizes windows by their edges.** A band 6 px wide outside each edge (in the shadow), and 12 px at the corners, shows the resize pointer and resizes from the edge or corner pressed. The client is configured as the compositor's own resizes configure it, and its popups follow. A window states the sizes it can take (`SetSizeLimits`): the compositor never configures it outside them. The resize follows the pointer live, as Wayland's interactive resize does: one configure in flight, the next (the latest size the pointer wants) sent once the client shows a frame for it, the window showing its last frame where it was meanwhile, so the edges not dragged never move; it ends at exactly the pointer's size. It is not animated.
6. **A window zooms and minimizes itself; it closes itself.**
   - **Zoom** (`SetMaximized`) fills the work area (the output without the panels' exclusive zones) and restores the frame it had. The frame animates between the two, as a Mac's zoom does (step 6).
   - **Minimize** (`Minimize`) is the shell's minimize, with its transition into the dock.
   - **The close bubble** is the application's own close request, the same path as the shell's `CloseRequested`. An application with something to save answers it (`onCloseRequest`, Bloom's); otherwise the window closes.
7. **The window is told its state.** `Configure` carries `maximized` as it carries `fullscreen`. A new event, `Activated`, says when a toplevel becomes and stops being the active window: the one that has, itself or through one of its popups or dialogs, the keyboard focus. The window learns it before the frame that shows it, so the controls and the content can draw it.
8. **The compositor draws corners and shadows.**
   - Every toplevel and dialog is drawn with corners of the same radius, 16 px, cut from its pixels by composition. The radius is reported to Bloom, so its panels are concentric with the window.
   - The window has a shadow: wider and darker on the active window, lighter on the others.
   - A full-screen window has neither.

   Composition (`oxyos-gpu`) gains the corner mask and the shadow, on the CPU reference and in the shader with the same integer arithmetic, as opacity did ([ADR 0011](./0011-transitions)).

## Bloom's API

`@oxy.so/bloom/platform-surface` gains the `window` capability and its adapter field. An adapter that does not report it is today's:

```ts
interface PlatformWindow {
  readonly controls: 'bloom' | 'platform';     // who draws them
  readonly metrics: {
    controls: { start: number; top: number; width: number; height: number }; // leading-relative
    room: { top: number; leading: number };
    radius: number;
  };
  getState(): { active: boolean; maximized: boolean; fullscreen: boolean };
  subscribe(listener: () => void): () => void;
  close(): void;      // the application's close request
  minimize(): void;
  zoom(): void;       // maximize or restore
  startMove(): void;  // from the press being handled
}
```

These are new in Bloom:

- **`useWindow()`:** the state and the actions, or `null` without the capability.
- **`WindowControls`:** the bubbles. It draws them where `controls` is `bloom`, and nothing otherwise.
- **`WindowDragRegion`.**
- **`useWindowControlsRoom()`:** the room to keep, zero without the capability.

AppShell gains `windowControls?: 'auto' | 'overlay'` (`auto`, the default, is decision 3). The settings layout is split out of `SettingsModal` (`SettingsLayout`: the rail, the pages, the narrow-width navigation, the saved toast), sized by its container and keeping the controls' room in its rail, so the same settings are a modal, an application window or a panel.

## Steps

1. **The protocol and the compositor:**
   - `SetSizeLimits`, `SetMaximized`, `Minimize`, `Activated`, and `maximized` in `Configure`;
   - the edge resize band;
   - the C ABI.

   Tests on the scene (limits held when resizing, the work area, activation through a popup, no move or resize when full-screen or locked) and through real sockets.

   *Done.* `SetSizeLimits` (request 59: the minimum and maximum width and height, a zero no bound), `SetMaximized` (60), `Minimize` (61), `Activated` (event 51), and `maximized` beside `fullscreen` in `Configure`; in the C ABI, `oxyos_client_begin_move`, `oxyos_client_set_fullscreen`, `oxyos_client_set_maximized`, `oxyos_client_minimize`, `oxyos_client_set_size_limits`, `OXYOS_EVENT_ACTIVATED` and the CONFIGURE flag's bits (`OXYOS_CONFIGURE_FULLSCREEN`, `OXYOS_CONFIGURE_MAXIMIZED`). The scene (`scene/window.rs`):
   - **Resizing.** Over a toplevel's band (6 px beyond each edge, the corners 12 px along them) the pointer is the resize shape for that edge or corner, and no window is entered. A press there raises and focuses the window, reaches no client and authorizes nothing, and each motion configures the window from the edges pressed, the others staying where they were, within its limits and never under 64×64. Whatever is drawn over the band and takes input there takes the press instead; a full-screen window has no band, and neither does anything under the lock or behind an open menu.
   - **Limits.** Limits a window is outside of configure it at once, and the shell's resizes keep to them too.
   - **Zoom** fills the output's work area (centred at the widest it can be if its limits are smaller) and restores the frame it had; a resize by hand ends it.
   - **Minimize** is the shell's minimize, and does nothing under the lock.
   - **The active window** is the toplevel whose window group has the focus; the shell's panels taking focus keep it, its overlays and the lock do not, and the window that goes is not told.

   Tests on the scene cover each of these (the edges and their shapes, a drag from the right, the left and both corners within limits, the band under another window, full screen and the lock, zoom against a top panel and with a maximum width, minimizing and the lock, activation through a menu, a panel, an overlay, the lock and a window destroyed). Through real sockets they come with the Bloom host's adapter (step 4).
2. **Corners and shadows in composition,** byte for byte on the CPU reference and the Vulkan device, and drawn only where they change (ADR 0005).

   *Done.* A layer has a corner radius (`Layer::radius`, at most half its shorter side): a pixel in a corner's square is covered by how far its centre is inside the corner's circle, to 1/64 pixel (an integer square root), so the edge is smooth, and the coverage scales the layer's opacity there (`opacity × coverage / 255`); at radius 0 every coverage is 255 and a layer is composed exactly as before. A shadow is a layer of its own (`Paint::Shadow`). Its pixels are a profile computed once on the CPU (`ShadowProfile`): the shape blurred by a Gaussian as CSS's `box-shadow` blurs it (σ = blur / 2), premultiplied black at the shadow's alpha, an entry per 1/8 pixel of signed distance from the shape, out to 3σ; the device keeps it as it keeps a surface's pixels. Each output pixel takes the entry for its distance from the rounded rectangle casting it, at the size the shadow is drawn, and none of it is drawn within the window it is beneath (as CSS draws an outer shadow, so a translucent window shows what is under it, not its own shadow). Along a side that is the true blur; past a corner the distance is to the corner's circle, a little rounder than a true blur. The reference and the shader share this arithmetic operation for operation (`oxyos-gpu`'s `shape.rs`), so they still agree byte for byte. The frame file is `OXYFRAME3` (each layer's radius, a shadow's shape, what it is beneath and its scale); `OXYFRAME2` and `OXYFRAME1` files read with square layers.

   The compositor (`window.rs`) draws every toplevel and dialog with corners of `WINDOW_RADIUS` (16 logical pixels, in `oxyos-protocol`, for the Bloom adapter to report, step 4) and its shadow beneath it; never a popup, the shell's surfaces, a lock surface or the splash. A frame is composed in the output's logical pixels, as every surface's rectangle is, so the radius is 16 of them. The active window's shadow falls 18 px with a blur of 48 at 35 % black; the others' 8 px with a blur of 24 at 20 %, close to a Mac's. The active window is step 1's, the one told `Activated` (`Scene::active_window`), and its dialogs are drawn active with it (`Scene::window_look`); a window drawn full-screen has neither corners nor shadow, from the frame that draws it full-screen (as its geometry follows the frame for its configure), and has them again from its first windowed frame. A window in a transition is drawn with its corners and shadow scaled, moved and faded with it, a window going from its last frame with its look then. A window's damage is its rectangle and its shadow's wherever it comes, goes, moves, changes places in the stacking or changes its look (active, inactive, full-screen), and in transition where it was and is drawn; a new frame of its own damages only itself.

   Tests: the coverage of a corner of radius 4 worked by hand (0, 51, 179 and 247 along its first row, mirrored in the other three corners), with an opacity (128 · 51/255 = 25); square layers against the digests of frames the reference composed before this step (at three opacities and two sizes); the profile (its ends, both sides of its edge, an entry 1.14σ out) and a shadow's pixels beside a side, below the window it is beneath, past a corner and at half size, each from the entry worked by hand; the Vulkan device against the reference with corners and shadows at six radii, four opacities, four scales and two profiles, and composed from damage (on Mesa's lavapipe: changing the shader's coverage, its distance or its clip each fails it); the frame file in its third and second formats; the scene's looks (toplevels, a dialog and its window, a menu, a panel, full-screen only from the frame drawn so and back, none active under the lock); and through real sockets, two windows: a corner pixel shows the desktop under the shadow's faint edge and the window is white inside the curve, the shadow below is darker than above and the active window's deeper, making the other active swaps them in a frame composed from its damage alone, and a full-screen window has neither. A change of look alone damages both shadows (failing when it is left out); every transition test's frames are still within their damage with shadows drawn. The development compositor's damaged share (`damaged_fraction_median`) now counts the union of the damage, not the sum of its rectangles, which a window's shadow overlapping itself in transition took past the whole output. Every host scenario that runs without a display passes with them (25, none of whose expected pixels needed to change: none is in a shadow), seven of them with every frame composed on lavapipe identical to the reference; the development compositor's own check that a dismissed menu leaves nothing compares a point below the window, now in its shadow, with what it showed once the window had arrived, not with the other window's colour.
3. **Bloom:** the capability, `useWindow`, `WindowControls`, `WindowDragRegion`, AppShell keeping the room, and `SettingsLayout`, with Bloom's tests for each shape (sidebar, toolbar, overlay, none).

   *Done* in Bloom ([OxyHQ/Bloom#517](https://github.com/OxyHQ/Bloom/pull/517), no release yet). The pieces:
   - **The capability:** `@oxy.so/bloom/platform-surface` reports `window`, with `PlatformWindow` as above and `usePlatformWindow()`; reporting it without the object throws, as `popup-surface` does.
   - **`@oxy.so/bloom/window`:**
     - `useWindow()`, its state compared by value, so an equal state does not render again;
     - `useWindowControlsRoom()`, zero without the capability or in full screen;
     - `WindowControls`, three 20 px targets across `metrics.controls`, labelled in Bloom's 14 languages;
     - `WindowDragRegion`, which claims a press only in the bubble phase, so any control inside wins; a second press within 500 ms zooms.
   - **AppShell** keeps the room for each shape (`windowControls`). A shell or settings layout inside another one that holds the window's corner is a panel, so the controls are never drawn twice.
   - **`SettingsLayout`** is split out of `SettingsModal`, sized by its own width; the modal is unchanged.
   - **Tests:** each shape, the drag region against react-native-web's real responder system, and the layout's width tiers. 23 renders of the modal and the shell without the capability are identical to before.
4. **The OxyOS adapter** in the Bloom host. A scenario on a sample window checks:
   - its controls (minimize into the dock, zoom filling the work area and back, close);
   - a move from its toolbar and a resize from its edge;
   - its look when active and inactive.

   *Done* (all but closing and the inactive look, below). The pieces:
   - **The adapter.** `sample/oxyos/platform-surface.js` gives Bloom the window when the host runs an application in a window of its own (not the shell, which has none). Its metrics are the ones above (`OxyOSWindow`'s constants), its state comes from `OXYOS_EVENT_ACTIVATED` and the configure's bits (`OxyOSWindowChanged` when they change), and its actions go through the C ABI.
   - **Moving.** `startMove()` sends `BeginMove` with the press being handled.
   - **Closing.** `close()` ends the application as the shell's close request does.
   - **The scenario.** `scenarios/windows.json` (`sample/WindowProbe.tsx`, Bloom's `SettingsLayout` in a 900×600 window) checks:
     - the page says the window is active, neither zoomed nor full screen;
     - a press held on the rail's top band moves the window with the pointer, through the development compositor's new `grab` step, which holds the press while the application answers it, as a hand does;
     - the green bubble zooms the window to the work area and the page is told, and pressing it again restores the frame;
     - the band beyond the right edge resizes the window, its left edge staying;
     - the yellow bubble minimizes it.

   It passes; with the adapter's `window` taken away (no controls, no state) all six steps fail. **Not covered by a scenario yet:** the close bubble, which ends the host and so the run, and the inactive look, which needs a second window. They are covered by Bloom's tests and the scene's activation tests (step 1).
5. **The Settings application** (`so.oxy.settings`), built on it.
6. **Live resize and zoom.** On the platform's ThinkPad (Iris Xe) an edge resize lagged and jumped, and zoom jumped to its new frame at once.

   *Done* (on the development compositor only; on the ThinkPad still to check). The causes, measured with a new step of the development compositor, `drag_paced` (a press on the output moved in N motions at a rate, the compositor serving the application between them, as a hand drags; it reports the configures sent and answered, the frames shown, the time from each motion to a frame answering it and where each edge was in every frame):
   - **A configure for every motion.** Each motion configured the window, and a client slower than the motions (any, at a mouse's 125 Hz) answered each with a frame already stale when it arrived: it was discarded, and the window showed nothing new until the pointer stopped. Dragging the sample's Inbox 300 px in 60 motions at 60 Hz sent 60 configures, 12 answered by a frame shown (1 when the machine was busier); at 125 Hz, 1 of 125, the window frozen for the second the drag took.
   - **Not the causes:** layout (Fabric's commit, Yoga and the mount: 0.2 to 0.7 ms a size), a new shared buffer at each size (0.4 ms to present, as at the same size), the opposite edge (already applied with the frame that answers the configure).
   - **The host's whole redraw** at each size (4 to 9 ms for the Inbox, 1.5 to 2 for the probe, on this shared machine) is what a size costs.

   The pieces:
   - **One configure in flight** (`scene/window.rs`). While the window has not shown a frame for its latest configure, an edge resize keeps only the geometry the pointer wants last (`Node::wanted`), and `end_frame` sends it when that frame is displayed. Any other configure (zoom, the shell's, limits, a new scale) replaces it.
   - **The zoom's transition** (`transitions.rs`, `Change::Zoomed`): a window's frame changed at once (`SetMaximized` either way, the shell's `resize_window` for maximizing and tiling) goes from where it was drawn to where it is configured in 250 ms easing out, each edge by its own distance (`Shift::Between`), so width and height scale each by their own factor. Its content is whatever frame it shows: its old one stretched toward the new rectangle until the client shows the new size, then that one from the rectangle between to its own. It is drawn as a window of the size it is drawn at (corners of 16 px, its shadow unscaled), and damaged where it was and is drawn, frame by frame, as every transition is (ADR 0005, 0011). Composition samples nearest neighbour (ADR 0004), as for every transition (a window's arrival at 96 %, its minimize into the dock): a filtered zoom needs bilinear sampling in the CPU reference and the shader with the same integer arithmetic, byte for byte, which is composition's own change, for every transition at once, and not part of this step. It is held at the end while the client has not shown the new size (at most a second), is over if the window is moved meanwhile, begins from where it is drawn if zoomed again, and is at once with reduce motion. Edge resizes are not animated.
   - **Never drawn scaled.** The platform's author saw a window's content pixelated while resizing it. The cause: on a display a frame is composed, then shown at the flip; a configure arriving between (a motion, before one configure in flight) replaced the geometry that frame answered, so it was drawn, and then shown, at the window's old size, its buffer stretched to it by nearest neighbour. Now a frame on its way keeps the geometry of the configure it answers (`Node::answering`) when a newer one replaces it, and is drawn and shown at that, its own size; one configure in flight already keeps a resize's from overlapping. During a resize the window shows its last frame at its own size, as a Mac's does; nothing is ever stretched but by the zoom's transition.
   - **Every configure is answered.** The host presents a frame for each configure, even one that changes nothing it draws (`Window::answered`): shrunk a pixel, the Inbox's centred content does not move, and without that frame the resize waited for it forever. A configure of the size shown (a new state, the same scale again) holds no resize, since a client may have nothing new to draw for it.
   - **The host draws only what changed at a new size** (`damage.cpp`, `drawDamaged`): the window is compared view by view across a new size at the same scale, a plain fill resized in place (`drawsFlat`) damaging only where it gained or lost area, and the previous frame kept where both sizes overlap. The probe, whose sidebar stays, redraws 0.7 of the window per size; the Inbox, which centres its content, all of it, as all of it moves.

   Measured (median of three runs each, the development compositor on the CPU, on a shared 32-core machine whose load was high and varied, so the times are a range, not a benchmark):

   | Drag (motions, rate) | Configures sent | Frames shown | Motion to its frame, median / max | Drawing a size, share drawn |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | Inbox, right edge 300 px in (60, 60 Hz) | 60 → 53 | 12 → 53 | 118 / 583 ms → 26 / 68 ms | 5.2 → 4.5 ms, all |
   | Inbox, right edge 300 px in (125, 125 Hz) | 125 → 59 | 1 → 59 | 522 / 1021 ms → 30 / 84 ms | 4.8 → 5.0 ms, all |
   | Inbox, left edge 150 px out (62, 125 Hz) | 62 → 37 | 1 → 37 | 278 / 520 ms → 24 / 45 ms | 4.5 → 4.2 ms, all |
   | Inbox, a corner 200 × 100 px (125, 125 Hz) | 125 → 77 | 1 → 77 | 519 / 1016 ms → 22 / 42 ms | 4.2 → 3.9 ms, all |
   | Probe, right edge 300 px in (60, 60 Hz) | 60 → 60 | 60 → 60 | 13 / 16 ms → 14 / 19 ms | 1.9 → 1.5 ms, all → 0.70 |
   | Probe, right edge 300 px in (125, 125 Hz) | 125 → 109 | 5 → 109 | 390 / 884 ms → 17 / 30 ms | 2.0 → 1.3 ms, all → 0.70 |
   | Probe, left edge 150 px out (62, 125 Hz) | 62 → 54 | 5 → 54 | 102 / 313 ms → 16 / 33 ms | 1.8 → 1.3 ms, all → 0.69 |
   | Probe, a corner 200 × 100 px (125, 125 Hz) | 125 → 110 | 5 → 110 | 173 / 649 ms → 17 / 36 ms | 1.7 → 1.7 ms, all → 0.97 |

   After, every configure sent is answered by a frame shown (none wasted; before, at 125 Hz, all but one to five went unanswered), and the window shows a new size every 9 to 19 ms while the pointer moves, where before it showed one or a few in the whole drag. Laying out a size takes 0.15 to 0.44 ms before and after. The motion to its frame is from a motion to the first frame shown that answers a configure sent at or after it; before, that was mostly the frame after the release. A corner's drag redraws nearly all of the probe, as its height changes everything below its top.

   Tests: on the scene, a drag's motions faster than the client configure it once, the latest size goes when its frame shows and the release ends at the pointer's size with nothing after it; a drag from a corner keeps the far edges in every frame shown; the shell's resize replaces what a drag wanted; a configure of the size shown holds nothing; a drag from the left edge, its client answering every other motion, a motion while each frame is on its way to the display, and the shell's resize while a frame answering the drag's is: every surface, displayed or being composed, drawn at its buffer's size (without `answering`, the last fails, a 523 px frame drawn at 511). On the transitions, a zoom's rectangle at its start, halfway (87.5 %, edge by edge) and end, held until the new size shows, ended after a second if never shown, over when moved, from where it is when zoomed again, and nothing with reduce motion. Through real sockets, a zoom on the compositor's clock: the old frame drawn stretched into the rectangle between, then the new one, every frame within its damage, ending exactly at the work area; restored with reduce motion, at once. The windows scenario's zoom and restore report their `zoomed` transitions, and a new step drags the left edge 200 px in 50 motions at 125 Hz: every configure answered by a frame shown, no more configures than frames, no frame composed drawing the window at another size than its buffer's (`drag_paced`'s `scaled`, checked in every paced drag), the right, top and bottom edges never moving, the window at exactly the pointer's size. `scenarios/window-resize.json` drags the Inbox's right edge, left edge and a corner at 125 Hz, the corner's first motion a pixel, with the same checks. Without one configure in flight the scene's first test, that step and every step of `window-resize.json` fail (49 of 50, 61 of 62, 124 of 125 configures unanswered, one frame shown); with the host that left a configure changing nothing unanswered, `window-resize.json`'s corner stalls; without the zoom's transition the socket test and both zoom steps fail. The scene's corner test held before too: the far edges already moved with the frame answering their configure. On several displays ([ADR 0018](./0018-several-displays)), through real sockets on two outputs: a window zoomed on the second (at 1.5) fills that output's work area, its frame between drawn on that screen; one across both, its origin on the first, fills the first's, its frame between drawn on both screens while it crosses them; every frame of each screen within that screen's damage. The development compositor's steps settle only once the window shows a frame for its latest configure (on lavapipe a restore's frame came after its last frame had been unchanged for the settling time). Every host scenario that runs without a display passes (31), with every frame the host drew from its damage checked against the frame drawn whole; the windows, window-resize and session-two-outputs scenarios also with every frame composed on Mesa's lavapipe from its damage identical to the CPU reference, the zooms' frames among them. Not verified: the session on a real display (KMS, the Iris Xe, a real mouse), where a configure follows the flip that shows the frame answering the last; the platform's author checks it on the ThinkPad.

## Not in this decision

- Full-screen's own controls (a Mac shows the menu bar and the controls when the pointer reaches the top edge), and tiling.
- The window's menu (a long press on zoom) and the menu bar ([ADR 0015](./0015-application-model)).
- Windows of clients that are not Bloom applications: they get corners, shadows, resizing and their state, and draw their own controls.
