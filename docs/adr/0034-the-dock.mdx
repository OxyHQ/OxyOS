# ADR 0034: The dock's application menu and the Trash

Status: **accepted; implemented**. Builds on [ADR 0009](./0009-session-shell) (the shell), [ADR 0013](./0013-launching-applications) (launching, launch records), [ADR 0016](./0016-windows) (windows and the close request), [ADR 0019](./0019-settings) (the user's preferences), [ADR 0024](./0024-system-shortcuts) (hiding and quitting an application), [ADR 0028](./0028-file-access) (the Trash, opening in another application), [ADR 0030](./0030-installing-applications) (system sheets) and [ADR 0033](./0033-protocol-versions) (protocol versions).

**10 November 2026: the dock hides automatically, a Mac's *Automatically hide and show the Dock* and ⌥⌘D.** A preference of the person's, `dock_autohide` (off by default, as a Mac's), kept by the session as every preference ([ADR 0019](./0019-settings)), turned on and off by Settings › Desktop & Dock's switch (*Automatically hide and show the Dock*; *Ocultar y mostrar el Dock automáticamente*, in its own page as a Mac's) and by **Super+Alt+D**, a Mac's ⌥⌘D with Super as ⌘ ([ADR 0024](./0024-system-shortcuts)): the compositor takes the chord as every system shortcut and asks the program running it to turn the preference over, which it keeps (the file written, every client told, Settings' switch following) and applies, exactly as a change from the switch.
- **The window server hides it, as a Mac's does; the shell draws its dock as always.** While the preference is on, the shell's bottom panel keeps no strip of its output out of where windows go (the work area reaches the output's bottom, so a window zoomed fills it, [ADR 0016](./0016-windows)) and is off the screen but while the person wants it: the pointer at the output's last row of pixels brings it up; it stays while the pointer is over it, while it has the keyboard (Super+F3 brings it up to take it, [ADR 0039](./0039-keyboard-access)), while a menu or tooltip of it is open, and for a second after a window went into it (the genie's half second, [ADR 0043](./0043-the-genie), and as long again, so the window is seen arriving); it goes down once none of these holds. Hidden, it is not drawn and takes no input, as a minimized window; it comes up over what is beneath it. Locked, nothing moves beneath the lock. The scene's `dock_hiding.rs` decides; the turn follows it once (the keyboard, the popups, the moment after a minimize) and the pointer as it moves.
- **It slides,** all of its height, down off the output's bottom edge and back up, unfaded, on the zoom's spring (340 ms, critically damped, [ADR 0011](./0011-transitions)), as transitions `EdgeHidden` and `EdgeShown` (`edge_hidden`, `edge_shown` in the development compositor's report); with Reduce Motion, at once. A Mac's delay before the Dock comes up and its animation's time are not documented by Apple; this one comes up at once at the edge.
- **Tests.** The scene (`scene/tests/dock_hiding.rs`): no strip kept, the dock down, the edge's last row bringing it up under the pointer and the row above it not, staying over it and going once left, off again shown with its strip; a window minimized holding it up for its second and no longer; Super+F3 bringing it up with the keyboard, kept while it has it and down once the window has it back; Super+Alt+D asking the program and reaching no application, Super+D and Super+Alt+Ctrl+D nothing; locked, nothing moving. The compositor (`settings.rs`): Super+Alt+D turning the preference over and applying it, Settings' switch the same, twice in a turn over and back. `oxyos-preferences`: the key, its default and its refusal of anything but true and false. Settings: the key's name. Scenarios `dock-hiding.json` (Spanish, at 1) and `-scaled` (English, at 1.25): Settings › Desktop & Dock's switch on (the preference kept, the work area to the bottom, the dock sliding down), the pointer near the edge (nothing), at its last row (sliding up), over the dock (staying) and away (sliding down), Super+Alt+D off (kept, the strip back, the switch off, Settings hearing nothing) and on again.
- **Not yet:** the shell's own overlays still leave the dock's room at the bottom (Launchpad's grid, the overview) while it hides; a Mac's Dock magnification, position on the screen's left or right and the minimize effect's choice.


**8 November 2026: Open at Login is Settings' too, and applications add themselves to it** ([ADR 0060](./0060-login-items-and-background-activity)). Decision 5's `open_at_login` is the one list Settings › General › Login Items & Extensions shows and changes (`SetApplicationList` with `OpenAtLogin` is taken from the `settings` grant as from `shell`, and both are told it), so the dock's menu and Settings each show the other's change at once; an application adds itself to it, or takes itself out, through the session (`BackgroundItem`, protocol version 33), as a Mac's application does through `SMAppService`.

**6 November 2026: the dock's icons are arranged as a Mac's** (decisions 10 and 11, the owner's request: *"tampoco puedo reordenar ni hacer nada con los items del dock como sí se puede en macOS"*). An icon dragged along the dock moves there, the others sliding apart; dragged off, it leaves the dock in a poof with its sound, or, running, stays until it quits; an application dragged from Launchpad, or from any application that offers one, is kept where it is dropped; files dropped on an application's icon open in it, the icon answering only when it opens them all; and every bit of it from the keyboard and the screen reader. *Dragging icons*, below, is no longer to come.

**6 November 2026: a press on the frontmost application's icon minimizes it** (decision 9, the owner's decision, asked twice: *"en el dock cuando toco una app que ya está abierta, no se minimiza"*). A Mac's Dock does nothing more for a click on the frontmost application's icon than bring it forward again; OxyOS's minimizes its windows shown on the desktop into the icon through the genie ([ADR 0043](./0043-the-genie)), and a press again brings them back out of it, the genie in reverse, the one that was frontmost frontmost again with the keyboard. The rest is a Mac's: an application running behind comes forward, one whose windows are all minimized has the one used last restored, and an Option-click brings an application forward and hides the others.

**5 November 2026: Show All Windows is App Exposé.** The menu's Show All Windows opened the overview with the application's windows alone, a filtered Mission Control; a Mac's opens App Exposé: the application's windows laid out large across the screen, the others gone, its minimized windows in a row below, the windows themselves flying to their places and back. It now opens App Exposé ([ADR 0045](./0045-app-expose)), the same that Super+↓, three or four fingers swiped down and a scroll up on the application's icon open, and the overview no longer filters: one App Exposé, not two. A scroll down on the icon closes it, and while it is open a press on another application's icon shows that application's windows instead, as a Mac's Dock does. Decision 2's entry below reads so.

**5 November 2026: the dock's top corners are round, square only beside a zoomed window** (correcting the note of 3 November). The platform's author: the dock's two top corners are round, and stop being round only while an application is maximized. The note of 3 November made them square at all times, because the shell was not told which windows are zoomed; that was the wrong call, and the platform now tells it. The compositor tells the session's shell, for each output, whether a window shown there covers it (`OutputCovered`, event 81, protocol version 22, [ADR 0033](./0033-protocol-versions)): zoomed to fill its work area ([ADR 0016](./0016-windows), decision 7) or full screen, on the active workspace, neither minimized nor hidden with its application (the lock leaves it as it is beneath, as the desktop stays beneath the lock screen); after the output's `Output` when it is first told, and whenever it changes. The shell keeps it apart from its state, as the windows' sizes are (`useDisplayCovered`, the platform's), and only the dock's shelf reads it (`DockShelf`, `apps/shell/src/components/Dock.tsx`): its top corners have the radius they had before 3 November, half its height (28), while nothing covers its display, the wallpaper showing above their curve, and are square while a window does, so the window meets it along a straight edge; between the two they move on the zoom's spring (340 ms, critically damped, [ADR 0011](./0011-transitions), step 6), squaring off with the window's own corners, and with Reduce Motion they change at once. A window zoomed or restored renders the shelf and nothing else of the shell. The rest of the note of 3 November stands: no outline and no border (on a rounded view a border keeps the background 0.8 device pixels inside every edge), flush with the display's sides and bottom, and the icons their own pictures. Tests: the scene tells each output's cover once, as a window zooms, is minimized and restored, leaves and comes back with its workspace, hides with its application, goes full screen and closes, on its own output alone, nothing for the lock, and a shell that connects is told it (`scene/tests/dock.rs`); version 22's event is never written for version 21 (protocol tests) and is the C ABI's `OXYOS_EVENT_OUTPUT_COVERED`. `dock-edges.json`, `dock-edges-scaled.json`, `dock-edges-dark.json` and `dock-edges-dark-scaled.json` (1 and 1.25, light and dark) check the dock with nothing zoomed (the wallpaper above each curve, the shelf's colour just inside it, its curve's anti-aliased pixels, its bottom row and sides the shelf's colour exactly), square while a window is zoomed (the shelf's colour in its very corner pixels, the window's last row right above) and round again restored, the shelf's radii going from 28 to 0 and back monotonically over the frames between (the host's report of what React's commits change); `dock-edges-reduce-motion.json` checks the change at once with Reduce Motion; `window-zoom-scaled.json` the wallpaper at the dock's corner restored; `shell-events.json` that a zoom and a restore render the dock's shelf alone.

**3 November 2026: the dock is one surface.** The platform's author saw the dock with "a border, a margin around it that it should not have", and had asked before that, as a zoomed window's corners are square ([ADR 0016](./0016-windows), decision 7), the dock's should not be round either. The dock was a full-width strip with a one-sided top border (`border-t`, `#e5e9ef`; `#2c2f36` dark) and fully rounded top corners, and its icons were each put on a white circle with a ring. Three things drew what he saw: the border was a line along its top and round its corners; a bordered view with rounded corners keeps its background 0.8 device pixels inside every edge, as Android's `BackgroundDrawable` does (its `pathAdjustment`, which the Bloom host draws faithfully), so the wallpaper showed down the display's sides and along its bottom; and the round corners showed the wallpaper past them, between the dock and a zoomed window too. Now the dock is the shelf's colour from edge to edge of the display, flush with its sides and its bottom, with square corners and no outline: what ChromeOS's shelf is beside a maximized window, and what the author asked for; it keeps its place, height, colours and icons. Each icon is its own picture with nothing added (`IconBody`, `apps/shell/src/apps.tsx`): a picture that is its own shape (Dropsea's, as the platform measures it, [ADR 0011](./0011-transitions)'s launcher) is drawn as it is, and any other is clipped to the icon grid body's corners and drawn on nothing, so Dropsea's own shape, Atlas's and Inbox's own white and the Trash's bin on its own navy show as they are, never a ring or a plate. A floating dock, as a Mac's, was not chosen: it keeps a margin round itself by design. `scenarios/dock-edges.json`, `dock-edges-scaled.json`, `dock-edges-dark.json` and `dock-edges-dark-scaled.json`, at 1 and 1.25, light and dark, check every corner and edge pixel of the dock exactly (the shelf's colour, no outline, no wallpaper), with a window beside it, zoomed (its last row right above the dock at both sides) and restored, and Dropsea's and Atlas's pictures to their tiles' edges; each check fails with the dock as it was.

**4 November 2026: windows minimize into their application's icon.** A Mac's Dock keeps a section of minimized windows by the Trash unless *Minimize windows into application icon* is on; OxyOS's dock has no such section, so a window minimized goes into its application's icon, through the genie ([ADR 0043](./0043-the-genie); since 5 November 2026 the only effect, with no setting). The dock tells the compositor where each icon is for each of its application's windows (`PlaceMinimizeTarget`, protocol version 20); the running mark stays under the icon while the window is minimized.

**2 November 2026:** Stash, named below, was renamed **Dropsea** ([OxyHQ/Dropsea](https://github.com/OxyHQ/Dropsea), [dropsea.net](https://dropsea.net)). Its application id is now `so.oxy.dropsea`, its URL scheme `dropsea://` (the dock's Trash opens `dropsea://trash`), the variable its package is built with `DROPSEA_DIR`, and its scenarios `dropsea*.json`, `keyboard-dropsea*.json` and `perf-dropsea*.json`. The decision stands as written; its text keeps the name it was taken under.

**5 November 2026:** the Trash sounds as a Mac's: files dropped on it, and its emptying once the person accepted the sheet, each play the system's Trash sound once ([ADR 0044](./0044-system-sounds)).

## Problem

On a Mac, pressing and holding a Dock icon, right-clicking it or Control-clicking it opens a menu above it: the application's windows (the current one checked), Show All Windows, Hide, Options (Keep in Dock, Open at Login, Show in Finder) and Quit, which Option turns into Force Quit and which an application that does not respond shows directly. An application not running offers Open and Options. The Trash sits at the Dock's end: a click opens it in Finder, and its menu offers Open and Empty Trash.

OxyOS's dock (`apps/shell/src/components/Dock.tsx`) had no menu at all. The platform had some of the pieces: the shell hid and quit an application by any of its windows (`HideApplication`, `QuitApplication`, ADR 0024) and knew each window's application, title and state. It had no way to end an application that does not answer, to know that one does not, to keep the person's dock or the applications they open at login, to open an application at a place of its own, or to tell a Control-click on a surface without the keyboard from a plain one. Nothing showed a Trash.

## Decision

1. **The menu is the shell's, drawn with Bloom's context menu; the name is Bloom's tooltip.** The pointer over an icon shows its name above it, as a Mac's Dock does: Bloom's `Tooltip` (15.84.0), which on a platform with popups is in a popup of the platform's (`role: 'tooltip'`: no grab, presses passing through it), since the dock is a shell strip as tall as itself and clips anything drawn above it; it goes as the pointer leaves and while the icon's menu is open, and a press hides it until the pointer comes back, as a desktop's tooltip. The host opens a tooltip's popup without a grab and with an empty input region. For the menu, each icon is a `ContextMenuTrigger` of Bloom's `ContextMenu` (the rows `DropdownMenu` and `Menubar` share), in the platform's popup above the icon (`side="top"`, centred, 10 px off it), its submenu (Options) flying out beside its row. Its keys are Bloom's popup navigation (arrows, Home, End, Return, Escape, a letter), and the popup is announced as a menu named by the application. It opens on:
   - a long press, held still for half a second (Bloom's trigger's long press);
   - a secondary click (the trigger's secondary button);
   - a Control-click, a primary press with Control held, which then opens nothing else.

   The dock never draws a menu of its own. What the menu offers is worked out from the snapshot (`apps/shell/src/dock.ts`), and an entry the platform cannot do (an action it does not give) is not offered.

2. **What the menu offers, as a Mac's Dock:**
   - **A running application:** its windows by title (choosing one shows it; its current one checked: the active one, else the one it used last, since the menu's popup has the keyboard), Show All Windows (App Exposé, [ADR 0045](./0045-app-expose); before 5 November 2026 the overview with that application's windows alone), Hide, Options › Keep in Dock and Open at Login, and Quit.
   - **An application not running:** Open and Options.
   - **Force Quit** replaces Quit while Option (Alt) is held as the menu opens, and whenever a window of the application does not respond; the menu then says so first (*La aplicación no responde*).

   Quit is the application's own close request to each of its windows (ADR 0024): the application may answer it (unsaved changes, Bloom's `onCloseRequest`), and a Bloom application whose last window closes ends its process. Force Quit is the session's (decision 4).

3. **Whether an application responds is the compositor's to know.** A client of version 8 or newer is sent `Ping` when it is given input (a press, a key, a touch), asked to close a window or activated, while no Ping to it is unanswered. Its library answers `Pong` as it reads its events, so a process stuck in its own work does not. One that has not answered in five seconds (`PING_TIMEOUT`, Mutter's and close to a Mac's) is not responding: its windows say so to the shell (`responding` at the end of `WindowState`) until it answers. A client of an older version is never pinged, and so always responds.

4. **The session decides force quit, never the application.** The shell asks by a window (`ForceQuitApplication`). The scene names the clients of that window's application that the session started (their launch records, ADR 0013) and no other; the program running the compositor ends their processes (SIGKILL) only if its launcher started them (`Launcher::force_quit`). The shell, the login screen, the lock screen and a client claiming an application's id are never launches, so they are never ended. The application is told nothing and cannot refuse.

5. **The person's lists of applications are user preferences the session keeps.** Keep in Dock and Open at Login change one of two lists: the applications kept in the dock, in its order (`dock`), and those opened at login (`open_at_login`). The shell sends a list whole (`SetApplicationList`, the ids one a line); the session validates it (each id the registry's rule, none twice, at most 64), keeps it in the preferences file (`dock = …`, `open_at_login = …`, ADR 0019), answers (`PreferenceResult`) and tells every shell (`ApplicationList`), when one connects too. Until the person changes the dock, it is the registry's (each entry's `X-OxyOS-Pinned`) and `chosen` says so. An application not kept but running stays in the dock until it quits, after those kept. A whole list, not a toggle, is what dragging icons to reorder them will send.

   The session opens the applications at login when it starts, in their order (`oxyos-session`; the development compositor as it starts its shell), never on the login screen.

6. **Modifiers reach the surface under the pointer.** Every `Pointer` event carries the keyboard's modifiers held (`modifiers`, version 8: Shift, Control, Alt, Super), whichever surface has the keyboard, as X11's and macOS's pointer events carry them. The dock never has the keyboard, so a Control-click or an Option-click on it is told so; the Bloom host gives them to React Native's W3C pointer events (`ctrlKey`, `altKey`). A client of an older version reads none held and keeps following its own keys.

7. **The Trash sits at the dock's end, past a divider.** Its icon is the bin the platform's author gave (`apps/shell/assets/trash.png`), the same full or empty. It is the platform's Trash, freedesktop.org's (ADR 0028, decision 5), which every file manager shares:
   - **A click opens it in the file manager.** The shell asks the session to open a URL (`OpenUrl`, citing the press), the platform's launch with a URL: the session starts the application whose entry declares the URL's scheme (`MimeType=x-scheme-handler/…`, as freedesktop.org's entries declare the schemes they open; an installed package's `opens.schemes` are written so) with the URL as React Native's `Linking` initial URL and expo-linking's linking URL (`OXYOS_LAUNCH_URL`), and answers with `LaunchResult`. The OxyOS platform's shell opens `stash://trash`: Stash (`so.oxy.stash`, the file manager) declares its own `stash:` scheme, and Expo Router opens the URL at its route `app/(browser)/trash.tsx`, nothing of Stash in the OS. An application already running is started again with the URL, a window of its own, as Finder opens another window on its Trash. A `file:` URL is not opened this way (a file opens by its type), nor one no entry declares.
   - **Files dropped on it** from any application go into it ([ADR 0035](./0035-drag-and-drop), decision 12): a move, the shell asking the session with the drop's serial.
   - **Its menu:** Open, and Empty Trash while it holds anything. The session tells the shell how many items the user's Trashes hold (`TrashItems`), following them as they change (inotify, through `oxyos-files`' watcher, and the mount table for volumes' Trashes).
   - **Emptying asks through a system sheet.** The shell asks (`EmptyTrash`, citing the press); the session asks the person in a system sheet (ADR 0030, decision 9: *¿Seguro que quieres eliminar permanentemente los ítems de la Papelera?*, how many go, *Vaciar Papelera* destructive, *Cancelar* first) and empties every Trash of the user's (`oxyos_files::trash::empty`) only once they accept, citing their own press. The OS empties only its own Trash: what an application keeps in a Trash of its own (Oxy Files' in Stash) is the application's.

8. **Protocol version 8** (ADR 0033), added only:

   | Direction | Opcode | Message |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | Request | 230 | `ForceQuitApplication { window }` (shell) |
   | Request | 231 | `Pong { serial }` (any client) |
   | Request | 232 | `SetApplicationList { serial, list, index, last, text }` (shell) |
   | Request | 233 | `OpenUrl { serial, input, index, last, text }` (shell) |
   | Request | 234 | `EmptyTrash { input }` (shell) |
   | Event | 230 | `Ping { serial }`, sent unasked: never to a client of version 7 or older |
   | Event | 231 | `ApplicationList { list, chosen, index, last, text }` (shell) |
   | Event | 232 | `TrashItems { items }` (shell) |
   | Event | 4 | `Pointer` grows `modifiers` (a version 7 reader reads none held) |
   | Event | 37 | `WindowState` grows `responding` (a version 7 reader reads responding) |

   The grown fields are `versions.rs`' `GROWN` table, which now lists what each version added to each event; the client library refuses the requests to a compositor of version 7. In the C ABI: `OxyOSEvent.modifiers`, `OXYOS_MODIFIER_*`, `OXYOS_WINDOW_RESPONDING`, `OXYOS_MANAGE_FORCE_QUIT_APPLICATION`, `OXYOS_EVENT_APPLICATION_LIST`, `OXYOS_EVENT_TRASH_ITEMS`, `oxyos_client_set_application_list`, `oxyos_client_open_url` and `oxyos_client_empty_trash`. `Ping` is answered inside the library and never reaches a C client.

9. **A press on an application's icon** (6 November 2026; the shell's `dockPress`, `apps/shell/src/dock.ts`) does what a Mac's Dock does, and one thing more, which is the platform's author's decision and no Mac's: **the frontmost application's icon pressed minimizes it.**
   - **The frontmost application** (the active window's): its windows shown on the desktop the person is on, neither minimized nor hidden, go into its icon together, each through the genie ([ADR 0043](./0043-the-genie)); the keyboard goes to the window used last of what stays, as Super+M gives it. The dock keeps which windows its press minimized.
   - **Pressed again**, every window it has minimized, those windows come back out of the icon together, the genie in reverse, restored in the order they were used so the one that was frontmost is frontmost again, with the keyboard. When none of them is still minimized (each was minimized on its own, Super+M or its title bar), the one used last comes back alone, as a Mac's Dock restores a minimized window.
   - **An application running and not frontmost** comes forward: its window used last is activated, its desktop shown, and an application hidden (Super+H) shows again. Nothing is minimized.
   - **An application not running** opens: the session starts it (ADR 0013).
   - **Option (Alt) held:** on another application, it comes forward and every other application shown (a window neither minimized nor hidden) hides, as a Mac's Option-click on a Dock icon; on the frontmost application, it hides, as a Mac's does. A Mac hides only the application left behind with Option alone and every other one with Option and Command; OxyOS hides every other one with Option, as the person asked: one modifier for the one meaning.
   - **Launchpad, Mission Control or App Exposé open:** a press brings the application forward and closes them; it never minimizes what the person cannot see. In App Exposé another application's icon shows that application's windows instead (5 November 2026, above).
   - A Control-click, a secondary click and a long press open the menu (decision 1) and press nothing; a scroll on the icon is App Exposé's ([ADR 0045](./0045-app-expose)). From the keyboard (Super+F3, [ADR 0039](./0039-keyboard-access)) Space and Return are the same press, without Option.

   The shell asks the compositor for each window (`MinimizeWindow`, `FocusWindow`, `HideApplication`, ADR 0009 and ADR 0024): nothing new in the protocol.

10. **Arranging the dock** (6 November 2026), as a Mac's Dock, through the platform's drag ([ADR 0035](./0035-drag-and-drop)): an application dragged is the platform's media type for one, `application/x-oxyos-application`, its registry id, beside `application/x-oxyos-files`'s files, so any application can offer one and only the dock reads it, at the drop.
    - **An icon dragged along the dock** (a mouse press moved four points, as Dropsea's items; a Control-click, a long press and a secondary click are the menu's) lifts out of its place as the platform's drag, the icon as it looks its image. Its place closes and a gap opens where it was, at once, so nothing moves as it lifts. Over the dock the gap follows the pointer among the applications kept, the icons sliding apart and together on a critically damped spring of 250 ms (at once with Reduce Motion): it is worked out from the places as laid out with the gap open (`gapAt`), so it is always under the pointer and never flickers between two places. Past those kept (over the applications only running, the Trash) it is their end.
    - **Dropped on the dock** (the whole shelf is the target, its ends and the space around the icons too), the application is kept at the gap, the person's list sent whole (`SetApplicationList`, decision 5) and kept in their preferences; the dock shows that order at once, the icon in the gap, until the session tells its list. An application running and not kept, dragged into the dock, is kept there.
    - **Dragged off the dock and let go** anywhere the drag finds no taker (the desktop, a window), it leaves the dock: one not running vanishes where it was let go in a poof with the poof's sound, as a Mac's (the platform's vanishing drag, ADR 0035's `DragVanish`, the compositor drawing the poof and playing `poof`, [ADR 0044](./0044-system-sounds)); one running is no longer kept but stays, after those kept, until it quits, and its image goes back. **Escape** puts it back where it was, and nothing changes.
    - **From Launchpad**: an application's icon dragged (the same slop) is the same drag; dropped on the dock it is kept at the gap, the one it was already kept at leaving its place as it goes. Launchpad stays open. The shell knows what it drags from the dock and Launchpad itself (`shellDrag`); another application's drag says only its type before the drop, so its gap opens, and the dock reads its id at the drop.
    - **From the keyboard** (the dock with the keyboard, Super+F3, [ADR 0039](./0039-keyboard-access)): Option and ← or → move the focused icon kept a place along those kept, as kept; at an end it stays and nothing is said. **From the screen reader**, an application's icon offers its actions: *Mover a la izquierda* and *Mover a la derecha* where it can, *Quitar del Dock* when kept, *Mantener en el Dock* when not (React Native's accessibility actions, beside the menu's own). A move says the icon's new place, politely, once: *Atlas, posición 3 de 3*.
11. **Files dropped on an application's icon open in it** (6 November 2026), as on a Mac. Each application's icon is a drop target taking files only when the application declares that it opens every dragged file's type: its entry's `MimeType` (`opens`, which the session tells the shell), against the types the drag says its files are (ADR 0035's `DragFileType`, as HTML5's items say theirs, the content never seen before the drop). Then its picture darkens as the Trash's does, and nothing else answers; an icon of an application that opens none of them does not, and a drop there drops nothing, its image going back. On the drop the shell asks the session to open the files with that application (`OpenFiles`, citing the drop), which opens each file of the person's that the application declares, starting it if it is not running, as a file opens by its type (ADR 0028, decision 6). The Trash keeps decision 7's files.

## Tests

- **Protocol:** every new message round-trips; a version 8 `Pointer` is 35 bytes and a version 7 writer leaves its modifiers out, read back as none; a version 7 `WindowState` reads as responding; a `Ping` is never written for version 7; which version introduced each request.
- **Scene** (`scene/tests/dock.rs`): a press pings a client of version 8 once while unanswered; within the timeout it responds, at the timeout the shell is told its window does not, a Pong of another serial changes nothing and its own restores it; a client of version 7 is never pinged; a close request and an activation ping, a disconnect forgets the ping; force quit names the launched clients of the window's application (both processes of one application) and none of a client the session did not start, only from the shell; a list in chunks handed whole, every shell told the session's, a late shell too, nothing told twice; a URL handed with whether it cited an input; a pointer press carries the modifiers held while another surface has the keyboard, left and right keys each their own.
- **Preferences:** the lists kept and read back, an empty dock the person's choice, bad, repeated and too many ids refused with nothing changed.
- **Launcher:** a URL opens in the application declaring its scheme with its URL (case of the scheme ignored); no scheme, a `file:` URL and an undeclared scheme refused; a force quit ends a process it started and refuses one it did not. An installed package's entry declares its kinds and schemes.
- **The Trash follower** (`crates/oxyos-compositor/src/trash.rs`): the shell told 0, then 1 once a file manager trashes an item (inotify); a request citing no input asks nothing; one that does shows a sheet saying how many go, one at a time; refused, nothing goes; accepted, it is emptied and the shell told 0; an empty Trash asks nothing.
- **Bloom** (OxyHQ/Bloom#571, 15.84.0): the native tooltip in the platform's popup, as a `tooltip` (its role, side, alignment, label and anchor), with no backdrop, closed by the pointer leaving and by the platform (`TooltipPlatformPopup.test.tsx`).

## Not in this decision

- **Show in Finder (Show in Dropsea), and an application dragged from Dropsea:** an OxyOS application is a package the install service keeps (ADR 0030), not a file in the person's folders. Dropsea has no Applications place to show one in or drag one from; when it has one (the registry's applications, which no application can list yet), its items are dragged as `application/x-oxyos-application` and the dock takes them as it takes Launchpad's (decision 10), and the menu's Options gains *Mostrar en Dropsea*.
- **An icon dropped on the Trash**, which a Mac's Dock also takes as removing it: dragged off the dock does it.
- **Option pressed while the menu is open:** a Mac turns Quit into Force Quit live; the popup is told key presses, not releases, so Option counts as the menu opens.
- **A full Trash icon:** one icon was given; the dock shows it in both states.
- **Opening a URL from an application** (`Linking.openURL`): the session opens URLs only for the shell, citing its press; an application's request is ADR 0028 decision 6's to come.
