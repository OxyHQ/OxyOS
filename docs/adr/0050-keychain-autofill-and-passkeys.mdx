# ADR 0050: The keychain, AutoFill and passkeys

Status: **accepted** (decisions 1 to 9, the owner's direction of 7 November 2026: "Oxy debería guardar las passwords y esas cosas en la cuenta de Oxy … bien hecho de raíz, sin tricky things"). The vault, its keys, its roster and its cryptography are [Oxy ADR 0063](https://github.com/OxyHQ/oxy/blob/main/docs/adr/0063-end-to-end-encrypted-keychain.md) ([OxyHQ/oxy#2218](https://github.com/OxyHQ/oxy/pull/2218)); this decision is OxyOS's side. Builds on [ADR 0008](./0008-input-method) (the field describes itself; text committed into it), [ADR 0010](./0010-lock-screen) (the lock), [ADR 0013](./0013-launching-applications) (launch records), [ADR 0015](./0015-application-model) (the platform supplies, the application declares), [ADR 0019](./0019-settings), [ADR 0020](./0020-oxy-account-login) (`oxyos-accountd`, the account's password, the registrar) and [ADR 0029](./0029-chromium-on-oxyos) (Astro).

## Problem

OxyOS has no password manager, no passkeys and no FIDO2. A Mac has iCloud Keychain (synced, end-to-end encrypted), AutoFill in every text field, the Passwords app, and passkeys any application or Safari can create and use, plus security keys over USB and NFC. The person's Oxy account is what all their devices share, so it is where their passwords belong (Oxy ADR 0063). This decision says who on the machine holds the vault, when it is open, what an application may ask of it, and how a credential reaches a field.

## Decision

1. **The keychain is `oxyos-accountd`'s, and the machine is a member device.**
   - The service that holds the account's credential (ADR 0020, decision 3) holds its keychain too: one service holds the account's secrets, as `securityd` does on a Mac. The logic is a crate of its own, `oxyos-keychain` (the vault's formats, the roster's verification, the local store, the access rules, sync), which the service runs; nothing else links it.
   - Each enrolled account on the machine is one member of its account's vault (Oxy ADR 0063, D3): its own Ed25519 and X25519 keys, made on the machine, never leaving it. It talks to Oxy with OxyOS's own application token (`self`, ADR 0020 step 5): the keychain routes accept it and no other application's.
   - Its state is `/var/lib/oxyos/keychain/<user>/`, root's alone (0700, files 0600, written to a new file, flushed and renamed): the member's keys, the keyring, the verified roster's head and the items' ciphertext as Oxy holds it, so the keychain works offline.

2. **The keychain is open only while the person's session is.**
   - At rest everything above is sealed under a **keybag key**: 32 random bytes, kept only wrapped by a key derived from the account's password with Argon2id (64 MiB, 3 passes, one lane, a random 16-byte salt; RustCrypto's `argon2`), as a Mac's login keychain is sealed by the login password. The yescrypt verifier in `/etc/shadow` is not that key and cannot open it.
   - **Unlocking.** The service is given the password where it already is: the login program, after PAM accepts it, sends it to the service with the account named (root's socket, as `enroll` is); the session's `check` at unlock (ADR 0020 step 5) unlocks too once the password is accepted. The keybag key and the keyring are then held in memory, locked against swap (`mlock`), overwritten when dropped.
   - **Locking.** When the session locks (ADR 0010), signs out, or its registrar's connection closes, the session tells the service and it drops the keybag key, the keyring and every decrypted item at once. While locked nothing is decrypted, suggested or signed: every request answers `locked`.
   - **A password changed elsewhere.** When the machine learns a new password (ADR 0020 decision 2: Oxy accepts one the machine does not know), the keybag is still sealed under the old one. The person is asked once for the previous password to re-seal it, as a Mac asks to update the login keychain. Without it, the machine leaves the vault and joins again (decision 4); the local copy is deleted, nothing synced is lost.

3. **The first vault, and the recovery key.** If the account has no vault, Settings › Contraseñas offers to turn the keychain on: the machine makes the vault (genesis), shows the recovery key once (eight groups of four characters), offers to print it, and asks for it back before the vault exists (Oxy ADR 0063, D6). The copy says plainly that Oxy cannot open the keychain and that without a device or the recovery key it cannot be recovered.

4. **Joining an existing vault.** If the account has a vault and this machine is not a member, Settings asks to join: by **another device's approval** (both show the same six-digit code; the person confirms on the device that approves, Oxy ADR 0063 D5) or by **the recovery key**. On a member machine, a request from another device appears as a notification ([ADR 0026](./0026-notifications)) that opens Settings' approval sheet; nothing is approved without the person confirming the code. Settings › Contraseñas › Dispositivos lists the members and removes one (with the vault key's rotation); a member whose Oxy device session ended (Oxy's device list, the account removed elsewhere) is removed by the next member that sees it.

5. **What an application may ask, decided by its launch record.** An application is the process the session registered for a desktop entry id ([ADR 0013](./0013-launching-applications), ADR 0020 step 5's pidfd registrations); the service never believes what a process says about itself. An application may:
   - **never** list, search or read the keychain, and never learn whether it holds anything;
   - be **offered** credentials in its own fields (decision 6), only those **associated** with it: items whose `apps` name its desktop entry id, or whose URLs' registrable domains are domains associated with it. An Oxy application's associated domains are its `webOrigins` in Oxy's registry (the same rows that name it for its token, Oxy ADR 0058); any other application's are declared by its package and proven by the domain (`https://<domain>/.well-known/oxyos-app-association.json` naming its id, fetched by the service, as Apple's `apple-app-site-association`);
   - **ask to save** a credential it has just used (`savePassword {username, password, domain?}`): the system shows the sheet "¿Guardar la contraseña?" with the app's name and the username; only on the person's yes is it saved, associated with that application;
   - use **passkeys** for relying-party ids associated with it (decision 7).

   Settings, as OxyOS's own application (a system registration of `so.oxy.settings`), is the one that may manage the keychain (list, read, edit, delete), and only after the person proves themselves in it (decision 8). Every other registration, and every unregistered process, is refused.

6. **AutoFill fills a field through the platform; the application sees only what is chosen.**
   - **The field says what it is for.** [ADR 0008](./0008-input-method)'s `TextInputPurpose` gains a content type (protocol version bumped, [ADR 0033](./0033-protocol-versions)): `username`, `email`, `password`, `newPassword`, `oneTimeCode`, or none. The Bloom host sets it from React Native's `textContentType`/`autoComplete` (`username`, `password`, `new-password`, `one-time-code`, `email`), the props Bloom's `TextInput` already takes, so an application declares it and changes nothing else ([ADR 0015](./0015-application-model)). Astro sets it from the page's form, as Chromium's autofill classifies fields.
   - **The suggestion is the platform's, above the field.** When such a field takes the focus, the compositor asks the service, on its registrar connection and naming the focused surface's launch record, for the suggestions of that application: titles and usernames only, never a password. If there are some, the compositor shows them in a popup of its own anchored to the field's caret rectangle (ADR 0008's positioner), as a Mac shows the key-and-name list under a login field: the application cannot draw, read or move it. ↑ ↓ and Return choose with the keyboard; Escape dismisses it; the screen reader says each one ([ADR 0014](./0014-screen-reader)).
   - **Filling.** Once the person picks one, the compositor asks the service for that item for that application (associated, unlocked, checked again) and commits the text into the field as an input method commits ([ADR 0008](./0008-input-method), decision 3): the username into the focused field and the password into the paired password field of the same window, which the host names when it sets the content type. The application receives typed text, as if the person had typed it.
   - **A new password.** A `newPassword` field is offered a strong password (decision 8's generator); choosing it fills the field and keeps the password as a pending item, saved when the application asks to save or the person confirms.
   - **Verification codes.** A `oneTimeCode` field is offered the current TOTP code of the associated login items (RFC 6238 over RustCrypto's HMAC, decision 9), filled the same way.
   - Nothing is filled without the person's pick, and nothing at all while the session is locked or the field is in a window that is not the key window.

7. **OxyOS is a platform authenticator; passkeys are keychain items.**
   - **The API.** The service answers WebAuthn's two ceremonies with CTAP2's semantics, as an authenticator: `passkey.create { rpId, rpName, user { id, name, displayName }, clientDataHash, excludeCredentials, algorithms }` and `passkey.get { rpId, clientDataHash, allowCredentials }`. ES256 (P-256, RustCrypto's `p256`) is the algorithm; attestation is `none`; the AAGUID is OxyOS's own; the flags are UP and UV, BE and BS (a synced passkey), and the signature counter 0, as iCloud Keychain's passkeys. Bloom applications reach it through the platform adapter Bloom already has for such capabilities ([ADR 0002](./0002-bloom-platform-surface-api)); the client data (origin, challenge) is the caller's to make and hash, as an authenticator never sees it.
   - **Who may use which relying party.** An application may use only an `rpId` associated with it (decision 5). **Astro**, the browser, is the one application allowed any `rpId`, because the browser itself has checked the page's origin against the `rpId` (WebAuthn's rules), as a Mac grants browsers an entitlement for it. That privilege is the system's: OxyOS lists Astro's desktop entry id as the browser in its own configuration; an application cannot declare it.
   - **The person verifies.** Every create and get shows the system's sheet (the compositor's, over the application's window): the relying party, the account name, and the account's password field (or, later, a fingerprint, [ADR 0010](./0010-lock-screen)'s PAM path). The private key signs only after that check succeeds; a create stores the new passkey item, synced to the account's other devices. The private key never leaves the service.
   - **Astro.** Chromium on OxyOS sends its WebAuthn requests to the platform, as Chromium on Windows sends them to Windows' WebAuthn API: Astro's `engine/oxyos` layer implements Chromium's authenticator for its platform over the client C ABI, so a page's passkeys are the keychain's, and the same sheet appears.
   - **Security keys.** USB and NFC security keys are reached through libfido2 (Debian's `libfido2-1`, Yubico's, the established library), by the service, not by applications: the same two requests may be answered by a key, the sheet then saying "Conecta y toca tu llave de seguridad" and asking for its PIN when the key needs one. Applications and Astro never open `/dev/hidraw*`; the service is the only process given them (a udev rule for FIDO devices). This is Windows' model: one platform API for platform and roaming authenticators. Phones as authenticators (hybrid, caBLE) are later.

8. **Settings › Contraseñas, as a Mac's Passwords.**
   - Opening it asks for the account's password (the same sheet), as the Passwords app asks for Touch ID; it stays open for five minutes of inactivity or until the session locks.
   - **List and search** (all items, Contraseñas, Llaves de acceso, Códigos, Wi-Fi, Notas), with the site's icon, title and username; **detail** with the password hidden until shown, copy (cleared from the clipboard after 90 seconds, [ADR 0007](./0007-clipboard)'s sensitive mark), the verification code counting down, the passkey's relying party; **edit**, **delete** (with undo while the sheet is open), **new**.
   - **A strong password** is generated by the service, as Safari's: 20 characters from `[a-z][A-Z][0-9]` in four groups of five joined by hyphens, at least one of each class, from the system's CSPRNG (about 100 bits).
   - **Warnings, computed on the machine:** reused passwords (the same password on several items), weak ones (zxcvbn's estimate under 10⁸ guesses), and compromised ones when the person turns on the check (the k-anonymity range API of Have I Been Pwned: only the first five hex characters of a SHA-1 are sent, never the password, never an account).
   - Settings shows the members and pending approvals (decision 4) and the recovery key's replacement.
   - Settings never holds the vault: it asks the service for what it shows, item by item, and the service decrypts.

9. **Rules that bind every step.**
   - **No hand-made cryptography.** Ed25519, X25519, XChaCha20-Poly1305, HKDF-SHA256, SHA-256, Argon2id and P-256 come from RustCrypto and dalek crates, checked against Oxy's test vectors so the machine and the SDK agree byte for byte; JCS (RFC 8785) from an established crate. TOTP (RFC 6238) is HMAC from RustCrypto under the RFC's own test vectors.
   - **Failing closed.** A roster that does not verify, a wrap from no member, a counter that goes back, a keybag that does not open, an application with no registration, a session that cannot say it is unlocked: each refuses and says so; none falls back to something weaker.
   - **No secret in a log, a scenario, an error or `Debug`**: passwords, keys, codes, ciphertext, recovery keys and the items' names and usernames. Secrets are overwritten when dropped, as `oxyos-account`'s are.
   - **The person decides each disclosure**: nothing is filled, saved, approved, signed or shown without their act in a surface only the system draws.

## Steps

1. **`oxyos-keychain`'s core**: the vault's formats (Oxy ADR 0063 D8) checked against Oxy's vectors; the roster's verification; the keyring; the item codec; the keybag sealed by the password; the local store; the generator; TOTP. No socket yet. Tested without Oxy and without root.

   *Done.* `crates/oxyos-keychain`, a library alone: the seal, member and recovery keys (the recovery key's text read as the SDK reads it: case, hyphens and white space ignored, `I`/`L` as 1, `O` as 0), the keyring, wraps, the roster's verifier, the enrollment's commitment and code, the items, the strong-password generator, TOTP and the keybag (Argon2id at 64 MiB, 3 passes, 1 lane) with the store it opens. Its rules are the SDK's (`@oxy.so/core`'s `crypto/keychain`): closed schemas read only in their JCS form, timestamps as `toISOString` writes them, public keys of small order refused, one signature on every entry after the genesis, an item's every field present. Beyond the SDK, a wrap signed by the recovery key opens only for a member the recovery key admitted, and only for the recipient whose keys the roster names. The SDK's vectors (`tests/vectors/oxy-keychain-v1.json`, from OxyHQ/oxy `fd06e237`) are reproduced byte for byte: every key, seal, keyring, wrap, roster entry and signature, enrollment code, padded item and its ciphertext, TOTP code, and every rejection for the SDK's reason. Beside them, RFC 7748, 8032, 6238, 4226 and 9106's vectors, every tamper and fork, the keybag refusing a wrong password and weaker parameters, the store refusing a directory or a file others can reach, a link or a second name, and no key, password, code, name or ciphertext in `Debug`. Locking the keys in memory (`mlock`) comes with the service (step 2).
2. **The service's side**: vault creation, joining by approval and by recovery key, sync, rotation on removal, unlock and lock wired to the login program and the registrar, the access rules of decision 5 on the session socket. Tested on real sockets with registered processes, as ADR 0020 step 5.
3. **AutoFill**: the content type in the protocol and the Bloom host, the compositor's suggestion popup and its fill, the save sheet. Tested in the development compositor's scenarios, with a fixture keychain.
4. **Settings › Contraseñas** (decision 8).
5. **Passkeys**: the platform authenticator and its sheet, Bloom's adapter, then Astro's authenticator in `engine/oxyos`, then security keys through libfido2.

## Not in this decision

- Sharing passwords with other people, and organization vaults (Oxy ADR 0063 D9).
- A fingerprint for user verification: it comes with fingerprint support for unlocking (ADR 0010).
- Passkeys on a phone used from OxyOS (hybrid transport) and OxyOS used as a phone's authenticator.
- Importing from other password managers (CSV, Apple's and Chrome's exports): a Settings feature for later.
- Sandboxing applications: until it exists, Yama's scope 1 is what keeps one application from tracing another or the session (ADR 0020 step 5); the keychain adds no new trust in applications, since they receive only what the person chose for them.
