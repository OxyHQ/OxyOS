# ADR 0060: Login items and background activity

Status: **accepted; implemented** (8 November 2026, at the platform author's request to close macOS's parity here, "bien hecho de raíz"). Amended by [ADR 0066](./0066-quality-of-service) (10 November 2026), decisions 4 and 5: a process running on in the background is hidden to the session, so its host naps (nothing the display paces runs, its timers' slack long) and its scope is in the CPU's idle class with a low disk weight; [below](#running-in-the-background-and-the-nap). Builds on [ADR 0013](./0013-launching-applications) (launch records), [ADR 0019](./0019-settings) (the session the one writer of the preferences, Settings and its grant), [ADR 0026](./0026-notifications), [ADR 0030](./0030-installing-applications) (capabilities, decision 8; the account's decisions kept by `oxyos-installd`), [ADR 0033](./0033-protocol-versions), [ADR 0034](./0034-the-dock) (Open at Login, decision 5) and [ADR 0058](./0058-the-platform-api) (the platform API's shape). It amends ADR 0013, ADR 0030 and ADR 0034 where they say so.

## Problem

OxyOS opened applications at login only as the dock's menu chose them (ADR 0034: Options › Open at Login, the session's `open_at_login`), and nothing else could see or change that list. ADR 0030 declared two capabilities, `background` and `login-item` ("Running without a window, or at sign-in", *Asked*), and the package format, the install sheet and Settings' Privacy page carried them, but nothing gave them a meaning:

- **no application could run without a window.** One process is one window (ADR 0013, ADR 0034, decision 2: a Bloom application whose last window closes ends its process), so a chat closed by the person stopped receiving its messages;
- **no application could add itself to Open at Login**, nor ask whether it was there;
- **Privacy's switches for them changed nothing**: there was nothing to turn off.

## What a Mac does

System Settings › General › **Login Items & Extensions** has three parts. *Open at Login*: the applications opened as the person logs in, each with its kind, added with + (an application chooser) and removed with −; the Dock's Options › Open at Login is the same list. *App Background Activity* (*Allow in the Background* before macOS 15): the items applications registered to run in the background (launch agents, helper login items), each with a switch; turned off, the item is booted out at once (`launchctl bootout`) and stays off. *Extensions*, by kind.

An application manages its own through `SMAppService` (macOS 13): `mainApp` (itself, opened at login, into the same Open at Login list), `agent(plistName:)` and `loginItem(identifier:)` (helpers that run in the background), each with `register()`, `unregister()` and `status` (`notRegistered`, `enabled`, `requiresApproval`, `notFound`). Registering asks nothing of the person: the system posts a notification, *Background Items Added: "App" added items that can run in the background. You can manage this in Login Items & Extensions.*, which opens that page; an item the person turned off is `requiresApproval`, and registering it again does not turn it back on.

## Decision

1. **One list of login items: `open_at_login`.** Settings' Open at Login and the dock's Options › Open at Login are one list, the session's (ADR 0034, decision 5). Settings changes it as the dock does (`SetApplicationList` with `OpenAtLogin`, now taken from the `settings` grant as from `shell`), and the session tells both every change (`ApplicationList`), so each shows the other's at once. Settings keeps no list of its own. An application that adds itself (decision 3) adds itself to this list.

2. **An application has two kinds of background item, as `SMAppService` gives a Mac's:**
   - **its login item**: itself, opened at login, its place in `open_at_login` (`mainApp`'s);
   - **its background activity**: its process running on once its window closes. A Mac's helpers are executables of their own; a store package has no native code (ADR 0030, decision 1) and every process of an application is its host running its JavaScript, so on OxyOS the application's work in the background is its own JavaScript going on without a window, as a Mac's application with no window open goes on running. Registered and allowed, the Bloom host, asked to close its window (the close button, Quit, Super+W), closes the window and keeps running: its timers, its network and its notifications go on, its React tree stays mounted and is never drawn (as iOS keeps a backgrounded application's views), and React Native's `AppState` becomes `background` (`appStateDidChange`). One process of an application runs in the background at a time; a second one whose window closes ends, as any does. It ends when it ends itself, unregisters, is turned off (decision 5) or the session ends. **Quit ends it**: closing an application's window keeps it running, as on a Mac, but the person quitting it (the dock's Quit, the switcher's, Super+Q, which ask its windows to close) ends its process as they close: the scene tells the program whose application was quit (`take_quits`), and the session ends the process instead of keeping it. A quit the application refused (its unsaved changes) still stands when its window closes later. Background activity does not start anything at login: what opens at login is a login item.

3. **The session decides, by the launch record and the capability; the client's claim is nothing.** An application asks with `BackgroundItem` (decision 8): how one of its items stands, registering it or unregistering it. The program running the compositor (`oxyos_compositor::background`, the session's and the development compositor's) answers with `BackgroundItemState`, whose statuses are `SMAppService.Status`'s:
   - **a process the session did not launch**, and **a development entry** (unverified, ADR 0030, decision 7: no capability), have none: `notPermitted`. The person may still put any application in Open at Login;
   - **a system application** of the image's policy has both (what the image ships is trusted);
   - **an installed application** has what it declared (`login-item`, `background`), as its account decided: granted, it registers; turned off (`denied`), `requiresApproval`, and nothing changes; **declared and never decided, it is granted as it registers**: the session asks `oxyos-installd` to keep the decision (`decide`, as Settings does) and registers once it is kept. Not declared: `notPermitted`.
   - **in Safe Mode** ([ADR 0054](./0054-recovery-and-erasing), decision 10, added 8 November 2026), an installed application's background activity is `notPermitted` for the whole start, whatever its account decided (its process ends with its window), and the session opens nothing at login; a system application's is as always. Nothing is written: the decisions and the list stand for the next normal start.

   This amends ADR 0030, decision 8, for these two capabilities: they are not asked in a sheet. A sheet at an application's first registration would ask the person to judge what they have not seen run; opening at login and running in the background are visible and reversible, so, as a Mac, the system tells the person afterwards (decision 4) and they turn it off where they see it (decision 6).

4. **The person is told once, by the session's notification, which opens Login Items & Extensions.** When an application adds something, the session posts, as Settings (ADR 0057's system notifications), *Background Items Added* — "“Chat” added an item that can run in the background. You can manage this in Login Items & Extensions." (*Ítems en segundo plano añadidos* — "«Chat» ha añadido un ítem que puede ejecutarse en segundo plano. Puedes gestionarlo en Ítems de inicio y extensiones."), in the session's language, without a sound. It is posted only for something new: a login item the application put in the list (not one already there), and background activity granted as it registered (not a later process's registration, nor a system application's, which is the system's own, as a Mac does not tell of its own). One a time per application (`background-items:ID`, a second replacing the first). Pressed, it opens `oxyos-settings://login-items`: a notification the session posts may name a URL to open (never an application's), and the scene, pressed in the shell citing the shell's own input, asks to open it as the shell's own `OpenUrl` (serial 0, which answers no request) instead of activating Settings; Settings opens at the page, as any `oxyos-settings://` URL opens it (ADR 0019).

5. **Turned off, it applies at once.** The person's switch is the application's `background` decision, the account's one record, `oxyos-installd`'s (the same Privacy shows, ADR 0030, decision 8). When the service has kept a change, the session applies it in the same turn:
   - **turned off**: the application's process running in the background **is ended** (SIGTERM, from the launcher that started it), as a Mac boots its agent out; it has no window, so the person loses nothing they see, and leaving it running would make the switch a lie. Its processes with windows **lose the right**: each is told `requiresApproval` and ends as its window closes, as any application's does; a window the person is using is theirs, and ending it would lose what they were doing. Registering again answers `requiresApproval`;
   - **turned on**: allowed again; nothing is started (a process ended stays ended), and the application's processes that had registered are told `enabled`.

   Privacy's "a change takes effect the next time the app opens" no longer holds for `background`: it takes effect at once, wherever it is changed. The person removing an application from Open at Login (the dock's menu, Settings' −) changes its login item too: its processes are told `notRegistered`. An application may add itself again, and the person is told again; to keep it from doing so they turn its `login-item` off in Privacy (`requiresApproval`).

6. **Settings › General › Login Items & Extensions** (`apps/settings/src/LoginItemsPage.tsx`, `login-items`, `oxyos-settings://login-items`, after Date & Time), drawn with Bloom:
   - **Open at Login**: the list, each row its application's name and kind (*Application*), selectable as Keyboard's Input Sources list is (Bloom's `ChoiceList`); **+** opens a `Dialog` choosing among the registry's applications not in the list, **−** removes the one selected (named "Remove Chat from Open at Login"), the keyboard moving to + when − goes disabled; each change said once the session's list shows it;
   - **App Background Activity**: each installed application whose `background` was decided (it registered, or the person decided it), by name, saying *Running in the background*, *Allowed in the background* or *Not allowed in the background*, with a switch named "Allow Chat in the background". Its source is the account's applications as the session tells Settings (`privacy`), each now saying whether a process of it runs in the background (`runningInBackground`), told again unasked (serial 0) when an application was granted as it registered or when what runs in the background changed;
   - **no Extensions section**: OxyOS has no third-party extension point yet. The page keeps a Mac's name, and its section comes with the first one.

   It is searchable (`apps/settings/searchable.json`: login items, open at login, startup, background activity…), in English and Spanish.

7. **What an application calls**: `@oxyos/platform`'s `application` framework (ADR 0058, decision 1), its first part, in ADR 0058's shape: `application.loginItem` and `application.backgroundActivity` (`oxyos/platform/src/application.ts`), each `available`, `status()`, `register()`, `unregister()` (each taking `{signal}`) and a `Followed` of how it stands (`current()`, `subscribe(listener)`, asked once as its first follower subscribes, then told by every answer and by the person's changes; `useFollowed` for a view). `register()` resolves `'enabled'` or rejects with a `PlatformError` (`domain` `'application'`; `notPermitted`, with `reason` `'requiresApproval'` when the person turned it off; `failed`; `unavailable`; `cancelled`). The Bloom host's `OxyOSApplication` asks (`backgroundItem(kind, action)`, its serial) and tells `OxyOSBackgroundItemState`; `AppState` follows the window. Settings follows the account's applications through the same package (`privacy`, told unasked as decision 6 says).

8. **Protocol version 33** ([ADR 0033](./0033-protocol-versions)), added only:

   | Direction | Opcode | Message |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | Request | 165 | `BackgroundItem { serial, kind, action }` (an application): `kind` `LoginItem` or `Background`, `action` `Status`, `Register` or `Unregister` |
   | Event | 165 | `BackgroundItemState { serial, kind, status }`: `status` `NotRegistered`, `Enabled`, `RequiresApproval` or `NotPermitted`; `serial` 0 unasked, the person's change |

   The client library refuses the request to a compositor of version 32, and the event is never sent to a client of version 32. In the C ABI: `oxyos_client_background_item`, `OXYOS_EVENT_BACKGROUND_ITEM_STATE`, `OXYOS_BACKGROUND_ITEM_*` and `OXYOS_BACKGROUND_*`.

### Running in the background and the nap

*Amended by ADR 0066 (decisions 4 and 5), 10 November 2026.* A process running on without its window (decision 2) is told it is hidden (`AppVisibility`), as a minimized one is: its host naps (requestAnimationFrame, Animated's and Reanimated's frames wait; its timers' slack is 50 ms), and AppState stays `background`, as decision 2 already said it. The session gives its scope (ADR 0066, decision 3) the CPU's idle class (`CPUWeight=idle`) and a disk weight of 10, as a Mac's background daemons are `DARWIN_BG`: its JavaScript, its network and its notifications go on, on whatever the person's applications leave. Opened again (a window of it shown), it is weighed as any application at once.

## Steps

1. **The protocol and the scene.** *Done.* Version 33's messages; the scene takes any client's ask and hands it to the program (`take_background_item_requests`), tells only a client of version 33 an answer, forgets a client's asks when it goes; Open at Login changed and told by `shell` or `settings` (`list_holder`); the session's notification that opens a URL (`post_system_notification_opening`, `open_url_for_shell`).
2. **The session's side.** *Done.* `oxyos_compositor::background::BackgroundActivity`: the asks decided as decision 3 says; a login item kept in the preferences (`Settings::set_open_at_login`) and the application told when the person changed it; a grant at registration asked of the install service (`Installs::grant`, `take_grants`) and the person told; the person's decisions applied as decision 5 says (`Installs::take_decisions`); the processes that registered watched, only while there are any, and one whose window closed kept in the background or ended. `oxyos-session` and the development compositor serve it after Settings' changes; the session's ends go through its launcher (`terminate`).
3. **The host and the API.** *Done.* `Window::backgroundItem` and `closeToplevel` (the window destroyed, its last size kept for what reads it, never presented again, a configure sent before the compositor read its going ignored, and nothing asked of it afterwards: its menus, text fields, announcements, accessibility, pointer and popups, any of which the compositor would end the connection over); the close that keeps running; `AppState`; `OxyOSApplication.backgroundItem`; `@oxyos/platform`'s `application` framework, and the session's `privacy` followed.
4. **Settings.** *Done.* The page of decision 6, `open_at_login` among its preferences as the list the session tells it (`window.cpp`'s `kOpenAtLogin`, as `notification_sounds_off`), the account's applications followed (`privacy`).

## Tests

- **Protocol** (`oxyos-protocol`): version 33's messages only version 33's both ways; the kinds, actions and statuses closed sets; the documentation's table.
- **C ABI** (`oxyos-client-ffi`): the constants are the header's, the event converts, an unknown kind or action sends nothing, an ask crosses whole.
- **Scene** (`scene/tests/background.rs`, `scene/tests/notifications.rs`): any client's ask handed to the program, in order, the scene answering none itself; the answer and an unasked change to the asker alone; nothing told a client of version 32 or one gone, its asks forgotten; past 32 waiting, refused; Open at Login changed by the shell and by Settings, never the dock by Settings nor either by an application, told to both and to a Settings that connects later; the session's notification that opens a URL opening it once as the shell's own, for the shell's own press only, Settings neither activated nor started nor given a response, the shell told no launch result.
- **The session's side** (`oxyos-compositor`'s `background::tests`): a process not launched and a development entry have nothing; a system application adds itself to Open at Login once, the person told once, unregistering takes it out; the person changing the list tells only the application whose place changed, once; an installed application granted as it registers (the service asked once, the person told once, a later process's registration asking and telling nothing); one that did not declare it, or that the person turned off, refused and nobody told; its window closed, one process runs in the background and a second ends; unregistering in the background ends it; turned off, the one in the background ends at once and those with windows are told and end as their windows close.
- **Settings** (`apps/settings/tests/login-items.test.ts`, `model.test.ts`, `spotlight.test.ts`): the list's rows named by the registry, what + offers, a change as the whole list, the selection moving on; App Background Activity's rows; the words in both languages; `open_at_login` among the preferences; the page searchable.
- **Scenarios** (`login-items.json` at 1 in English, `login-items-scaled.json` at 1.25 in Spanish), over a real `oxyos-installd` with a probe installed (`so.oxy.backgroundprobe`, "Chat", declaring both capabilities, `sample/BackgroundProbe.tsx`) and Settings at its page: the probe opened from the dock adds itself to Open at Login (Settings' list shows it at once, the person told once), registers its background activity (the decision kept, told once more), its window closes and it runs in the background (Settings says so), the person turns it off (the session ends it at once, the step's `background` result says so), and removes it from Open at Login with −, the dock's menu showing Open at Login unchecked; the keyboard's focus and what every client received checked at each step. `login-items-idle.json`: with an application in the background and Settings showing it, nothing wakes.

## Not in this decision

- **Starting an application in the background at login** (a Mac's agent with `RunAtLoad`): a login item opens with its window; one allowed in the background goes there when its window closes. A host started without a window is its own decision.
- **Reopening the process in the background** when the person opens the application again: the dock starts a new process with a window, as it does for an application with no window (ADR 0013), and the one in the background goes on; a Mac reopens the running one.
- **Extensions** (decision 6) and third-party extension points.
- **A login item hidden at login** (a Mac's former *Hide* checkbox).
- **Confinement**: the session ends a process that loses the right; until applications are confined (ADR 0030, decision 15), a native program of the user's own could run anything, as it can today.
