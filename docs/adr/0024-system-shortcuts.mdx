# ADR 0024: System shortcuts

Status: **accepted; implemented** (the scene, the protocol, the C ABI, the Bloom host and the shell; screenshots, decision 10, in the compositor, the session and the development compositor too). Builds on [ADR 0009](./0009-session-shell) (the shell and its `shortcuts` grant), [ADR 0010](./0010-lock-screen) (the lock and Ctrl+Super+Q), [ADR 0014](./0014-screen-reader) (keys taken before everything else), [ADR 0016](./0016-windows) (windows: minimize, zoom, the close request, the active window), [ADR 0018](./0018-several-displays) (a shell per display) and [ADR 0021](./0021-menu-bar) (applications' own shortcuts).

## Problem

A Mac is driven from the keyboard: ⌘Tab switches applications, ⌘W closes a window, ⌘Q quits, ⌘M minimizes, ⌘H hides, ⌘` goes through an application's windows, Ctrl+⌘F is full screen, ⌘Space searches. OxyOS had three of the system's own: Ctrl+Super+Q locks (ADR 0010), Super+F5 turns the screen reader on (ADR 0014), Ctrl+Alt+Fn and Ctrl+Alt+Backspace (the keymap's). There was no application switcher, and nothing to close, quit, minimize or hide from the keyboard.

The scene had a mechanism for the shell's shortcuts (`Scene::set_shortcuts`): raw key codes, without modifiers, forwarded as keys to one shell surface. No program ever set it, the shell's sockets did not grant `shortcuts`, and a key code alone cannot say Super+Tab.

Applications already have their shortcuts: Bloom's chords, `Mod+N`, where `Mod` is Ctrl on OxyOS (ADR 0021). Whatever the system takes must never meet them.

## Decision

1. **Super is the system's ⌘; Ctrl is the applications'.** Super, and every key pressed while Super is held, belongs to the system: no application and no input method receives the press, its release, or the text it would type, even a key released after Super. A key pressed before Super (Ctrl, say) is the application's, press and release. Applications' chords are Ctrl's (ADR 0021), so the two can never collide; Super with a key that is no shortcut does nothing, and reaches nobody.

2. **The shortcuts.**

   | Keys | What happens | Who |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | Super+Tab | Opens the application switcher on the application used before the active one; again, while Super is held, the next one. Super released at once switches straight back to it, with nothing shown, as a quick ⌘Tab. | Shell (`SwitcherNext`) |
   | Super+Shift+Tab | Opens it on the last application; while held, the previous one. | Shell (`SwitcherPrevious`) |
   | In the switcher: Tab, Shift+Tab, \`, Shift+\`, ← → | Next, previous, previous, next, previous, next. | Shell |
   | In the switcher: Q, H | Quits, hides the application it has chosen; the switcher stays. | Shell (`SwitcherQuit`, `SwitcherHide`) |
   | In the switcher: Esc | Closes it without a choice; Super still held, Tab opens it again. | Shell (`SwitcherCancel`) |
   | Super released | Activates the switcher's choice: its window used last, shown (restored, or brought back with its application). | Shell (`SwitcherDone`) |
   | Super+W | The active window's close request, as its close bubble's: the application may ask first or refuse. | Compositor |
   | Super+Q | Quits the active application: the close request to each of its windows, on any workspace. | Compositor |
   | Super+M | Minimizes the active window into the dock. | Compositor |
   | Super+H | Hides the active application: its windows leave the screen, not into the dock, until it is activated. | Compositor |
   | Super+\` (Super+Shift+\`) | The active application's next (previous) window on this workspace. Forwards, the active one goes behind the others, so presses go through them all. | Compositor |
   | Super+Ctrl+F | Full screen on and off for the active window. | Compositor |
   | Super+Space | The launcher with its search focused (Spotlight's place); again, it closes. | Shell (`Search`) |
   | Super+F2 | The keyboard to the top bar's menus, as a Mac's Control-F2 (decision 9). | Shell (`MenuBar`) |
   | Ctrl+Super+Q, Super+F5, Ctrl+Alt+Fn, Ctrl+Alt+Backspace | Unchanged: the keymap's own (`oxyos-input`), before the scene. | Session |

   An application is the windows the shell shows as one (ADR 0013): those of a process the session started for a registry entry, or claiming the same application id, or, claiming none, its client's alone.

   Screenshots, Super+Shift+3, 4 and 5, are decision 10's.

   **Not in this decision:** Super+Alt+H (hide the others), Super+Alt+M (minimize all), Super+Alt+Esc (force quit), Mission Control and Launchpad keys, tiling, and a window's own minimize-all or close-all variants.

3. **Keys by their place.** The scene has no keymap: keys are Linux key codes and are matched by place, named as on the US layout, as Bloom matches applications' chords (ADR 0021). So the two can never meet on any layout, and \` is the key above Tab, left of 1 (º on the Spanish layout), as a Mac's ⌘\` is on most ISO keyboards. On a layout that moves letters (AZERTY), Super+W is the key where W is on a US keyboard; when the compositor's keymap reaches the scene, system shortcuts and Bloom's chords move to the layout's characters together.

4. **The compositor owns what acts on windows.** Close, quit, minimize, hide, the application's windows and full screen are the scene's own (`scene/shortcuts.rs`), so they work whatever the shell is doing, stuck or gone, as a Mac's window server's do. They act on the active window (ADR 0016); with none active (nothing open, or the shell's overlay has the keyboard) they do nothing, and their keys still reach nobody. A menu open in a window is dismissed first, as a press elsewhere dismisses it.

5. **The shell owns what needs a picture,** through its `shortcuts` grant, which the session gives its shell sockets with `shell` and `lock`. The compositor sends `SystemShortcut` (event 100: the shortcut, a serial, the output) to the shell of the active window's output (else the pointer's; else any shell holding the grant), and the whole switcher (Super+Tab to Super's release) to that one shell. The serial is a user input of the shell's, as a key press is. The switcher's keys are decided by the compositor; the shell only shows and acts: its overlay never takes the keyboard, so the active application stays active while it is shown, and a stuck shell cannot hold Super's keys.
   - **The switcher** (`AppSwitcher`, `switcher.ts`): the running applications' icons in a row, the most recently used first, the chosen one framed and named. Its order comes from the compositor: `WindowUse` (event 101) tells each window's latest activation (a rising count, 0 never) and whether it is hidden with its application. Activating an application focuses its window used last; the compositor restores it, or brings back every window hidden with it.
   - **The search:** Super+Space opens the launcher (Launchpad) and focuses its search field. A shell overlay takes no keyboard when it appears; the shell asks for it with `FocusShellSurface` (request 112), citing the shortcut's serial, and the compositor gives it only to the shell's own panel or overlay, only for an input the shell received lately.
   - **Quit and hide by the shell:** `HideApplication` (110) and `QuitApplication` (111), by a window, which the switcher's Q and H and the application menu's Hide and Quit (ADR 0021) use, so the menu's Hide now hides the application and Quit asks every window of it.

6. **Precedence**, first to last: the keymap's own (a terminal switch, the end of the session, the lock's Ctrl+Super+Q, the reader's Super+F5, before the scene sees a key); the screen reader's keys (ADR 0014; Ctrl+Alt with an arrow or Space, Super held or not); the system shortcuts; the input method (ADR 0008); the application, where Bloom's chords come before the focused field (ADR 0021). The input method never sees Super or a key pressed with it, so Super+Space is search, never an input source switch.

7. **Locked, none acts.** While the session is locked no system shortcut acts and their keys reach nobody: not a letter of Super+W goes into the password. The lock's own keys are its: what is typed without Super, the reader's, and the keymap's. A switcher open when the session locks is cancelled.

8. **Key repeat.** The compositor sends no repeats (libinput has none, and neither does the scene): a shortcut acts once per press. When key repeat comes, Tab and \` in the switcher and Super+\` repeat while held, as a Mac's do; the others never.

9. **The menu bar from the keyboard** (amended: [ADR 0021](./0021-menu-bar)'s bar had no keyboard access). A Mac's Control-F2 moves the keyboard to its menu bar. On OxyOS it is **Super+F2**, not Ctrl+F2: Ctrl's chords are the applications' (decision 1), and an application may take Ctrl+F2 for itself, while the F2 key and the gesture are a Mac's. The compositor sends `MenuBar` (wire value 8) to the shell of the active window's output, as it sends `Search`, a menu open in a window closing first; the shell takes the keyboard for its top bar with `FocusShellSurface`, citing that shortcut, and the window stays the active one, its menus on the bar (ADR 0021). Then, as on a Mac:
   - the keyboard is on the bar's first menu, the Oxy menu (a Mac's Apple menu), its title highlighted and the assistive technology's focus on it;
   - Left and Right move to the previous and the next menu (round the ends: the Oxy menu, the application's own, then the window's), the next one opening if one is open;
   - Down, Return or Space open the menu; inside it Up, Down, Home, End, a letter (typeahead), Return and Escape are Bloom's popup navigation, as in any menu ([ADR 0021](./0021-menu-bar));
   - a row chosen gives the keyboard back to the window, then runs (a window's command reaches its application once);
   - Escape in an open menu closes it, the keyboard staying on its title; Escape on the bar leaves it and gives the keyboard back to the window (`FocusWindow` of the active window: its focused field keeps its focus, as after any focus change);
   - a press elsewhere (a window, the desktop) takes the keyboard from the bar, and the bar lets go of it.

   Bloom's popups begin with no row active, so the first Down after opening a menu makes its first row active; a Mac's menu opened from the keyboard begins on its first item. That is Bloom's to give (an initial active row for a popup opened from the keyboard, `floating/popup-navigation.tsx`), and OxyOS takes it when Bloom has it.

10. **Screenshots, as a Mac takes them** (amended: decision 2 left them out). Super+Shift+3, 4 and 5 are ⌘⇧3, 4 and 5 with Super as ⌘ (decision 1), so no application ever meets them.
   - **Super+Shift+3: every display, each its own file**, as a Mac saves one file per display. It is the compositor's own (`Scene::capture_screens`), as its window actions are (decision 4): it works whatever the shell is doing. An interface open (below) ends first.
   - **Super+Shift+4: the crosshair.** The compositor tells the shell of every display (`CaptureMode` `Selection`, event 200, the same serial to each), and each covers its display with an overlay showing the crosshair pointer: a drag draws the rectangle with its size in the display's pixels beside it, and its release asks for that rectangle (`Capture` `Area`, request 200, in global logical pixels on its display; a rectangle across a display's edge is cut to that display). **Space** switches to picking a window (`CaptureMode` `Window`) and back: the compositor tells the shells the window under the pointer as it moves onto another (`CaptureTarget`, event 201: its number and rectangle, never its pixels), the shell tints it, and a press asks for it (`Capture` `Window`). A press over no window does nothing.
   - **Super+Shift+5: the toolbar**, on the shell of the display in front (the active window's, else the pointer's), as `search` is (decision 5): its three captures (the entire screen, a selected window, a selected portion, which starts as the middle of the display and is dragged anew), its options (save to the Desktop or Documents; a timer of none, 5 or 10 seconds, the toolbar hiding behind a countdown), a close button (`EndCapture`, request 201) and Capturar; **Return** is Capturar (`CaptureMode` `Confirm`). The folder chosen goes with every capture and Super+Shift+3 uses it too, until the session ends. Screen recording, a Mac's other half of ⌘⇧5, is out of this decision.
   - **The interface's keys are the system's.** From Super+Shift+4 or 5 until it ends, every key is taken before the input method and the application (`system_key`), press and release: Escape ends it everywhere (`CaptureMode` `Ended`), Space and Return act as above, anything else does nothing; of Super's shortcuts only Super+Shift+3, 4 and 5 act (each replacing the interface). Its overlays never take the keyboard, and a press on them moves no focus: the active application stays active, its field focused, as during a Mac's ⌘⇧4. A capture asked for ends the interface on every display, and a shell may only ask citing the serial of the interface it was told to show, while it is open.
   - **What is captured: the frame the displays show.** The compositor composes a capture with its next frame, from that frame's own layers (`Compositor::compose_captures`), so it is that frame pixel for pixel: each display at its own pixels (a 1280 × 800 display at 1.25 is 1600 × 1000), on the CPU, the reference (ADR 0004), or on the GPU where only the GPU composes (a session's, ADR 0031: the CPU then has no pixels of clients' GPU buffers). An area is its display's capture cut to the pixels the rectangle covers, its edges rounded as the display places them. A window is its own layers alone, with its shadow, popups and dialogs, at its display's scale, over nothing: frames are opaque, so it is composed twice, over black and over white, and each pixel's opacity is what the two differ by (source over is affine in the background), exact where the window is opaque or absent and within a level in its shadow's edge. The pointer is on its display's cursor plane, never in a frame, so it is never captured.
   - **What is never captured.** Locked, nothing (decision 7): the shortcuts do nothing, the lock ends an open interface and drops a capture not yet composed, a capture is never composed while locked or at a login screen, and a lock surface is never in a capture. A shell surface the shell leaves out (`ExcludeFromCapture`, request 202, shell surfaces only): the screenshot interface and the thumbnail, so a capture never shows its own picker. Any client's **secure regions** (`ClearSecureRegions` and `AddSecureRegion`, requests 203 and 204, at most 32, going with the surface's next frame as window views do, ADR 0022): drawn opaque black in every capture; the Bloom host sends every `secureTextEntry` field's rectangle, so a password field is black in a screenshot as on a Mac a protected window is, even typing into it.
   - **The file, written by the session as its user.** The compositor hands each capture to the session's writer (`screenshots::Writer`), which encodes and writes it on a thread of its own, never in the compositor's turn: a PNG (RGB; a window's RGBA, its colours no longer premultiplied), sRGB, its density the display's (72 dots an inch at 1, 90 at 1.25, as a Mac writes a Retina display's 144). Into the Desktop (or Documents) where xdg-user-dirs keeps it (`$XDG_CONFIG_HOME/user-dirs.dirs`, else `~/Desktop`), made if it is missing. Named as a Mac names it, in the session's language: "Screenshot 2026-10-31 at 10.04.05.png", "Captura de pantalla 2026-10-31 a las 10.04.05.png" (local time, 24 hours), the second display's "… (2).png". Written whole under a hidden name, synced, then linked to its name, which never replaces a file: a name taken (two captures in a second) goes on to the next number. The shutter sounds as the capture is taken when the system has the sound theme's `screen-capture` (OxyOS's theme, else freedesktop.org's, through PipeWire's `pw-play`); the image ships no sound theme yet, so today it is silent.
   - **The thumbnail.** Once a file is written, the compositor tells the shell of its display (`ScreenshotSaved`, event 202: the session's number for it, its display, its size and its path), which shows it in the bottom-right corner (above the dock where there is one), sliding in, for five seconds, and the screen reader says "Captura de pantalla guardada", as VoiceOver says a Mac's. A press opens it (`OpenScreenshot`, request 205, citing that press): the session opens the file it wrote, never a path the shell names, in the application whose entry declares `image/png` (`MimeType`), else the file manager, Stash; started with the file in its launch record and given it as React Native's `Linking` initial URL (`file://`), the launch half of [ADR 0028](./0028-file-access)'s decision 6. Dragging it moves it, and a drag off to the right sends it away, as a Mac's. Dropping it into an application, a Mac's other use of the drag, needs a drag-and-drop protocol OxyOS does not have.
   - **The protocol is version 5** ([ADR 0033](./0033-protocol-versions)): opcodes 200 to 205 both ways, `CaptureMode` sent unasked, so never to a client of version 4 or older. The interface is a new event, not new values of `SystemShortcut`: an older shell would refuse a value its version did not have.

## Results

- **Protocol:** `SystemShortcut` (event 100) with the `SystemShortcut` wire enum (`SwitcherNext`, `SwitcherPrevious`, `SwitcherQuit`, `SwitcherHide`, `SwitcherDone`, `SwitcherCancel`, `Search`), `WindowUse` (101), `HideApplication` (request 110), `QuitApplication` (111) and `FocusShellSurface` (112). The C ABI: `OXYOS_EVENT_SYSTEM_SHORTCUT`, `OXYOS_SHORTCUT_*`, `OXYOS_EVENT_WINDOW_USE`, `OXYOS_MANAGE_HIDE_APPLICATION`, `OXYOS_MANAGE_QUIT_APPLICATION` and `oxyos_client_focus_shell_surface`.
- **The scene** (`scene/shortcuts.rs`): `Scene::key` and `Scene::keyboard` ask `system_key` after the reader and before the input method; it keeps the modifiers held and the keys it took, so their releases are taken too, and the switcher's shell and output. Windows count their activations (`update_active`), are hidden with their application (`conceal`, the minimize's path with a `Gone` appearance) and shown again by `restore` (`Arrived`). `SetFullscreen`'s body is `set_fullscreen`, which Super+Ctrl+F uses. `Scene::set_shortcuts` and the shell's shortcut target are gone: no program used them.
- **The Bloom host:** `OxyOSShellShortcut` events to JavaScript, in order; `OxyOSShell.hideApplication` and `quitApplication`; the state's windows carry `used` and `hidden`; a text field JavaScript focuses in a shell region asks for its surface's keyboard, once that surface is on the screen, citing the latest input.
- **The shell** (`apps/shell`): `AppSwitcher` (Bloom's `Button` and `Text`; the row a `list` named "Cambiar de aplicación", each icon a button named by its application, the chosen one selected and announced to the screen reader; a press on an icon activates it), `switcher.ts` (the order, opening, moving and following applications that quit while it is open), `Shell`'s `commands`, every one handled once and in order (a quick Super+Tab sends the switcher's opening and its end together), and Launchpad's `searchRequest`.
- **The menu bar from the keyboard** (decision 9): `SystemShortcut::MenuBar` and `OXYOS_SHORTCUT_MENU_BAR`; the scene's Super+F2; the Bloom host's shell regions take a `keyboard` prop (`OxyOSShellSurface`): the host asks for the region's surface citing the latest input, and tells JavaScript (`OxyOSShellKeyboardLost`) when the keyboard, once it had it, left the region and its popups; the shell's `TopBar` drives its menus from the platform's keys (`menu-bar-keys.ts`) and gives the keyboard back through `returnKeyboard` (the active window's `focusWindow`). Two rules made it work, both general: an input in a popup that closes counts as its parent's from then on (the scene, `remove_node`), so Right on an open menu may open the next one citing that key, which the grab rule refused as the input of a surface gone; and the shell's `FocusWindow` while a menu of its own is open gives the window the keyboard as that menu closes, not the bar the menu came from. The host closes the popups gone before opening new ones, so a menu replaced in one commit opens with the old grab ended. Tested on the scene (`super_f2_moves_the_keyboard_to_the_top_bar_and_the_shell_gives_it_back`, `a_key_on_an_open_menu_opens_its_sibling_once_it_closed`), in the shell (`menu-bar-keys.test.ts`) and by `scenarios/menu-bar-keyboard.json` and `menu-bar-keyboard-scaled.json` (at 1.25): every step's React events, deliveries, focus and assistive technology's nodes exactly; on main the director waits in vain for any answer to Super+F2.
- **Screenshots** (decision 10):
  - **Protocol, version 5:** `Capture` (request 200), `EndCapture` (201), `ExcludeFromCapture` (202), `ClearSecureRegions` (203), `AddSecureRegion` (204), `OpenScreenshot` (205); `CaptureMode` (event 200, with the `CaptureMode` wire enum: `Selection`, `Window`, `Toolbar`, `Confirm`, `Ended`), `CaptureTarget` (201) and `ScreenshotSaved` (202); `CaptureKind` and `CaptureDestination`.   - **The scene** (`scene/screenshots.rs`): Super+Shift+3, 4 and 5 in `system_shortcut`; the interface's keys in `system_key`, before the input method; the window under the pointer followed as the pointer moves; the shell's captures checked against the interface's serial and shells; secure regions sent and shown with their frames; the shell surfaces left out; presses on the interface moving no focus; the lock ending everything; `take_captures`, `captured`, `secure_regions`, `part_of_window`, `take_screenshot_opens` and `screenshot_saved` for the compositor and the session.
  - **The compositor** (`compose_captures`, `screenshots.rs`): captures composed with the next frame from its layers, on the CPU or, composing on the GPU alone, there; areas cut from their display's capture, windows composed over black and white for their alpha; the `Writer`: PNG encoding, the names in English and Spanish, the hidden-then-linked writing that never replaces a file, the shutter from the sound theme, its descriptor waking the turn; `follow_screenshots` telling the shell.   - **The session:** a writer saving to the Desktop and Documents of `user-dirs.dirs`, named in the session's language, with the sound theme's shutter (none at the login screen); a thumbnail pressed opens the file through `Launcher::open` (`oxyos-apps`: an entry's `MimeType`, the launch record's `opened`, `OXYOS_LAUNCH_URL`), else Stash.

  - **The shell** (`Screenshots.tsx`, `screenshots.ts`): the crosshair with the selection's size in the display's pixels, window picking with the window tinted, the toolbar with its three captures, its options and its timer, the thumbnail (sliding in, five seconds, a press opening it, a drag right sending it away) and "Captura de pantalla guardada" said; nothing of it takes the keyboard.
  - **The development compositor:** `--screenshots DIR` writes them as the session does, into DIR's `Desktop` and `Documents`, and opens them as it does; each step reports what was saved and opened (`screenshots`), which scenarios check, reading the PNGs themselves.
- **The development compositor:** `--key-down` and `--key-up` (scenarios' `key_down`, `key_up`) hold keys across steps; what the applications the shell launched receive is counted as `launched` (a step may expect it exactly), not as leaked; `close.requested` is counted as input.

## Tests

- **Scene** (`scene/tests/shortcuts.rs`, 14 tests): no key, text or composition of any Super chord reaches the application (Tab, Shift+Tab, \`, Space, Ctrl+F, a letter, Shift with a letter), while its Ctrl chord reaches it exactly; Ctrl pressed before Super reaches it, press and release, a key pressed with Super does not even released after it; Super+W asks the active window alone, nothing with none active; Super+Q asks every window of the application, by a claimed id across clients, by the registry's for a launched one, by its client without an id; Super+M minimizes into the dock and focus moves on; Super+H hides the application's shown windows (its minimized one stays in the dock), the shell told `hidden`, and focusing one brings both back, with `Gone` and `Arrived`; Super+\` goes through three windows and comes back, Shift goes back, minimized and other applications' windows skipped; Super+Ctrl+F on and off, Super+F nothing; the switcher's exact sequence of commands to the shell (Tab, Tab, Shift+Tab, \`, →, ←, Q, H, W and M doing nothing, Esc, Shift+Tab again, Super's release), no keys to the shell or the application; with no window, and dismissing an open menu; the shell of the active window's output on two outputs, and none without the grant; locked, nothing acts and the lock screen receives none of the keys, a switcher open at the lock cancelled; the input method never sees Super or its chords; `FocusShellSurface` refused with a serial the shell never had or the application's, granted with the shortcut's, refused for a background (`InvalidRole`) and from an application (`Unauthorized`); the shell's hide and quit by a window, activation counts, and a window gone taking its use. The tests that used `set_shortcuts` now use Super+Space, and the screen reader's precedence test holds Super.
- **Protocol and C ABI:** every new message round-trips, the shortcut is a closed set, the table matches `native-foundation.mdx`, the header's constants and `oxyos_client_focus_shell_surface`, each event converted field by field.
- **The shell** (`apps/shell/tests/switcher.test.ts`): the most recently used first by an application's latest window, unlaunched claims left out, opening forwards and backwards with and without an active application, moving round both ends, an application quit while open.
- **Host scenario** `scenarios/system-shortcuts.json`, Allo (`EditingProbe`, its field focused) and Inbox launched from the dock, every step with exact `react` and `delivered` (Allo's) and, where it matters, `launched` (Inbox's):
  1. Inbox from the dock comes in front, the dock marks both running;
  2. Super+Tab released at once: Allo active again (focus on its window, its pixels in front, Inbox told `focus.out`), no switcher;
  3. Super held, then Tab: the switcher, Allo then Inbox, Inbox chosen (its frame's pixels, a `list` and a selected `Inbox` button for the assistive technology, "Inbox" said); Tab: round to Allo (said); Shift+Tab: Inbox (said); Super released: Inbox in front, the switcher gone from the assistive technology's trees;
  4. Super+H: Inbox's window goes (a `gone` transition), Allo active, the dock still marks Inbox; Super+Tab: Inbox comes back (`arrived`);
  5. Super+W: Inbox is asked once (`close.requested`), goes, and the dock no longer marks it;
  6. Super+Space: the launcher, its search focused (React's `focus@launcher-search`); "inb" typed goes to the search (`change@launcher-search` three times, only Inbox shown) and Allo receives nothing; Super+Space again closes it, Allo has the keyboard;
  7. Super+M: Allo minimized into the dock; its dock icon restores it;
  8. the control: "ok" typed without Super is Allo's (two keys, two texts, the field shows it).

  React receives nothing of any Super chord in any step. With the compositor not taking Super's chords (`system_key` answering false), 60 expectations fail in 13 of its 17 steps (Inbox receives every chord's keys, no switcher, nothing hidden, closed, searched or minimized); the first two steps and the control pass.
