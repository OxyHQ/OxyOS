# ADR 0018: Several displays

Status: **accepted; implemented** in the compositor, the session and the development compositor (steps 1 to 5). Not yet run with a second display, nor on a display faster than 60 Hz: the ThinkPad's panel is 60 Hz alone, and it needs an external monitor for both ([Validation](#validation)). Builds on [ADR 0004](./0004-gpu-composition) (composition and output), [ADR 0005](./0005-damage) (damage), [ADR 0006](./0006-seat) (the seat), [ADR 0009](./0009-session-shell) (the shell), [ADR 0010](./0010-lock-screen) (the lock) and [ADR 0011](./0011-transitions) (transitions).

## Problem

The session shows one display: the first connected connector of the first card with one, at its size, at scale 1. A second monitor stays dark, and one plugged in later is never seen. The scene already holds several outputs: each has a rectangle in a global space of logical pixels and a scale, windows open on the output under the pointer and are moved between outputs (and asked for buffers at the new scale), popups follow their anchor's output, panels keep their output's work area, the lock covers every output without a lock surface, and a removed output's windows go to another. Everything around it is single:
- the compositor composes one framebuffer, the global space's origin at its top-left corner, at scale 1;
- it has one display, one CRTC, one pair of scanout buffers, one flip on its way and one cursor plane;
- the scene's frame is one: every surface's frame begins and completes together;
- the pointer is confined to one rectangle;
- the shell is told its output by an argument (`--shell OUTPUT`): clients cannot learn the outputs ([ADR 0009](./0009-session-shell), step 1).

A Mac is the model. Its displays are arranged side by side, the pointer crosses where they meet, a window dragged across moves to the other display and is drawn at its density, each display has the menu bar and the Dock is on the main display, and unplugging a display moves its windows to the one left.

## Decision

1. **One card.** The session drives every connected connector of the card it opens (the first with a connected output, as before), each on a CRTC and planes of its own: the card's displays share its descriptor and its events (`oxyos_kms::Display::on_connector`; flips read for one CRTC wait in the card for that CRTC's display). A connector for which no CRTC is free is not shown, and the session says so. Several GPUs are not in this decision: an output of a second card (a dock's DisplayLink, a discrete GPU's ports) needs buffers shared between devices (PRIME) and a composition per device. The ThinkPad's NVIDIA T550 has no outputs.
2. **The arrangement.** Outputs are side by side, left to right, top-aligned, with no gap, in the global space's logical pixels. The built-in panel (eDP, LVDS, DSI) comes first, then the other connectors in the card's order. The first is the **primary**: it has the dock and takes the windows of an output that goes. A monitor plugged in comes at the right end. One unplugged goes: those that touched only it come back beside the others, nearest where they were (those right of it move left to close the gap), with what is on them, and the primary takes its windows (the scene's `remove_output`). With nothing connected, the outputs stay, unshown, until a display comes, and an output for it takes their windows (the scene keeps every window on some output). Another display on the same connector (another monitor on the port, decision 10's identity) is a new output. The person may arrange them otherwise, and choose the primary, from Settings (decision 10); the arrangement kept for the set of displays connected is then the one used.
3. **Mode and scale.** Each output is driven at its own mode unless the person chose another (decision 10): its native resolution (the pixels of the mode its EDID prefers) at the **fastest refresh the connector offers at that resolution**, as a Mac drives a ProMotion panel at 120 Hz and a 144 Hz monitor at 144 by default. The card must take it first (the atomic commit that only tests, `Display::test`, in `Heads::prepare`): a rate it refuses (a dock's link without the bandwidth for 1440p at 144 Hz) is passed over for the next fastest at the same pixels, and a display none of whose rates it takes is not shown (`Connector::own_modes`, `displays::choices`). A monitor whose preferred mode is its 60 Hz one, as many say, is shown at its fastest all the same; another resolution's faster rate is not taken over the native pixels. The session's log names each display's mode, whether it is the display's own or chosen, and whether it can refresh at a variable rate (decision 12). Its scale is its density over a reference, to the nearest quarter, from 1 to 3, in pixels per inch from its mode and the physical size its EDID gives: 135 for a built-in panel, seen from closer, 110 for a monitor (`oxyos_compositor::arrangement`). An unknown or implausible size (a projector's, an aspect ratio given as a size) is scale 1. The output's logical size is its pixels at its scale, rounded up. So:

   | Display | Density | Scale | Logical size |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | ThinkPad P14s Gen 3, 14-inch 1920×1200 panel | 162 ppi | 1.25 | 1536×960 |
   | 27-inch 3840×2160 monitor | 163 ppi | 1.5 | 2560×1440 |
   | 27-inch 2560×1440 monitor | 109 ppi | 1 | 2560×1440 |
   | 24-inch 1920×1080 monitor | 92 ppi | 1 | 1920×1080 |

   The ThinkPad's panel was at scale 1 before this decision; it is now drawn at 1.25. Another mode (another resolution, or another refresh) and another scale may be chosen from Settings and are kept for the display (decision 10).
4. **Each output composes and flips on its own.** Each output is a screen of the compositor (`oxyos_compositor::Screen`): it composes the scene's frame list clipped to its rectangle and scaled to its pixels (each layer's edges rounded to the nearest pixel, so what meets in the global space meets on the screen; nearest-neighbour then, filtered since [ADR 0011](./0011-transitions), step 5, where a surface's damage is taken at the size it is drawn on each screen), with its own damage relative to the last frame it composed, on its own CRTC, at its own vblank. The scene's frame is per output (`Scene::begin_frame_on`, `end_frame_on`): an output's frame presents the queued frames of the surfaces on it (the output a surface was placed on) and completes at that output's flip, whatever the others do, so a window on a 144 Hz monitor is paced by that monitor alone. A surface shown across two outputs is composed on both, from its latest frame, and presented with its own. A buffer replaced is released once no frame in flight that may read it remains: an output's frame may read the surfaces shown on it. A surface's own damage is used on an output only when that output composed the frame it is relative to; otherwise its whole rectangle is composed there. One Vulkan device composes every output, each into a pair of scanout buffers of its own; damage concerns only an output's own pair.
5. **The pointer crosses where outputs meet.** One pointer, in the global space, moves within the outputs' rectangles (not their bounding box): it crosses to the next output where they meet and slides along an edge where none continues, as a Mac's does at a shorter display's edge. It is shown on the cursor plane of the display it is over and hidden on the others; each display has its own images, chosen at its scale (32 pixels at 1.5 are 48). Absolute devices span the outputs' bounding box. A display without a cursor plane shows no pointer, as before; the pointer is not composed into frames.
6. **The shell: one per output, the top bar on every one, the dock on the primary.** The session starts its shell once for each output (the Bloom host's `--shell OUTPUT`, as before), on its socket granting `shell` and `lock`, and starts one for each output plugged in and stops the one of each output unplugged. Each is the same React tree, its root the background of its own output, as ADR 0009 decided for one. The compositor owns windows and workspaces, so the shells agree. As before, a shell that exits ends the session, unless the session is locked: that output's shell is then started again. The cost is a JavaScript runtime for each display.

   Every output has the top bar: macOS shows the menu bar on every display since 10.9, and on OxyOS the top bar is also where the clock, the status and the overview's button are. Only the primary has the dock, as only a Mac's main display has the Dock by default: one dock for the session, where windows minimize to from any output (the transition's target, the scene's `dock`). The launcher and the overview open on the output whose dock or top bar opened them. When the primary goes, the next output becomes the primary and its shell shows the dock. Workspaces are the session's, shared by every output (a Mac with "Displays have separate Spaces" off): switching shows the workspace's windows on every output.

   The protocol tells shell clients the outputs: `Output` (event 55: its id, its rectangle in the global space, scale, refresh, and whether it is the primary), for every output when the shell connects and as one is added or changes, and `OutputRemoved` (event 56). Each shell client is told what changed since it was last told, as with windows (ADR 0009). Applications are not told the arrangement: nothing they do needs it. Since [ADR 0029](./0029-chromium-on-oxyos) they are told each output's size, scale, refresh and work area (`ApplicationOutput`, `ApplicationOutputWorkArea`, `ApplicationOutputRemoved`, events 200 to 202) and which output each surface is on (`SurfaceOutput`, 203), what a browser's display list holds, but still never where an output is. What every client needs of its surfaces' output, its refresh, is decision 11's `Refresh`. The Bloom host's shell state carries them (`output`, `outputs`), and the shell draws the dock only once told that its output is the primary, so that another output's never shows one for a moment.
7. **The lock covers every output.** As [ADR 0010](./0010-lock-screen) decided: each output's shell makes a lock surface on its output, and an output without a ready one shows the compositor's cover, a new output too until its shell's lock surface is ready. The lock is on screen, and the machine may sleep, once every display shows a locked frame.
8. **Hotplug through the kernel's uevents.** The session listens for the card's `HOTPLUG=1` uevents on the kernel's netlink group (the one udev reads; from the kernel alone, port 0), which wake its turn, and then reads the connectors again and follows them (decision 2). Coming back from another session (logind resumes the card) reads them again too, since monitors may have come or gone meanwhile. An output that goes has its frame on its way completed unshown, its CRTC and planes turned off and freed, and its scanout buffers freed.
9. **The development compositor runs several outputs.** `oxyos-offscreen --launch --outputs WxH[@SCALE][:HZ],...` composes outputs of those pixels at those scales, arranged as the session arranges its displays, each into its own frame, and starts a shell on each; the director's points are in the global space, and each output's frame is written beside the primary's (`launch-N.output-ID.png`). An output given a refresh (`1600x1000@1.25:120`) is paced as a display at it (decision 11); one without is unpaced, a frame shown as soon as it is composed, and is said to be 60 Hz. With `--kms`, it still shows one output on one display. `--displays FILE` instead plays the session's displays on virtual connectors FILE describes (each one's identity, kind, physical size, modes, `*` after the preferred and `!` after one the card's test refuses, and names), shown, arranged and changed from Settings by the same code as the session's (decision 10), each paced at its mode's refresh.

10. **Settings changes the displays, as a Mac's Displays settings do, and the session keeps them for each display.**
    - **Who a display is.** Its identity is its EDID's maker, product and serial number (`DEL-A0B4-7Y9K2N3`, the serial's text when it gives one), so what is kept for a monitor follows it to any port; one with no serial is its maker and product on that connector, and one with no EDID its connector (`oxyos_kms::Output::identity`). Its name is its EDID's (`DELL U2723QE`); Settings calls a built-in panel "Built-in Display".
    - **What Settings is told.** A client granted `settings` (only Settings, by its launch record, [ADR 0019](./0019-settings)) is told each display the session shows, by its output: its name, its modes (each pixel size and refresh the connector lists, progressive only, the largest first, at most 64, which it prefers), its scale choices and the one its density recommends, its rectangle, scale and mode, its physical size, whether it is the primary and whether it is built in (`DisplayName`, `DisplayMode`, `DisplayScale`, then `Display`, events 130 to 133; `DisplayRemoved`, 134), when the grant is given and as any of it changes. No other client is told.
    - **Its scale choices.** As a Mac's "Larger Text … More Space": in quarters from 1 to 3, the recommended one (decision 3) and up to two each side of it (more on one side when the other has fewer), each leaving at least 1024×640 logical pixels, a Mac's smallest "looks like" choice for a laptop's panel (`arrangement::scale_choices`). The ThinkPad's panel: 1, 1.25 (recommended), 1.5 and 1.75; a 27-inch 4K monitor 1 to 2; a 24-inch 1080p one 1, 1.25 and 1.5. Settings shows each as what the display looks like then (1536 × 960). A scale is applied at once, as a Mac applies one.
    - **Where.** Settings asks for a display at a place (`SetDisplayOrigin`): it is put beside the others, the nearest place to where it was dropped that touches one along at least 64 logical pixels of an edge (the pointer crosses where they meet) and covers none, aligned with that one's top, bottom or centre when within 48 pixels of it (`arrangement::snap`). The arrangement's top-left corner is the global space's origin. When a display's size changes (another mode or scale), the others keep their places against it, as their edges were aligned or at their offsets along it, from the primary outwards (`arrangement::reflow`). `SetPrimaryDisplay` makes a display the primary: the shells are told, its shell shows the dock and the other's hides it; nothing moves.
    - **A mode on trial.** `SetDisplayMode` asks for one of the display's modes: the card must take it first (an atomic commit that only tests, `Display::test`), then it is shown at once, at the scale its density recommends there, and is on trial for 15 seconds (`DisplayConfirmation`, event 135): `ConfirmDisplays` keeps it, or takes it back at once; with neither, or if a display is plugged in or out meanwhile, every display goes back to the mode, scale and place it had, so a mode that leaves no usable picture is never kept, as a Mac's "Keep these display settings?" does. Meanwhile nothing else is changed. A mode the card refuses is refused, and the display shows what it did.
    - **Kept by identity.** What is changed is kept in the preferences file ([ADR 0019](./0019-settings)), written whole before it is applied, so a change that cannot be kept changes nothing: each display's mode, only if the person chose one, and its scale (`display.IDENTITY = 3840x2160@60000 1.5`; `display.IDENTITY = default 1.25` for a scale chosen on a display left at its own mode), and each set of displays' arrangement, the primary first (`arrangement.A+B = B 0,0 A 2560,480`); at most 32 of each, the one changed longest ago going first. A scale chosen never makes the mode shown a choice: a display left at its own mode stays at its own fastest, so the same monitor on a link that takes more is shown faster. They are the session's: Settings cannot write them as a preference (`SetPreference`), and no client is told them as one. When a display comes, at the start or plugged in, it is shown at the mode chosen for it if it still has that mode and the card takes it, else at its own (decision 3); at the scale kept with that mode (the chosen one, or its own when none was chosen) if it is still one of its choices there, else the one its density recommends; and the outputs as the arrangement kept for the set now connected says. A choice the card refuses today stays kept for when it can. Lines written before `default` existed name the mode shown then, and are read as chosen.
    - **The refresh.** Settings' Displays page offers the refresh rates of the resolution shown, the fastest first (a Mac's "Refresh Rate"), each a mode on trial as another resolution is; a resolution chosen keeps the refresh shown where it has it, else takes its fastest. The display's own, unless one is chosen, is its fastest (decision 3).
    - **Every request is answered** as a preference change is (`PreferenceResult`, its serial), each from the `settings` grant alone (`Unauthorized`, and the connection closed, otherwise).

11. **Every frame is paced by its display's refresh, never by an assumed 60 Hz.** A display at 120 Hz shows a frame every 8.3 ms, at 144 every 6.9: the compositor, the host and the fixture each take their pace from the output's refresh.
    - **The compositor.** An output's frame completes at its display's flip (decision 4), so a window on a 120 Hz display is offered a frame each 8.3 ms. While a transition runs, a turn wakes at most a refresh of the fastest display later (a display's flip wakes it sooner); ADR 0011's springs are functions of time, not of frames, so a transition lasts as long at any rate, with twice the frames at 120 Hz. A turn is logged as slow when its work takes longer than a refresh of the fastest display (`Compositor::fastest_refresh`). An output's refresh changes with its mode (`Compositor::set_output_refresh`, the scene's `set_output_refresh`): the shells are told the output again (`Output`).
    - **Its clients are told.** `Refresh` (event 57: a surface and its output's refresh in mHz) goes to every surface's client before the surface's first `Configure`, and again whenever it changes: the surface moved to another output (a window dragged onto a 144 Hz monitor), or its display is driven at another rate. Each client is told only what changed since it was last told, as with the shells' outputs. It is not part of `Configure`: a refresh is a fact about the display, nothing a client acknowledges or answers with a buffer. The C ABI is `OXYOS_EVENT_REFRESH` (`detail`, mHz).
    - **The Bloom host.** Everything it paces by the display takes one refresh of its window's output (`display-refresh.h`): requestAnimationFrame (a frame begins once the window's last was answered and a refresh has passed), Animated's native driver, worklets' and Reanimated's frames, and how long a resized window waits for JavaScript to lay it out before it presents what it has (ADR 0016's settle, a frame of the display). Until it is told (it always is before the window's first configure) and for a render to a file, which has no display, 60 Hz.
    - **The development compositor** paces an output given a refresh, and every virtual display, as a display: a frame composed is shown at the screen's next vblank, in a later turn, as on a display at its flip (`Screen::paced`), so a scenario checks what a 120 Hz display does without one. `regress.py` holds its expectations of "within a frame" to the scenario's refresh, and `perf.py` its budgets: every frame's work fits a refresh of the display it runs on.
    - **Composing as late as possible is not in this decision.** A turn composes as soon as something changes, and at a flip while a transition runs; a client's frame arriving just after composes at the next vblank but one. A frame clock that composes at a deadline before the vblank (as KWin's and Mutter's do) is next ([ADR 0004](./0004-gpu-composition)'s deadline), as is handing the client the vblank's time for its next frame.
12. **Variable refresh: not yet, and why.** A connector's `vrr_capable` says the display and the card can refresh it at a variable rate (Adaptive-Sync, FreeSync; a Mac's ProMotion is the same idea, its range 10 to 120 Hz): with the CRTC's `VRR_ENABLED` set, the panel shows a frame when it is flipped, within its EDID's range, instead of at a fixed vblank. The session reads both (`oxyos_kms::Output::vrr_capable`, `Edid::refresh_range_hz`) and logs them, and leaves `VRR_ENABLED` off: the display refreshes at its mode's fixed rate. The ThinkPad's panel is capable, 40 to 60 Hz. Turning it on is its own decision, after decision 11's frame clock, because:
    - with frames composed as soon as anything changes, a variable refresh shows them at whatever pace they come: a pointer moved at a mouse's 125 Hz drives the panel at its most, and a window dragged slower than the range's least makes the panel flicker as its brightness follows the rate (most panels' gamma depends on it), a known defect of always-on VRR on desktops;
    - only the cursor plane moving is a commit that may or may not flip the panel at a variable rate, by driver, so the pointer can stutter;
    - what it is for on a Mac (fewer refreshes when nothing moves, to save power; a video's own rate shown without judder) needs the compositor to know when nothing moves (it does: nothing is composed) and the rate content wants, which no client tells yet. On Intel panels, panel self-refresh already stops the refreshes of a still screen.
    So the next step is: with the frame clock, `VRR_ENABLED` on for a capable display while something animates at a known rate or a full-screen window presents at its own, off on the desktop, and never below the range's least.

## Steps

1. **Outputs in the compositor.** The scene's per-output frames, releases per frame, outputs moved with what is on them, windows left on no output going to the next output added, and the shell told the outputs (`Output`, `OutputRemoved`, in the protocol, its schema, the documentation's table and the C ABI); the compositor's screens, composition, damage, splash and transitions per output; the pointer confined to the outputs; the GPU's scanout buffers per display.

   *Done.* `crates/oxyos-compositor-core/src/scene/tests/outputs.rs` checks each output's frame presenting and completing on its own, a release waiting only for the frames that may read the buffer (a window across two outputs waits for both), an output moved taking its windows and panels, the shell told the outputs and the primary (and a new primary when the first goes), and windows left on no output going to the next one. `crates/oxyos-compositor/tests/outputs.rs`, through real sockets: each screen composes its part of the global space at its own scale (a 100×60 window is 150×90 pixels at 1.5), a new frame of a window composes only the screen it is on, the splash is centred on every screen, and a window on an output removed is drawn on the primary at the primary's scale. The pointer's confinement and the screens' placement are unit-tested; `oxyos-gpu` checks two displays' scanout buffers interleaving, each frame byte for byte the reference's, on a device that exports DMA-BUF (lavapipe does not: the test skips there).
2. **Displays in the session.** Every connector a display on its own CRTC sharing the card; flips per CRTC; the arrangement, the modes and the scales; hotplug; a shell per output.

   *Done.* `oxyos-kms`: connectors' physical size and whether they are built in, `Display::on_connector`, `objects`, `turn_off`, the card's flips kept for the display of their CRTC, and `Hotplug` (the uevent's filter unit-tested). `oxyos-session`: the plan of the outputs (unit-tested on the ThinkPad's connectors: the panel first, monitors plugged in at the right and unplugged closing the gap, another monitor on a port, nothing connected; since step 4 `oxyos_compositor::displays`, which the session drives through its card) and its application to the compositor, a shell per output, each display's pointer images at its scale.
3. **The shell's dock on the primary.** The Bloom host's shell state learns the outputs; `apps/shell`'s `Shell` takes `dock`; the session's shell passes it from its output being the primary.

   *Done.* `scenarios/session-two-outputs.json` runs the shell on a 1280×720 output at 1 and, right of it, a 1920×1080 output at 1.5 (1280×720 logical). Each output has its background and top bar; only the primary has the dock, and each output's work area leaves out its own panels. The application's window, moved onto the second output, is drawn there at 1.5 and takes its presses; the second output's top bar opens its menu under the item, on that output. With a dock on every output, or a window drawn at scale 1 there, it fails.

4. **Settings changes the displays.** The protocol's requests and events (decision 10) and their C ABI; the scene's displays (`scene/displays.rs`: what Settings is told, its requests handed on) and outputs given another size and scale (`Scene::set_output_geometry`) or made the primary; the shared displays (`oxyos_compositor::displays`, which the session and the development compositor both drive through `Heads`, the session's being its card); the EDID's identity and each mode's exact refresh (`oxyos-kms`); the kept displays and arrangements (`oxyos-preferences`); the Bloom host's `OxyOSSettings` and Settings' Displays page.

   *Done.*
   - **The scene** (`scene/tests/displays.rs`): an output resized keeps its windows within its work area (moved, and shrunk only when larger) and its panels on its edges, asking each surface for buffers at its scale; a full-screen window fills it; the primary changes with nothing moving and the shells told; Settings is told each display and then only what changes; only Settings asks, and a mode on trial is told, to a Settings that connects meanwhile too.
   - **The displays** (`oxyos-compositor`'s `displays/tests.rs`, over played connectors): the start, plugging in and out, another monitor on a port and nothing connected, as before; what is kept is how they are shown at the next start (the monitor the primary at its kept mode, the panel bottom-aligned right of it); a scale applied, kept, the other display moving with its edge; a mode on trial going back after 15 seconds unless kept, nothing else taken meanwhile, kept in the file once kept; a mode the card refuses refused and the old one shown again; a display dropped above the other placed touching it, the primary kept, the same arrangement read back; a change that cannot be written changing nothing. The arrangement's geometry (`arrangement.rs`): the scale choices of five displays, snapping, and displays keeping their places against one that changes size.
   - **The EDID** (`edid.rs`): a monitor's maker, product, serial text and name; a panel's serial number; none; what is not an EDID. The preferences' display lines: written, read back, bad ones nothing, the oldest going first. The C ABI's constants, events field by field, and the five requests through a socket.
   - **The scenario.** `scenarios/displays.json` runs Settings on the development compositor's displays (`--displays FILE`: virtual connectors, as a card reports them, which `oxyos_compositor::displays` shows and changes as the session does), a 14-inch 1920×1200 panel and a 24-inch 1080p monitor with the shell on each: Settings shows both and the scale choices; More space makes the panel 1920×1200 logical at once, the monitor moving with its edge and both shells' bars on their new edges; the monitor made the main display takes the dock; 1280×720 on the monitor is shown at once (its frame 1280×720) with "Keep these display settings?" counting down, Revert takes it back, leaving it alone takes it back after 15 seconds, Keep Changes keeps it; the monitor moved above the panel (its accessible action) goes there. The file holds both displays' modes and scales and the arrangement. With the grant given to another application, Settings is told no display and the run fails.

   Not verified: the session on a real card with two displays (the mode's atomic test and modeset, a CRTC freed and taken again, the pointer's images at a new scale), and dragging a display in the arrangement by hand (the scenario arranges through the accessible action).

5. **Each display at its fastest, everything paced by it** (decisions 3, 11 and 12).

   *Done.*
   - **The mode** (`displays/tests.rs`): a 1440p monitor of 60, 120 and 144 Hz whose preferred mode is its 60 Hz one is driven at 144, not 60 nor 1080p's faster 144.001; the card refusing 144 (its test) shows it at 120, at the same pixels and scale, and refusing every rate of its native pixels shows nothing; a scale chosen on it keeps `default 1.25`; 60 Hz chosen in Settings is shown at once at the same pixels, paced at 60 (the scene's output, its screen, what its surfaces are told), kept, and wins at the next start; a scale then keeps it chosen; on a link that refuses it, its own fastest at the scale its density recommends, the choice still kept. The preferences' `default` line, written and read back. `oxyos-kms`: the EDID's range limits (the ThinkPad's own EDID: 40 to 60 Hz; rates past 255 Hz), the connector's `vrr_capable`.
   - **The pacing.** The scene (`scene/tests/outputs.rs`): a window told its output's refresh before its first configure, 60 Hz on the panel and 144 on the monitor, told once, told 144 once dragged onto the monitor, and 120 when that display is driven at 120 (with the shells told the output again, and no configure); a refresh of 0 refused. The compositor through real sockets (`tests/refresh.rs`): on an output paced at 120 Hz, a client drawing each frame as its last is shown has one shown on each vblank, 8.33 ms apart, twice the frames of 60 Hz in the same time; the window's arrival lasts as long at both rates (its last frame 0.44 s in), with twice the frames at 120. The protocol's and the C ABI's `Refresh`.
   - **The scenarios** (`--outputs ...:120`): `refresh-120-animations.json` runs AnimatedProbe at 120 Hz: requestAnimationFrame's frames 8.3 ms apart (median 8.41, never under 8.33) and about 72 in a 600 ms animation (36 at 60 Hz, `native-animated.json`), the native driver's 600 ms animations about 72 frames, each frame's value where a linear animation is within a 120 Hz frame; with the host paced at 60 Hz it fails. `refresh-120-windows.json` and `refresh-60-windows.json` zoom WindowProbe's window and back beside the shell, each zoom's last frame 0.49 s in at both rates, and drag it at a mouse's 125 Hz: a frame composed each refresh while the hand moves, 8.29 ms apart (97 frames) at 120 Hz, 16.68 (49) at 60. Their zooms draw a frame each two refreshes at 120 Hz: this fixture composes on the CPU, and a window stretched across the work area takes it longer than 8.3 ms; a session composes on the GPU.

   Not verified: a display faster than 60 Hz on a real card (the session choosing its fastest mode and the card's test, frames at its flips, a window moved between a 60 and a 120 Hz display told each), and variable refresh, left off.

## Validation

What is checked without hardware: the scene, the compositor's screens through real sockets, the session's plan, the fixture's scenario with two outputs, and every existing scenario on one output. What only a machine with a second display checks, on the ThinkPad with a monitor on USB-C (DisplayPort) or HDMI:
- both displays lit at their preferred modes, the panel the primary at 1.25, the monitor at its own scale, with the session's log naming each output, its connector, mode, scale and place;
- a frame per output at each display's own refresh (a 60 Hz panel and a monitor at another rate), on the GPU into each display's own buffers;
- the pointer crossing between them where they meet, shown on the cursor plane of the one it is over, at its scale;
- a window dragged across, drawn sharp on the monitor once it has redrawn at its scale;
- the top bar on both, the dock on the panel only;
- from Settings' Displays page: the monitor made the main display (the dock on it), another scale on each (the pointer's images at it), a resolution kept and one left to go back after 15 seconds, the monitor dragged above the panel; then the monitor unplugged and plugged into another port, and the session started again, each shown as it was kept;
- unplugging the monitor: its windows on the panel, its CRTC off, the session going on; plugging it in again: lit at the right, its shell's top bar on it;
- locking with both: the lock screen on both, sleep held until both show it;
- a switch to another terminal and back with both displays, and with the monitor plugged in or out while away;
- a boot through the boot logo with the monitor connected ([ADR 0017](./0017-boot-logo)): each display keeps the CRTC already lighting it where it is free.

What only a display faster than 60 Hz checks (a 120 or 144 Hz monitor on USB-C or HDMI, or a laptop with such a panel); the ThinkPad's own panel offers 1920×1200 at 60.003 Hz alone (`vrr_capable`, 40 to 60 Hz):
- the session's log naming the monitor at its native resolution and its fastest refresh (`… @143.998 (its own)`), and, behind a dock that cannot carry it, the card's refusal and the next fastest;
- flips 6.9 ms apart (at 144 Hz) while a window is dragged, a transition runs or an animation plays, with the slow-turn log (`[compositor] a turn worked …`, now past a refresh of the fastest display) quiet; on the GPU, every frame's composition within a refresh;
- requestAnimationFrame in an application on it at 144 a second (its host's report, `OXYOS_HOST_REPORT`: `refresh` 143998, `animation_frame` 6.9 ms apart), and at 60 again once its window is dragged onto the panel;
- Settings' Displays page: the monitor's refresh rates, the fastest shown; 60 Hz chosen, kept and shown at the next start; a scale changed on a display left at its own rate keeping `default` in the preferences file.

## Not in this decision

- Several GPUs and rotation.
- Mirroring. A mirrored display shows another output's rectangle, at its own mode and pixels, and is no output of the scene: nothing is placed on it and it has no shell. The scene's frames are per output (decision 4), so a display that composes another's surfaces needs frames of its own, paced by its own flips, and releases that wait for them too; that is its own decision.
- Variable refresh (decision 12 says why not yet), and the display's colour (HDR, profiles).
- Workspaces of their own for each display, and the shell's overview showing each display's windows (`WindowState` does not say which output a window is on).
- Telling applications the outputs, and windows following their output's scale change while dragged across before they redraw (they are drawn at the old scale, scaled, until then).
- A pointer composed into frames for displays without a cursor plane.
