# ADR 0057: Battery and energy

Status: **accepted; implemented** (the session's side, the menu bar's battery menu, Settings › Battery, scenarios over played services); what only the ThinkPad can show is in [What to verify on the ThinkPad](#what-to-verify-on-the-thinkpad). Amended on 8 November 2026 (decision 2): the charge limit across reboots, batteries put back and waking, verified in UPower 1.90.9's source, and kept in effect. Builds on [ADR 0006](./0006-seat) (logind), [ADR 0010](./0010-lock-screen) (the idle lock and sleep held back for the lock), [ADR 0019](./0019-settings) (preferences, Settings and its grant), [ADR 0025](./0025-hardware-keys-and-control-center) (the battery from UPower, followed by its signals), [ADR 0026](./0026-notifications) and [ADR 0030](./0030-installing-applications) (the session's vocabulary over `SystemRequest`).

## Problem

The top bar drew the battery's charge (ADR 0025) and nothing else of energy: no menu, no percentage, no Settings page, no Low Power Mode, no charge limit, no display that turns off or machine that sleeps for want of input (the session only locked, ADR 0010), no warning when the battery runs low, no way to know which application drains it. A Mac has all of it:

- **the menu bar's battery**, its charge (and its percentage, as the person chooses), a bolt while charging; its menu says the charge, the power source, the time left or until full, the applications *Using Significant Energy* and *Battery Settings…*;
- **System Settings › Battery**: the charge and *Battery Health* (maximum capacity, cycles); the charge over the last 24 hours and 10 days, and the screen's use; *Low Power Mode* (Never, Always, Only on Battery, Only on Power Adapter); *Options…*: *Optimized Battery Charging*, the display's sleep on battery and on the adapter, *Wake for network access*, *Slightly dim the display on battery*;
- the display dimming, then turning off, then the Mac sleeping; a media player keeping it awake; the lid closing putting it to sleep; *Low Battery* warnings, then sleep when it runs out.

What the ThinkPad P14s Gen 3 has (looked at read-only for ADR 0025): UPower 1.90.9 on `BAT0` (whose kernel driver, `thinkpad_acpi`, has `charge_control_start_threshold` and `charge_control_end_threshold`), logind, `thinkpad_acpi`'s `platform_profile` and the processor's energy preference (`intel_pstate`'s EPP). The image installs `upower`; not power-profiles-daemon until now.

## Decision

1. **The session asks the system's own power services; it reads and writes no power sysfs itself.** UPower for the battery (its display device for the charge, as ADR 0025; the battery device itself for its health: `EnergyFull`, `EnergyFullDesign`, `ChargeCycles`; `WarningLevel`, `GetCriticalAction`), power-profiles-daemon for Low Power Mode, logind for sleep and its inhibitors. `crates/oxyos-power` is their client (`zbus`, blocking, every call off the compositor's turn) and keeps the history and the applications' energy; `oxyos_compositor::power` is the session's side.

2. **The charge limit is UPower's charge threshold**, not a helper of OxyOS's own. UPower 1.90 (the image's and the ThinkPad's) has `EnableChargeThreshold`: UPower runs as root, polkit decides (`org.freedesktop.UPower.enable-charging-limit`: the active session's user without a password, others never), and it writes the battery's `charge_control_start_threshold` and `charge_control_end_threshold` (the thresholds its hardware database gives the battery, 75 % and 80 % by default; 0 and 100 when turned off), keeps the choice in its own state (`/var/lib/upower/charging-threshold-status`) and writes it again at every start and whenever the battery appears. That is exactly the privileged helper with polkit the feature needs, already in the image, and the one GNOME and KDE use. A helper of OxyOS's own would be a second root writer of the same files, which UPower knows nothing of: its `ChargeThresholdEnabled` would lie, and the next `EnableChargeThreshold(false)` from any program (or UPower's own recovery) would undo ours. The thresholds are the machine's (an administrator changes them with a hwdb `CHARGE_LIMIT` entry, as UPower documents), not a per-person slider: a charge limit is the hardware's state, for every account. Settings shows it as a Mac shows *Optimized Battery Charging*, saying the limit: *Limit charge to 80 %* (UPower's `ChargeEndThreshold`); it is hidden where UPower says the battery cannot (`ChargeThresholdSupported` false).

   **Across reboots, batteries and waking** (amended 8 November 2026, verified in the source of UPower 1.90.9, Debian trixie's `upower` and the image's: `src/up-device-battery.c`, `src/linux/up-device-supply-battery.c`, `src/linux/up-backend.c`, tag `v1.90.9`):
   - **The choice is kept.** `EnableChargeThreshold` (`up_device_battery_set_charge_threshold`) writes `1` or `0` to `STATE_DIR/charging-threshold-status` (`/var/lib/upower`) before it writes the thresholds (`up_device_battery_charge_threshold_state_write`), and `ChargeThresholdEnabled` is set only once both are written.
   - **Written again whenever the battery appears.** `up_device_battery_update_info`, on a battery present that was not (UPower starting, or a battery put back), calls `up_device_battery_recover_battery_charging_threshold`: it reads the file and, if it says `1` and the battery supports a threshold, writes the hwdb's `CHARGE_LIMIT` thresholds again (`up_device_supply_battery_set_battery_charge_thresholds`); `ChargeThresholdEnabled` is what it read, false if the write failed. So **a reboot keeps the limit**, whatever the firmware did meanwhile.
   - **Not when the machine wakes.** `up_backend_prepare_for_sleep` refreshes every device on waking (`UP_REFRESH_RESUME`), and `up_device_battery_update_info` on a battery already present writes nothing; and UPower never reads the thresholds back. The ThinkPad's `thinkpad_acpi` keeps them in the EC across sleep, but other firmware does not always: asus-wmi's EC forgets on a power cycle (its driver sets 100 % as a battery is added, `asus_wmi_battery_add`), which hibernation (UPower's own critical action) is, and another program may write them. UPower would then go on saying the limit is on while the battery charges full.
   - **So the session keeps UPower's own choice in effect, through UPower.** `upower::details` reads the battery's thresholds from its directory in `/sys/class/power_supply` (UPower's `NativePath`; reading only, as decision 1 allows nothing else) and says the limit is on only while UPower's choice is on and the battery holds UPower's thresholds (where they can be read). As the session starts and each time the machine wakes (logind's `PrepareForSleep(false)`), where UPower says on and the battery does not hold them, `upower::keep_charge_limit` asks UPower to write its own choice again (`EnableChargeThreshold(true)`, as polkit allows the active session's user); refused, Settings shows the limit off, the truth, and turning it on asks UPower again. The session still never writes sysfs, and asks nothing while the battery holds the limit.

3. **Low Power Mode is power-profiles-daemon's `power-saver`, held** (`HoldProfile("power-saver", "Low Power Mode", "so.oxy.oxyos")`), not the kernel's `platform_profile` and EPP written directly. The daemon already drives every machine's mix (`platform_profile`, `intel_pstate`'s and `amd_pstate`'s energy preferences, AMD's panel power, the battery's trickle charge) from one D-Bus property, polkit lets the active session's user hold a profile without a password (`org.freedesktop.UPower.PowerProfiles.hold-profile`), and **a hold goes with the connection that took it**: a session that ends or fails leaves the machine as the person set it, which writing the files (as root, through a helper) never would. A hold is never the person's choice of profile (`ActiveProfile`, which the daemon keeps across boots): Low Power Mode is the session's, as the person's preference says. The preference `low_power_mode` is `never`, `always`, `battery` (Only on Battery) or `adapter` (Only on Power Adapter), applied whenever it or the power source changes. Another program choosing a profile releases the hold (`ProfileReleased`): that choice stands until Low Power Mode next changes; the daemon restarting loses it, and the session holds it again (`oxyos_power::follow`, two narrow matches). Without the daemon, Low Power Mode still lowers OxyOS's own work. `power-profiles-daemon` is added to the image's manifest.

4. **Low Power Mode lowers OxyOS's own work too, by one signal every part obeys.** `oxyos_power::LowPower` (the session's `Power::low_power()`): whether it is on, and a descriptor per part that becomes readable when it changes, so a thread waiting on its own work waits on it too, never on a clock. Its parts:
   - **what is drawn goes at most 60 times a second** (`Scene::set_refresh_cap`): every surface on a faster display is told 60 Hz as its refresh (`Refresh`), as a Mac's ProMotion display holds 60 Hz in Low Power Mode; on a 60 Hz panel (the ThinkPad's) nothing changes;
   - **background work waits**: the content index (Spotlight's), the updates' checks and any later part that works on its own obey `LowPower` (pause while on, go on when it goes off). Nothing else of OxyOS works in the background today (docs/performance.mdx, *Idle*).
   The shell and Settings are told it (`lowPower.on`); the menu bar's battery is drawn yellow while it is on, as a Mac's.

5. **The charge history is the session's, recorded from UPower's change signals.** `$XDG_STATE_HOME/oxyos/battery-history` (`~/.local/state`), a line per change appended (`SECONDS b 64 d`, `s 1`, `z 1`, `o 1`): the charge and its state as UPower signals them (the controls' narrow match on the display device, ADR 0025), the display turned off and on, the machine going to sleep and waking, the session starting and ending. A machine on its adapter at 100 % records nothing for hours, and nothing wakes to record it. Ten days are kept (about three thousand lines); what is older is left out when the session starts (the file written anew and renamed). UPower's own `GetHistory` was not chosen: it is per battery device at a resolution the daemon picks, empty when it restarts, and says nothing of the screen; the screen's use is the session's to know anyway. Where no session ran (the machine off, another account), the charts show nothing, as a Mac's do. Settings asks for it charted (`powerHistory`): the last 24 hours in quarters of an hour and the last ten days by the hour, each bin's charge at its end, whether it charged in it and the seconds the display was on in it (Settings sums the days by its own time zone).

6. **The display dims, turns off and the machine sleeps for want of input** (`idle.rs`, extending ADR 0010's idle lock on the same clock and the same input): the display dims (a quarter of its brightness, through the controls, no bezel) ten seconds before it turns off (half its timer when shorter); turns off after the person's timer for the power source (`display_off_battery`, 2 minutes by default; `display_off_adapter`, 10), every output's CRTC turned off through KMS (`Display::turn_off`) and nothing composed, so no client is given a frame and what animates stops; and the machine sleeps after its own (`sleep_battery`, 10 minutes; `sleep_adapter`, never), never before the display is off, through logind's `Suspend` (not interactive; refused without asking when polkit would ask, `CanSuspend` not `yes`). Input brings the display back at once (modeset with the next frame) and its brightness as it was, unless the person changed it meanwhile. Each timer is a deadline the turn waits for, never a poll; a timer changed, unplugging or plugging in, idle no longer held back and the machine waking arm them again. **Inhibitors are logind's**: while one of the user's programs holds an `idle` block inhibitor (a video playing) the display stays on and the machine awake, and logind refuses `Suspend` while any program holds a `sleep` block inhibitor (the session's own delay inhibitor still makes sleep wait for the lock on screen, ADR 0010). **The lid is logind's**: `HandleLidSwitch=suspend` is its default and the session takes no `handle-lid-switch` inhibitor, so closing it sleeps the machine (and, with an external display, logind's `HandleLidSwitchDocked=ignore` keeps it awake), the lock on screen first. The login screen has none of this yet.

7. **Each application's energy** is the processor's and the GPU's time of every process the session started for it (the one it launched and those below it): `/proc/PID/stat` (`utime`, `stime`, and the children it waited for), and each DRM client's engines' busy time (`drm-engine-*` in `/proc/PID/fdinfo/FD`, counted once per client), as `top` and `intel_gpu_top` read them. It is sampled when the battery changes (UPower's signal, the history's moment) and when asked (the menu opening, Settings' page), never on a clock. An application *uses significant energy* when, since the newest sample at least a minute old (or since it started), its processor and GPU time together were at least a fifth of one core's. Settings lists every application of the session with its processor and GPU time.

8. **The low battery is warned of as a Mac does, and UPower acts.** Discharging, the session posts a notification at 10 % and again at 5 % (*Low Battery*, "10% battery remaining. Plug your computer into a power outlet."), once each per discharge, as Settings (activating it opens Settings), with its sound, the second replacing the first. At UPower's action level (`WarningLevel` 5, at `PercentageAction`, 2 % by default) it says what UPower is about to do (`GetCriticalAction`: Debian's `HybridSleep`, or `Hibernate`, `Suspend`, `PowerOff`) — and **UPower does it**, twenty seconds later, as root: it has done so for every session since 0.99, falls back to `PowerOff` where the machine cannot hibernate, and is the only safe owner (a session that failed or is locked still has the machine saved). The session never does it a second time.

9. **The Battery options are preferences** the session applies (ADR 0019's file, Settings changing them as any): `display_off_battery`, `display_off_adapter`, `sleep_battery`, `sleep_adapter` (minutes, 0 never, at most 180), `dim_on_battery` (on by default: unplugged, the display goes to three quarters of its brightness, and back when plugged in), `battery_percentage` (the menu bar's percentage) and `low_power_mode` (decision 3). A Mac's *Wake for network access* is not offered ([Not in this decision](#not-in-this-decision)): OxyOS shows no control that does nothing.

10. **Who may ask, and what they are told: the session's vocabulary, no new protocol message.** Over `SystemRequest` (ADR 0030), read strictly, answered by `SystemText` naming its serial:

    | Request | Who | Input | Answer |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | `{"power":{}}` | the shell (`shell`), Settings (`settings`) | no | `{"power": State}` (the health read again, told when read) |
    | `{"powerHistory":{}}` | the shell, Settings | no | `{"powerHistory": {day, days}}` |
    | `{"energy":{}}` | the shell, Settings | no | `{"energy": {apps}}`, sampled now |
    | `{"chargeLimit":{"enabled":bool}}` | Settings | yes | `{"chargeLimit":{}}` or `{"failed":{"reason"}}` |

    `State` is `{battery, health, chargeLimit, lowPower, showPercentage, criticalAction}`: `battery` (`percent`, `state`: `charging`, `discharging`, `full`, `notCharging`; `onAc`, `minutes`, `low`) or null without one; `health` (`maxCapacity` in percent, `cycles`, `worn`: below 80 %, a Mac's *Service Recommended*) or null; `chargeLimit` (`enabled`, `end`, `start`) or null where UPower cannot; `lowPower` (`mode`, `on`, `profiles`: the daemon is there, `held`). It is told, unasked (serial 0), to the shell and Settings whenever it changes. A refusal is `{"refused":{"reason"}}` (`notPermitted`, `needsInput`, `invalidRequest`); a failure's reason `unsupported`, `notPermitted`, `unavailable` or `failed`.

11. **The menu bar's battery** (`TopBar`'s `BatteryMenuItem`), once the session told the state: a status menu of the bar, as the input source's (ADR 0041): its icon (red at 10 % and below, yellow in Low Power Mode, a bolt while charging) and, with `battery_percentage`, its charge before it; named *Battery*, its charge and state its value (as the level indicator before). Its menu: *Battery* and the charge, *Power Source: Battery* or *Power Adapter*, the time (*3 h 20 min remaining*, *45 min until full*, *Fully Charged*, *Not Charging*, *Calculating…*), *Low Power Mode* while on, *Using Significant Energy* and the applications (or *No Apps Using Significant Energy*), sampled as it opens, and *Battery Settings…*, which opens `oxyos-settings://battery`. It is the first of the status items for the keyboard (Super+F8, ← →).

12. **Settings › Battery**, as a Mac's: the charge and its state; *Battery Health* (Normal or Service Recommended, maximum capacity, cycles); *Low Power Mode*; the charts of the last 24 hours and 10 days (the charge, green where it charged; the screen's use); the applications' energy; *Options…*: *Limit charge to 80 %*, the display's and the machine's sleep on battery and on the adapter, *Slightly dim the display on battery*, *Show battery percentage in menu bar*.

## What to verify on the ThinkPad

With this branch's `oxyos-session`, Bloom host and bundles deployed as the previous ADRs', `power-profiles-daemon` installed (`apt install power-profiles-daemon`), in a session on a seat:

1. **Battery.** The menu bar's battery matches `upower -i /org/freedesktop/UPower/devices/DisplayDevice`; its menu says the same charge, *Power Source*, and the time left (or until full while charging). Unplug: within a second the menu says *Power Source: Battery* and the display goes to three quarters of its brightness (`cat /sys/class/backlight/intel_backlight/brightness`); plug in: back.
2. **Health.** Settings › Battery's maximum capacity is `energy-full / energy-full-design` of `upower -i /org/freedesktop/UPower/devices/battery_BAT0` (rounded), and its cycles the `charge-cycles` there (shown only if the firmware reports them).
3. **Charge limit.** `upower -i …/battery_BAT0` says `charge-threshold-supported: yes`. *Limit charge to 80 %* on: no password asked; `cat /sys/class/power_supply/BAT0/charge_control_start_threshold charge_control_end_threshold` says `75` and `80`, `charge-threshold-enabled: yes`; plugged in above 80 %, the battery says `pending-charge` and the menu *Not Charging*. Off: `0` and `100`. After a reboot the choice is still there (UPower writes it again), and after sleeping (`systemctl suspend`, then the same `cat`; the session's log says `the battery had forgotten the charge limit` only if the EC lost it).
4. **Low Power Mode.** *Only on Battery*, unplugged: `powerprofilesctl` lists the session's hold (`so.oxy.oxyos`, "Low Power Mode") and `power-saver` active, `cat /sys/firmware/acpi/platform_profile` says `low-power`, the battery icon is yellow; plugged in: `balanced`, no hold. End the session (sign out): no hold left.
5. **Display and sleep.** On battery, no input: the display dims at 1 min 50 s, is off at 2 min (its backlight and the panel off: `cat /sys/class/drm/card*-eDP-1/dpms` says `Off`), any key brings it back as it was. With `systemd-inhibit --what=idle sleep 600` it stays on. At 10 minutes the machine suspends (`journalctl -b -u systemd-logind` says `Suspending`), the lock on screen when it wakes; with `systemd-inhibit --what=sleep sleep 600`, logind refuses and the display stays off. On the adapter it never sleeps by default.
6. **Lid.** Closing the lid suspends (`journalctl` shows `Lid closed` then the suspend); opening it shows the lock screen. With an external display connected it stays awake.
7. **Low battery.** Discharging past 10 % and 5 %: a *Low Battery* notification each (`upower -i …DisplayDevice` for the charge). At 2 % UPower's warning-level `action` and the notification says it will hibernate; UPower hibernates (or, without a swap it can hibernate to, powers off) twenty seconds later. Do this only with work saved.
8. **Energy and idle.** An application spinning (a scrolling list held, a video) appears under *Using Significant Energy* after a minute or at once if just started; idle with nothing on screen, `perf.py session` still shows the session within its budget (no new wakeups: the power threads wait on their jobs and signals).

## Not in this decision

- The login screen's display sleep; a Mac's *Optimized* charging that learns the person's routine (the limit here is fixed); High Power Mode; per-application *Battery usage* over ten days (the energy is this session's); applications told Low Power Mode (`ProcessInfo.isLowPowerModeEnabled`); hibernation set up by OxyOS (a swap the size of memory and the resume device are the installer's, ADR 0023).
- *Wake for network access.* On Linux it is Wake-on-LAN and Wake-on-WLAN's magic packet, which NetworkManager keeps per saved connection (`802-3-ethernet.wake-on-lan`, `802-11-wireless.wake-on-wlan`, every user's connection, `settings.modify.system`) and applies only when a connection is activated; *Only on Power Adapter* would rewrite every saved connection at each plug. It needs its own decision (likely NetworkManager's global default, an administrator's), so it is neither a preference nor shown.
