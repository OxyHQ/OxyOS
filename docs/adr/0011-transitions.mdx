# ADR 0011: Transitions

Status: **accepted**; steps 1 and 2 implemented. Builds on [ADR 0004](./0004-gpu-composition) (composition on the CPU and a Vulkan device, byte for byte), [ADR 0005](./0005-damage) (damage) and [ADR 0009](./0009-session-shell) (the shell's surfaces).

## Problem

Every surface appears and goes at once: a window is there on the frame its first buffer is, a menu vanishes on the frame after it is dismissed, the launcher and the lock screen cut in. A Mac never does this: windows open and close with a short zoom and fade, menus fade out as they are chosen, Launchpad zooms in, the lock screen fades. Doing it in each application would make every application implement it, differently, and could not animate a surface after its client destroyed it. Composition today cannot do it either: a layer is drawn at a size (scaled, nearest neighbour) but always whole, with no opacity.

## Decision

1. **The compositor animates arrivals and departures; applications animate their content.** As the macOS window server animates windows and menus while AppKit and the application animate what is inside them. The compositor owns:
   - **Windows and dialogs:** when a toplevel or a dialog is first shown, its frame grows from 96 % to its size and fades in, 180 ms, easing out; when it goes (destroyed, its client gone, minimized), its last frame shrinks to 96 % and fades out, 150 ms.
   - **Popups (menus):** they appear at once, as a Mac's do; they fade out, 150 ms, when dismissed or destroyed.
   - **The shell's overlays** (the launcher, the overview): they fade in and settle from 104 % to their size, 200 ms; they fade out, 150 ms.
   - **The lock** ([ADR 0010](./0010-lock-screen)): the covers are opaque on the next frame, as they are now: protection is never animated. The lock screen's surface fades in over them, 250 ms; on unlock it fades out, 250 ms, over the desktop shown again.
   - **The shell's panels**, when the session starts: they fade in, 250 ms.
   - **The session's start:** from its first frame until its shell is on screen, the session shows the Oxy mark, white, centred on black (an eighth of the output's height, Bloom's drawing of it, the one the Oxy SDK ships), as a Mac shows its own while it starts; it then fades out over the shell, 400 ms. It is the compositor's own frame (`splash.rs`), drawn over everything.

   What a surface shows is never changed by its transition: it is drawn whole, scaled about its centre, at an opacity. Input goes by a surface's real geometry from its first frame, and never to a surface going away; a transition never delays a configure, a frame's feedback or an event. Applications keep animating their own content (Bloom, Reanimated, the host's modal windows as on Android); nothing is added to the protocol.

2. **Composition draws a layer at an opacity.** `oxyos_gpu::Layer` gains an opacity (0 to 255; 255 is as now): its pixels, premultiplied, are scaled by it before they are blended, on the CPU reference and in the Vulkan shader with the same integer arithmetic, so the two still agree byte for byte. A layer at 255 is composed exactly as before.

3. **A surface going away keeps its last frame for its transition.** When a departing transition begins, the compositor copies the surface's displayed pixels, once; the scene forgets the surface at once (its client's resources are freed, its buffers released) and the copy is drawn until the transition ends. The copy is the compositor's alone and is dropped when the transition ends.

4. **Transitions run on the display's time.** They are measured on `CLOCK_MONOTONIC`; while one runs, the compositor composes a frame for every refresh (its turn wakes for the next), and a transitioning surface's rectangle is damaged on each. They ease out (cubic). A future accessibility setting, reduce motion, makes every duration zero; this decision leaves room for it and no more.

## Steps

1. **Opacity in composition.** `Layer::opacity` in `oxyos-gpu`, on the CPU and in the shader, with the frame file carrying it; tested byte for byte against the reference on the Vulkan device, and at 255 against frames composed before.

   *Done.* The pixel, premultiplied (an opaque one or a fill with alpha 255), and its alpha are scaled by `opacity / 255`, then blended source-over, in integers on both sides; at 255 that is the copy and the blend there were. The frame file is `OXYFRAME2` (an opacity byte per layer); `OXYFRAME1` files read with every layer at 255. Tests: exact values at half opacity worked by hand, nothing at 0, the Vulkan device against the reference at eight opacities, the file in both formats. On the ThinkPad's Intel Iris Xe every Vulkan test passes; there they caught a damaged region's background fill left at opacity 0, which no test without a device could see, so CI now installs Mesa's lavapipe and runs them.
2. **Transitions in the compositor.** Their state per surface, the departing copies, pacing and damage; tested on the scene's and the compositor's clocks: the opacities and sizes a transition goes through, input never reaching a departing surface, the lock's covers never late.
   *Done.* The scene reports what arrives and goes (`Scene::take_appearances`): a surface's first frame as it begins presenting (so the first frame composing it already draws it at 0), a window restored, and a surface destroyed, dismissed, minimized or, a lock surface, unlocked; a surface hidden with its workspace or under the lock is neither. The compositor (`transitions.rs`) keeps the last frame it composed of each surface shown (it reads every surface's pixels for each frame already, so keeping them costs memory, not copies) and draws one going from it. While anything runs, every frame is composed whole and the turn wakes for the next. Locked, nothing of the session going is drawn: what started going in the lock's own turn (a menu the lock dismisses, a window minimized) is dropped, and a surface destroyed under the lock has no frame to go from; a lock surface fading in does so over a cover. Tests: the curves and the motions, the scene's appearances (and none for a workspace or the lock), and through real sockets on the compositor's own clock the pixels of a window arriving (nothing on its first frame, 223/255 at 90 ms, whole at 180) and going (32/255 at 75 ms, gone at 150), and the lock showing nothing going (failing when the drop is removed). Every host scenario passes with them running.
3. **Scenarios and the ThinkPad.** The development compositor reports each step's transitions (frames, first and last opacity); scenarios check them, and on the ThinkPad every transition's frames are on time at 60 Hz.

   *Measured on the ThinkPad* (Intel Iris Xe, 1920×1200 at 60 Hz, composing on the device alone as a session does, `--gpu-only`, the virtual devices): `session-shell` and `lock-screen` pass with the transitions running, two runs each. 78 and about 148 frames shown; composition 5.4 ms median, 18–19 ms at most; 0 or 1 frame late per run. A late frame is one whose composition (reading every surface's buffer and composing the whole output, as every frame is while a transition runs) took longer than a refresh. With the CPU reference composed as well (`--gpu`, the development check) frames take 14 ms median and two are late per run: that check, not the session, is what costs there. Not yet: the director reporting each step's transitions so that scenarios check them.

   *Composing only what a transition changes.* A frame while surfaces arrive or go is no longer composed whole: its damage is what changed in the scene (ADR 0005) and, for each surface in transition, its rectangle and where it is placed, in this frame and in the last (so a surface growing, shrinking or ending its transition is drawn again wherever it was or is). The next frame is composed whole only when it must be: the first, after the display came back, or with the splash shown (`recompose`, which also composes such a frame when no surface changed). Tested through real sockets on the compositor's clock: at each point of a window arriving and going, every output pixel that changed is within the frame's damage (a display composing only the damage shows what one composing the whole frame does), and most of those frames are composed from their damage; without the transitions' rectangles it fails on the first frame.

## Not in this decision

- Minimizing into the dock (a Mac's genie), notifications sliding in, switching workspaces with a slide: each a transition of its own, on this mechanism.
- The reduce-motion setting itself.
- Transitions an application asks for: none is needed yet.
