# ADR 0011: Transitions

Status: **accepted**; steps 1 to 5 implemented. Builds on [ADR 0004](./0004-gpu-composition) (composition on the CPU and a Vulkan device, byte for byte), [ADR 0005](./0005-damage) (damage) and [ADR 0009](./0009-session-shell) (the shell's surfaces).

## Problem

Every surface appears and goes at once: a window is there on the frame its first buffer is, a menu vanishes on the frame after it is dismissed, the launcher and the lock screen cut in. A Mac never does this: windows open and close with a short zoom and fade, menus fade out as they are chosen, Launchpad zooms in, the lock screen fades. Doing it in each application would make every application implement it, differently, and could not animate a surface after its client destroyed it. Composition today cannot do it either: a layer is drawn at a size (scaled, nearest neighbour) but always whole, with no opacity.

## Decision

1. **The compositor animates arrivals and departures; applications animate their content.** As the macOS window server animates windows and menus while AppKit and the application animate what is inside them. The compositor owns:
   - **Windows and dialogs:** when a toplevel or a dialog is first shown, its frame grows from 96 % to its size and fades in, 180 ms, easing out; when it goes (destroyed, its client gone, minimized), its last frame shrinks to 96 % and fades out, 150 ms.
   - **Popups (menus):** they appear at once, as a Mac's do; they fade out, 150 ms, when dismissed or destroyed.
   - **The shell's overlays** (the launcher, the overview): they fade in and settle from 104 % to their size, 200 ms; they fade out, 150 ms.
   - **The lock** ([ADR 0010](./0010-lock-screen)): the covers are opaque on the next frame, as they are now: protection is never animated. The lock screen's surface fades in over them, 250 ms; on unlock it fades out, 250 ms, over the desktop shown again.
   - **The shell's panels**, when the session starts: they fade in, 250 ms.
   - **The session's start:** from its first frame until its shell is on screen, the session shows the Oxy mark, white, centred on black (an eighth of the output's height, Bloom's drawing of it, the one the Oxy SDK ships), as a Mac shows its own while it starts; it then fades out over the shell, 400 ms. It is the compositor's own frame (`splash.rs`), drawn over everything. Before the session, from Plymouth's first frame, the boot logo shows the same pixels, so the session taking the display over changes nothing on screen ([ADR 0017](./0017-boot-logo)); the mark's outline, not Bloom's view box, is what is centred and an eighth of the height.

   What a surface shows is never changed by its transition: it is drawn whole, scaled about its centre, at an opacity. Input goes by a surface's real geometry from its first frame, and never to a surface going away; a transition never delays a configure, a frame's feedback or an event. Applications keep animating their own content (Bloom, Reanimated, the host's modal windows as on Android); nothing is added to the protocol.

2. **Composition draws a layer at an opacity.** `oxyos_gpu::Layer` gains an opacity (0 to 255; 255 is as now): its pixels, premultiplied, are scaled by it before they are blended, on the CPU reference and in the Vulkan shader with the same integer arithmetic, so the two still agree byte for byte. A layer at 255 is composed exactly as before.

3. **A surface going away keeps its last frame for its transition.** When a departing transition begins, the compositor copies the surface's displayed pixels, once; the scene forgets the surface at once (its client's resources are freed, its buffers released) and the copy is drawn until the transition ends. The copy is the compositor's alone and is dropped when the transition ends.

4. **Transitions run on the display's time.** They are measured on `CLOCK_MONOTONIC`; while one runs, the compositor composes a frame for every refresh (its turn wakes for the next), and a transitioning surface's rectangle is damaged on each. They ease out (cubic). A future accessibility setting, reduce motion, makes every duration zero; this decision leaves room for it and no more.

## Steps

1. **Opacity in composition.** `Layer::opacity` in `oxyos-gpu`, on the CPU and in the shader, with the frame file carrying it; tested byte for byte against the reference on the Vulkan device, and at 255 against frames composed before.

   *Done.* The pixel, premultiplied (an opaque one or a fill with alpha 255), and its alpha are scaled by `opacity / 255`, then blended source-over, in integers on both sides; at 255 that is the copy and the blend there were. The frame file is `OXYFRAME2` (an opacity byte per layer); `OXYFRAME1` files read with every layer at 255. Tests: exact values at half opacity worked by hand, nothing at 0, the Vulkan device against the reference at eight opacities, the file in both formats. On the ThinkPad's Intel Iris Xe every Vulkan test passes; there they caught a damaged region's background fill left at opacity 0, which no test without a device could see, so CI now installs Mesa's lavapipe and runs them.
2. **Transitions in the compositor.** Their state per surface, the departing copies, pacing and damage; tested on the scene's and the compositor's clocks: the opacities and sizes a transition goes through, input never reaching a departing surface, the lock's covers never late.
   *Done.* The scene reports what arrives and goes (`Scene::take_appearances`): a surface's first frame as it begins presenting (so the first frame composing it already draws it at 0), a window restored, and a surface destroyed, dismissed, minimized or, a lock surface, unlocked; a surface hidden with its workspace or under the lock is neither. The compositor (`transitions.rs`) keeps the last frame it composed of each surface shown (it reads every surface's pixels for each frame already, so keeping them costs memory, not copies) and draws one going from it. While anything runs, every frame is composed whole and the turn wakes for the next. Locked, nothing of the session going is drawn: what started going in the lock's own turn (a menu the lock dismisses, a window minimized) is dropped, and a surface destroyed under the lock has no frame to go from; a lock surface fading in does so over a cover. Tests: the curves and the motions, the scene's appearances (and none for a workspace or the lock), and through real sockets on the compositor's own clock the pixels of a window arriving (nothing on its first frame, 223/255 at 90 ms, whole at 180) and going (32/255 at 75 ms, gone at 150), and the lock showing nothing going (failing when the drop is removed). Every host scenario passes with them running.
3. **Scenarios and the ThinkPad.** The development compositor reports each step's transitions (frames, first and last opacity); scenarios check them, and on the ThinkPad every transition's frames are on time at 60 Hz.

   *Measured on the ThinkPad* (Intel Iris Xe, 1920×1200 at 60 Hz, composing on the device alone as a session does, `--gpu-only`, the virtual devices): `session-shell` and `lock-screen` pass with the transitions running, two runs each. 78 and about 148 frames shown; composition 5.4 ms median, 18–19 ms at most; 0 or 1 frame late per run. A late frame is one whose composition (reading every surface's buffer and composing the whole output, as every frame is while a transition runs) took longer than a refresh. With the CPU reference composed as well (`--gpu`, the development check) frames take 14 ms median and two are late per run: that check, not the session, is what costs there.

   *Each step's transitions, checked.* The compositor records what each transition drew (`Transitions::take_seen`): its kind, whether a surface arrived, the opacities of the first and last frames drawn in it, how many, and whether it ran to its end (a window restored as it went, or something going the lock dropped, did not), and counts the frames composed while any ran. The development compositor reports them with each step (`transitions` in the step's record), and `regress.py` checks them (`transitions`: the fewest frames, the transitions each step must run, matched by any keys given, and how many). `session-shell` checks the launcher arriving from nothing and going, and the menu fading out; `lock-screen` the lock screen arriving from nothing as the session locks while the menu the lock dismissed is never drawn going (0 frames, not ended), and going as it unlocks; `sample` a chosen menu fading out. With every motion's duration zero, all three fail. The development compositor's summary reports how much of the output frames composed (`damaged_fraction_median`, `frames_whole`): locally, composing only what transitions change took `session-shell` from 143 whole frames of 150 to 73 (median share 1.0 to 0.94) and `lock-screen` from 189 of 233 to 144 (a full-screen lock screen fading is the whole output). The CPU reference composes every frame whole, so the time saved shows on a device (the Vulkan composition of the damage, a display), not here.

   *Composing only what a transition changes.* A frame while surfaces arrive or go is no longer composed whole: its damage is what changed in the scene (ADR 0005) and, for each surface in transition, its rectangle and where it is placed, in this frame and in the last (so a surface growing, shrinking or ending its transition is drawn again wherever it was or is). The next frame is composed whole only when it must be: the first, after the display came back, or with the splash shown (`recompose`, which also composes such a frame when no surface changed). Tested through real sockets on the compositor's clock: at each point of a window arriving and going, every output pixel that changed is within the frame's damage (a display composing only the damage shows what one composing the whole frame does), and most of those frames are composed from their damage; without the transitions' rectangles it fails on the first frame.

4. **More of a Mac's motions**, on the same mechanism: a window minimized goes into the dock and comes back out of it; a switch of workspace slides the windows aside; a notification slides in.

   *Done.* A motion moves a surface as well as scaling and fading it (`Shift`): toward a point, by a distance, or by a share of its own size, and it may not fade.
   - **Minimizing:** the window shrinks to a tenth of its size as it goes to the dock's centre (the shell's bottom panel, `Scene::dock`; without one, the output's bottom edge's centre), fading, 300 ms; restoring brings it back out of the dock the same way. This is the transition, not a Mac's genie warp: composition draws a surface as a rectangle, never bent.
   - **A switch of workspace:** the scene reports the windows hidden and shown as moved aside (`WorkspaceOut`, `WorkspaceIn`), not gone: the windows left slide out by the output's width, to the left going to a later workspace and to the right going back, and those of the workspace shown slide in from the other side, 300 ms, whole, never faded, as a Mac's spaces. A window moved to another workspace is hidden, not slid: the overview it is moved from covers it.
   - **Notifications** (`ShellKind::Notification`, a kind of their own): they slide in from beside themselves, a tenth beyond their own width, as they fade in, 250 ms, and fade out, 200 ms.

   Each transition reports how its surface came or went (`change`, in the development compositor's record). Tests: the motions (the window at the dock's centre at a tenth of its size at the end, the slide by the output's width each way, the notification beside itself), the scene's appearances (a minimized window, a restored one, a workspace's out and in, the dock as the shell's shown bottom panel), and through real sockets a window minimized, restored and slid out and back, every frame from its damage alone; `shell-windows` checks a window minimized into the dock and brought back by the dock's button.

5. **Filtered scaling.** On the ThinkPad the platform's author saw windows pixelated while they were drawn at another size than their buffers': arriving and going at 96 %, minimized into the dock down to a tenth, a zoom's frame stretched ([ADR 0016](./0016-windows), step 6), an overlay at 104 %, a notification, and a surface on an output whose scale makes its buffer larger than its rectangle. Composition sampled each output pixel's source by nearest neighbour: drawn smaller it drops source rows and columns (at 96 % one in 25, which crawls as the scale changes frame by frame; at a tenth nine in ten, so fine detail turns into patterns that are not there), drawn larger it repeats them in blocks. A Mac's window server filters scaled layers.

   *Decision.* Composition has one filter, `oxyos_gpu::Filter::Box`, the one the overview's window views brought ([ADR 0022](./0022-window-views)), made exact and extended to every scale; the compositor draws every surface with it (`Filter::Nearest` stays for frame files from before). A filtered layer drawn at another size samples its source, on each axis, over a box centred where the output pixel's centre falls in the source, as wide as the pixel's footprint there and never narrower than one source pixel (`filter.rs`):
   - **drawn smaller,** the box is the footprint: each output pixel is the average of the source pixels it covers, each by the share of it covered (area averaging, a box filter). No source pixel is dropped at any scale, so a minimize down to a tenth shows what the window holds, and a 2× buffer on a 1× rectangle is the exact average of each 2 × 2;
   - **drawn larger,** the box is one source pixel: linear interpolation between the two nearest pixel centres on each axis, bilinear;
   - the two meet at 1:1, where the box is the one pixel under the output pixel: **a layer drawn 1:1 is its pixels exactly**, and is composed by the same code as before (the fast path; its frames are the same bytes);
   - **an axis of 64 pixels or more within one pixel of its source's size is its pixels,** by nearest neighbour (`filter::SNAP`; a shorter one a pixel off, two pixels into one, is filtered): the difference is rounding (an output at a fractional scale places a window's edges on whole pixels, so at 1.5 its rectangle may be a pixel narrower than its buffer, ADR 0018) or the last frames of a transition, and filtering would soften the whole window, half its pixels blended with their neighbours, to hide one row or column;
   - beyond the edges the edge pixels continue (clamp to edge), so nothing from outside a layer bleeds into it; the axes' weights multiply; premultiplied colour and alpha are averaged alike, so a pixel stays premultiplied, and an opaque layer stays opaque. The filtered pixel then goes through the layer's opacity, corners and blending exactly as a sampled one did.

   *The arithmetic, shared byte for byte.* The shader has no 64-bit integers, so everything fits 32 bits. Positions are in 1/(2·length) of a source pixel, where every edge and centre is a whole number; a pixel's weight is its overlap with the box in 4096ths, from the running total of overlaps rounded at each pixel's end, so an axis's weights sum to exactly 4096 (a flat colour stays exactly that colour). A channel is Σ rows (weight × Σ columns (weight × channel)), at most 4096² × 255 < 2³², rounded once, `(sum + 2²³) >> 24`. Twelve bits per axis is the most that keeps that sum in 32 bits; it quantises a weight to within 1/8192, far below a level of 255. `compose_cpu` and `compose.wgsl` do the same integer operations; the CPU works each layer's weights out once per column and row, the shader per tap.

   *One filter, not two.* ADR 0022's box read at most 8 pixels along each axis, evenly spread, each counting alike: exact only where the footprint was whole pixels and no more than 8 (a window at a tenth would have been read at 8 of its 10 columns), never bilinear, so drawing larger was still nearest neighbour. This one subsumes it: down to an eighth at whole ratios it gives the same values (a one-pixel checkerboard at a quarter is 128, opaque red beside nothing halved is red at half alpha, at 1:1 the pixels themselves), and ADR 0022's tests pass unchanged but one, the 32-pixel footprint, which now reads all 32 (their mean, 124, not the 8 taps' 128). The overview's cards are drawn by it too.

   *Why a box and not a mip chain.* Minifying beyond 2× needs more than bilinear: bilinear reads 2 × 2 pixels whatever the scale, so at a tenth it would still drop most of them. A mip chain (trilinear) would have to be rebuilt whenever a surface's content changes (a window arriving animates its content while it scales; a zoom shows the client's live frames), on the CPU with an upload a third larger or in a second pass, both matched byte for byte, and it blurs between levels and steps through them during a 300 ms minimize. The box in the shader needs no memory, no pass and no change to uploads (`Layer::changed` stays rows of the source), is exact area averaging at every scale and continuous across them, and costs in proportion to the source pixels drawn, not the output: an output pixel at a tenth reads about 11 × 11, but there are a hundredth as many. Its work per output pixel is bounded: a layer whose source is more than 64 times its rectangle along an axis (`filter::MAX_RATIO`; a tenth at four times the output's scale is 40), or more than 16384 pixels along one, is sampled by nearest neighbour.

   *Damage.* Drawn larger, an output pixel reads up to a source pixel beyond its own footprint: a surface's damage on the output is now every output pixel whose sample reads a damaged buffer pixel (`filter::span`, exact; [ADR 0005](./0005-damage)), which at 1:1 is the damage itself. It is worked out on each screen at the size the surface is drawn there ([ADR 0018](./0018-several-displays)): a window at scale 1 on an output at 1.5 is drawn larger there, though 1:1 in the global space. Transitions already damage their whole rectangle, where it was and is drawn.

   *The frame file* is unchanged: `OXYFRAME4`'s filter byte (ADR 0022) says `Box`; `OXYFRAME3` and older files are read as they were composed, by nearest neighbour.

   *Done.* Tests: hand-worked values (twice as wide, [0, 255] is [0, 64, 191, 255]; 1.5 times, [90, 30, 0, 60] is [90, 60, 25, 5, 30, 60]; half, [10, 20, 30, 50] is [15, 40]; a third, [0, 0, 255] is 85; two thirds, [90, 30, 0, 60, 120, 240] is [70, 10, 80, 200]; a one-pixel checkerboard at half, 128 everywhere; transparent and opaque red, premultiplied, halved: 128 of each, over black and over white; an opaque layer opaque whatever its fourth byte); a flat colour exact at any size; an axis 1:1 while the other doubles; a 602-pixel row at 601 its pixels, one dropped, alone and with the other axis filtered; nearest neighbour beyond the largest ratio; the span of damage exact against every tap for every extent and length up to 23, and for long axes a pixel apart; changed pixels changing the output only within their span, drawn larger, smaller and each axis its own way. On Mesa's lavapipe the Vulkan composition is the reference byte for byte at scales 0.1, 0.5, 0.96, 1, 1.04, 1.5 and 2, at opacities 0, 77, 128 and 255, square and with corners, a premultiplied popup over a window, each axis by its own factor past the output's edges, one axis within a pixel of its size and the other filtered, and from damage; and ADR 0022's views, reduced, clipped and from damage. Layers drawn 1:1, filtered or not, give the same frames as before filtering (FNV-1a digests of twelve frames composed at fba8630: a window, a popup and a cover, and a shadow beneath corners, at three opacities), and the nearest-neighbour frames from before corners are unchanged.

   *Measured.* A pattern finer than a tenth can show (a zone plate and one-pixel lines, 640 × 400) drawn at a tenth, a fifth and 73 %: by nearest neighbour it is 107–115 levels of 255 (RMS) from the exact average of what each output pixel covers (its rings alias into rings that are not there, its lines into solid black or white); filtered, 0.3–0.4, rounding alone. Composing a 1920 × 1200 output with a full-size window drawn at each scale (`examples/sampling.rs`, medians), on lavapipe (a 32-thread CPU) and the CPU reference:

   | Scale | lavapipe, nearest | lavapipe, filtered | CPU reference, nearest | CPU reference, filtered |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | 1 (the fast path) | 3.4–3.8 ms | 3.3–3.4 ms | 12.1–12.6 ms | 12.1–12.4 ms |
   | 0.96 | 3.1–3.4 ms | 6.4–8.4 ms | 11.4–11.5 ms | 24.3–26.2 ms |
   | 0.5 | 1.7–1.8 ms | 2.7–2.9 ms | 3.3–3.4 ms | 6.8–7.1 ms |
   | 0.25 | 1.1–1.6 ms | 2.1–2.3 ms | 1.1–1.2 ms | 4.0–4.2 ms |
   | 0.1 | 0.9–1.1 ms | 2.0–2.6 ms | 0.5 ms | 2.3–2.5 ms |
   | 1.04 | 3.1–3.7 ms | 6.9–7.6 ms | 11.8–12.7 ms | 25.7–27.0 ms |
   | 2 | 4.9–5.1 ms | 9.1–9.9 ms | 12.2 ms | 25.6–26.6 ms |

   Three runs of 15 frames, the range of their medians; the device's time from its timestamps (the window's pixels already on it, as a transition's are). Filtering costs at most about twice nearest neighbour on lavapipe, most near 1:1, where every output pixel reads 2 × 2 to 3 × 3 source pixels and works a division out per tap; at a tenth the box is larger, but there are a hundredth as many output pixels. *Estimated, not measured, on the Iris Xe:* ADR 0004 measured its composition pass at about 0.5 ms for the regression's 1920 × 1200 frames, where lavapipe takes about 3.4 ms for this one at 1:1; at the same ratio the worst case here, a full-screen window at 96 % or 104 % or a zoom to twice, is about 1–1.5 ms, and a minimize (300 ms, mostly at its smaller sizes) less, against a 16.7 ms frame of which composing takes about 5.3 ms (ADR 0004). Like lavapipe's, the Iris Xe's integer division is not a single instruction, so the ratio is the honest guess; `cargo run --release -p oxyos-gpu --example sampling` on the ThinkPad measures it. The CPU reference (the development compositor's check, and `--kms` without a GPU) takes about twice as long filtered, 25 ms at worst for a full-screen window; the session composes on the device.

   Every host scenario that runs without a display passes (34) with `--check-damage`, none of their expectations changed: their pixels are checked where surfaces are settled at 1:1, or (`shell-thumbnails`) where a view's colour is flat, and a flat colour drawn smaller or larger is exactly that colour. `windows`, `window-resize`, `shell-windows`, `session-shell`, `lock-screen`, `shell-overview`, `shell-thumbnails` (the overview's views) and `session-two-outputs` (a second output at 1.5, where a window at scale 1 is drawn larger and a window asked for frames at 1.5 is drawn 1:1) also pass with every frame composed on lavapipe identical to the CPU reference, their transitions' frames among them. Not verified: the session on the ThinkPad's display, where the platform's author saw the pixelation.

## Not in this decision

- A Mac's genie (bending a window as it goes into the dock), and a slide for a window moved to another workspace.
- The reduce-motion setting itself.
- Transitions an application asks for: none is needed yet.
