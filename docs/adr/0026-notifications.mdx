# ADR 0026: Notifications

Status: **accepted; implemented** (the protocol, the scene, the C ABI, the Bloom host's expo-notifications and the shell). Builds on [ADR 0009](./0009-session-shell) (the shell), [ADR 0010](./0010-lock-screen) (the lock screen), [ADR 0011](./0011-transitions) (a notification's motion), [ADR 0013](./0013-launching-applications) (launch records), [ADR 0014](./0014-screen-reader) (what is said), [ADR 0015](./0015-application-model) (one API on every platform), [ADR 0019](./0019-settings) (preferences), [ADR 0024](./0024-system-shortcuts) and [ADR 0025](./0025-hardware-keys-and-control-center) (the Control Center, and the notification surface its bezel shares).

## Problem

An application cannot tell the person anything while they are in another one. A Mac has notifications: an application posts one (a title, a body, its icon, perhaps actions or a reply), a banner slides in at the top right and goes after a few seconds, every one collects in the Notification Center (opened from the menu bar's clock), and pressing one brings its application forward with what was pressed. Focus (Do Not Disturb) silences banners; the lock screen says how many there are but not what they say unless the person allows it; VoiceOver reads a banner without interrupting.

OxyOS had the surface for it and nothing else: the protocol's `ShellKind::Notification` (its layer, above panels and overlays) and its motion (ADR 0011: it slides in from beside itself, 250 ms, and fades out, 200 ms). No application could post, nothing kept notifications, the shell drew none and nothing reached the application back.

The Oxy applications already post notifications: every Expo application among them (Allo, Mention, Inbox, Alia, Homiio, Mercaria, Noted, Clarity, Schedio, Syra, Peable, Moovo, the Authenticator, and the Oxy SDK's `@oxy.so/services`) depends on `expo-notifications` (`~58.1.6` in most), none on Notifee. They call `scheduleNotificationAsync` with a `null` trigger for local notifications (Allo's and Mention's `createNotification`), `setNotificationHandler` for how one shows while they are in front, `addNotificationResponseReceivedListener` and `getLastNotificationResponse` for presses, and `setNotificationCategoryAsync` for actions.

## Decision

1. **Applications use expo-notifications; the Bloom host implements it as OxyOS's backend.** As ADR 0015 says, an application never asks which platform it is on: an Oxy application's `expo-notifications` calls work on OxyOS unchanged, as `expo-clipboard`'s already do (ADR 0007). The host's adapter (`sample/oxyos/expo-adapter.js`) is expo-notifications' native side: the modules it requires by name (`ExpoNotificationScheduler`, `ExpoNotificationsEmitter`, `ExpoNotificationsHandlerModule`, `ExpoNotificationPresenter`, `ExpoNotificationCategoriesModule`, `ExpoNotificationPermissionsModule` and the rest), over the host's `OxyOSNotifications` module, which speaks the protocol through the C ABI.
   - **What works:** presenting now (`scheduleNotificationAsync` with a `null` trigger), with a title, subtitle, body, `data` (JSON, kept for the response) and a category's actions (at most three; a `textInput` makes an action a reply; `opensAppToForeground: false` answers without bringing the application forward); the handler asked as Expo asks it (`onHandleNotification`, answered by `handleNotificationAsync` within three seconds, else shown), its `shouldShowBanner` choosing a banner and `shouldShowList` alone keeping it only in the Notification Center; the received and response listeners; `getLastNotificationResponse`; the presented notifications and dismissing them; permissions (granted: decision 3 decides who posts).
   - **What fails loudly,** as the adapter's rule is: scheduled triggers (a time, a date, a calendar), push tokens and push server registration, background notification tasks. Badges answer that none was set (the dock draws none yet). Android's channels are Android's: expo-notifications asks for them only there.
2. **The session keeps notifications, in the scene, for the session.** The compositor's scene (`scene/notifications.rs`) keeps each notification posted: its application, its texts, its actions, when it was posted, numbered by the session. The shell is told them; it keeps none of its own.
   - **Bounded per application:** at most 32 an application (a 33rd takes its oldest's place) and 256 in all (the oldest goes). Texts are bounded by the protocol: an identifier of 64 bytes, a title and subtitle of 256, a body of 2048, data of 4096, an action's id, label and placeholder of 64, a reply of 1024; at most 3 actions.
   - **Grouped per application** by the shell (decision 6), as a Mac groups them; the session keeps one list.
   - **Replaced by identifier:** posting with an identifier the application already has replaces that notification (the shell and the application are told the old one went), as expo-notifications and a Mac do.
   - **Not kept across a session restart.** The session keeps them in memory and writes nothing: what notifications say is private (another file would be another place to protect), they are about now, and the applications that posted them start again with the session and post what still matters (a chat's server pushes again). A shell started again within the session is told every one kept, as no banner.
   - **A rate limit:** an application posts at most 5 in any 10 seconds; beyond that a post is refused (`Throttled`) and the shell is told nothing, so a runaway application cannot fill the screen with banners. The scene reads no clock: the program running the compositor tells it the time (`Scene::set_clock`, `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` in a session) before it handles requests.
3. **The posting application is its launch record, never its claim.** Only a client whose process the session started (ADR 0013) is an application that may post; its notifications are that application's (the registry's id, its name and its icon in the shell), whatever its windows claim with `SetAppId`. A client the session did not start is refused (`Refused`). There is no per-application permission to ask: every application the session started may post, and the person decides how they show (decision 5). Per-application settings (banners off for one, a Mac's per-application notification settings) are not in this decision.
4. **A press reaches the application once, and brings it forward.** The shell, pressed (a banner, a Notification Center entry, one of its actions, a reply sent), sends `ActivateNotification`, citing the serial of the input it received (a press or a key; one it did not receive lately is ignored, so a shell cannot open applications on its own), with the reply in chunks before it. Nothing is activated while the session is locked. The scene:
   - removes the notification (the shell and the application are told it went), as a Mac's goes once opened;
   - gives the application, **once**, to one client (the one whose window was used last, else its first), the notification's texts (its identifier, title, subtitle, body, data), the chosen action's id and the reply, then `NotificationResponse` with a serial that is a user input of the application's (it may copy to the clipboard, open a menu, as after a key press);
   - **brings the application forward:** its window used last is shown and activated, restored from the dock or with its application if hidden (ADR 0024), on its workspace; an action posted to answer in the background does not;
   - **starts the application if none of its clients runs:** the response waits in the scene (at most 8 an application) and the program running the compositor starts the application from its registry (`take_notification_launches`; `oxyos-session` and the development compositor use their launchers); the first client whose launch record names that application is given the responses, once. One that cannot be started drops them (`notification_launch_failed`). The host keeps a response that comes before JavaScript is listening until the adapter takes it (`takeResponses`), so a response that started the application is its `getLastNotificationResponse` and reaches a listener present then; each is delivered once.
5. **Do Not Disturb and previews are preferences** ([ADR 0019](./0019-settings)), kept and changed as the others: `do_not_disturb` (`true`, `false`; off by default) and `notification_previews` (`always`, `unlocked`; `unlocked` by default, as a Mac's *Show previews: When Unlocked*). The session tells the scene the policy (`Scene::set_notification_policy`) and the shell is told it (`NotificationPolicy`) when it connects and when it changes.
   - **Where the person changes them:** Settings' *Notifications* page (`apps/settings`, a page of its own after *Wallpaper*): a *Do Not Disturb* switch and *Show previews: Always / When unlocked*, through `SetPreference` as every Settings page. And the Control Center's *No molestar* switch (ADR 0025), below its levels: the shell's `SetDoNotDisturb` (request 135), the same path as its Dark Mode's `SetAppearance`, a change of the user's preference that the session validates, keeps in the file and answers with a `PreferenceResult`, refused while locked. No new grant: the shell's `shell` grant changes only these two preferences.
   - **Do Not Disturb:** every notification is kept and listed, none is shown as a banner, and nothing is said to the screen reader (the shell says only banners). The Notification Center says it is on.
   - **The lock screen:** while the session is locked, no banner is shown (only lock surfaces are, ADR 0010). Without previews, the shell is told only whose a notification posted while locked is (its texts are *withheld*: they never reach the shell's process while the session is locked, so neither the lock screen, its accessibility tree nor a screen reader can show them), and is told the rest as the session unlocks; the lock screen shows, under the clock, each application's icon, name and how many it has. With previews, it shows the newest one's title and body.
6. **The shell shows them, with Bloom.**
   - **Banners** (`NotificationCard` in `apps/shell`): a notification that arrived to be a banner is shown at the top right, below the top bar, on a `ShellSurface` of kind `notification` at the right edge, each its own surface, which the compositor places at its output's top-right corner (above every window, panel and overlay, keeping nothing out of the work area) and slides in. A notification surface at the right edge is a banner and takes presses over its card; any other is ADR 0025's bezel, the whole output, which takes none. The card is Bloom's `Text`, `Button`, `CloseButton` and `TextField` with the application's own icon from the registry: its name and when, the title, subtitle and body, the actions; a reply action opens a field whose text Enter (or Enviar) sends. A banner goes after five seconds, while no reply is being typed; *Ocultar* hides it, and it stays in the Notification Center. Only the primary display shows banners (ADR 0018), as a Mac's main display does.
   - **The screen reader** is told a banner politely, after what it is saying, as VoiceOver says one: “Allo. Ana. ¿Comemos juntos?” (the shell's platform `announce`, an `AccessibilityAnnounce` with `Polite`). The card is one button named so; its actions and close are buttons of their own.
   - **The Notification Center** opens from the top bar's clock, as a Mac's: an overlay with a panel at the right, one group per application (its icon and name, the newest group first, each newest first), each notification pressed to open it in its application or removed (✕), each group cleared at once (*Borrar*), “No hay notificaciones” when there are none, “No molestar” when Do Not Disturb is on. A press beside it closes it. The top bar's change is the clock becoming a button. The Notification Center and the Control Center are open one at a time, as a Mac's.
7. **The protocol** uses requests 130–135 and events 120–125:
   - `NotificationText` (request 130: the post's serial, the field, the action, chunks as `SetAppId`'s) and `PostNotification` (131: whether it is a banner, which actions answer in the background), answered by `NotificationResult` (event 120: posted with the session's number, refused, throttled);
   - `WithdrawNotification` (132): an application its own (0: all of its own), the shell any (0: all);
   - `NotificationReply` (133) and `ActivateNotification` (134), the shell's; `SetDoNotDisturb` (135), its Control Center's (decision 5);
   - `NotificationText` (event 121, the same fields as the request's, its application's among them), `Notification` (122: show it as a banner now, how many actions, its age, whether its texts are withheld) to the shell; `NotificationResponse` (123) to the application; `NotificationRemoved` (124) to both; `NotificationPolicy` (125) to the shell.

## Results

- **Protocol:** the messages of decision 7, the `NotificationField` and `NotificationOutcome` wire enums and the limits (`MAX_NOTIFICATION_*`). The C ABI: `oxyos_client_post_notification` (an `OxyOSNotification` with its `OxyOSNotificationAction`s), `oxyos_client_withdraw_notification`, `oxyos_client_activate_notification`, `oxyos_client_set_do_not_disturb`, `OXYOS_EVENT_NOTIFICATION_*`, `OXYOS_NOTIFICATION_*` and `OXYOS_NOTIFICATIONS_*`.
- **The scene** (`scene/notifications.rs`): decisions 2 to 5; `set_clock`, `set_notification_policy`, `take_notification_launches`, `notification_launch_failed`. A notification shell surface is placed at its output's top-right corner whatever its edge (the bezel, the output's size, stays where it was). `SetDoNotDisturb` is the Control Center's request, asked as `SetAppearance` is (`ask_control_preference`).
- **The session:** `Settings::apply` tells the scene the policy from `do_not_disturb` and `notification_previews` (`oxyos-preferences`); the compositor tells the scene the time before each client's requests; `oxyos-session` and the development compositor start an application a notification's press needs. The development compositor counts a response as `notification.response` among what an application receives (`delivered`).
- **The Bloom host:** `OxyOSNotifications` (post, withdraw, take the responses; `OxyOSNotificationResult`, `OxyOSNotificationRemoved` and `OxyOSNotificationResponse` events), `notifications.h` (texts by notification, field and action), the shell's state (`notifications`, `notificationPolicy`) and `OxyOSShell`'s `activateNotification`, `removeNotification`, `setDoNotDisturb` (numbered with `setAppearance`'s) and `announce`; `OxyOSShellSurface` of kind `notification` at the right edge, which takes presses. The adapter of decision 1, with Expo's version 4 UUIDs (expo-notifications names a notification by one) from the adapter, as Hermes has no web crypto.
- **The shell** (`apps/shell`): `ShellNotification` and `ShellNotifications` in the model, `notifications.ts` (groups, the next banner, the lock screen's summary, when, what is said), `NotificationCard`, `NotificationCenter`, the clock, the lock screen's summary, the Control Center's *No molestar* module, and the platform's `announce`.
- **Settings** (`apps/settings`): `doNotDisturb` and `notificationPreviews` in its model, and the *Notifications* page.
- **The sample:** `sample/NotificationProbe.tsx`, an application that posts as Allo and Mention do, with a reply action, and shows the responses it hears; `expo-notifications` `~58.1.6` among the sample's development dependencies, as `expo-clipboard` is.

## Not in this decision

- Scheduled notifications (a time, a date, a calendar), which need the session to keep them across time and restarts; push notifications (a server's, through a push service); background notification tasks.
- Per-application notification settings (banners off, alerts that stay, previews per application), sounds, badges on the dock's icons, critical alerts that break through Do Not Disturb, Focus modes beyond Do Not Disturb, and scheduling Do Not Disturb.
- Notifications grouped by thread within an application, stacked banners, and a banner's hover keeping it.
- The shell's own notifications (a low battery, an update); the session's own processes post none yet.
